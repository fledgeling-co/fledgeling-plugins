# armada-sync icon notes

**Direction: "Entry of Record".** Tahoe gel-glass sub-register (a) *porcelain + gel object*, crossed with the device bank's #20 *data-as-glyph abstraction* and #24 *UI-primitive-as-mark*. The subject is a markdown file of record, so a porcelain tile is the paper itself rather than a borrowed ground, and the glyph quotes the exact artifact the skill acts on: an entry. Runner-up: Direction 7 *Diagonal Tool* (a broom or wrench crossing the plane), rejected on three counts: the catalogue names it the most template-worn move in the corpus, thin tool arms smear at 16px, and "maintenance skill, therefore a cleaning implement" is precisely the category-glyph trap the calibration flag warns about; the glyph has to name *this* subject, not its genre. Also considered: Direction 8 *Instrument Emblem*, dropped because armada-sync has no gauge or chart to quote; its in-product artifact is a line of text.

**Glyph (subject-mined, device bank #20 + #24 + #21 + #5):** five stacked capsule lines, left-aligned and ragged-right like prose, the last one trailing short because that is the single most reliable signal that a stack of bars is a *page* and not a chart. One wide vermilion seal band has landed on the exact middle line, overhanging its neighbours on both sides, and that entry is knocked out in frosted white *inside* the seal. Double read (#5): the band is simultaneously a stamp of record and a selected row in a table.

**Signature:** the seal lands on one line and nowhere else, with ink haloing softly into the porcelain around it; the entry was stamped a moment ago and the paper is still absorbing it. The frosted entry sits at 96→80% opacity down its height so the seal's hue visibly bleeds through its lower half (#21, authored overlap); that bleed is the era's craft tell and the thing a flat pre-masked raster cannot fake under system tinting. Risk taken: vermilion, in a field where the accent is almost always blue, and on a stack of rows where red carries a standing error-state association. It is held off by making the accent a band that *contains* the row rather than a red row, and both raster takes on the contact sheet are evidence of what happens when that discipline slips.

**Palette:** two families only: warm porcelain and graphite (`#FEFDFB→#F3F1EB→#E8E5DC` tile with a `#7A705C` vignette; `#5A616D→#434A55→#333944` entry lines) and one vermilion reserved strictly for the focal (`#F8845A→#EC5A33→#D33C21→#BB2F17`), with the frost at `#FFFFFF→#FFF3ED→#FFE2D6`. One soft top light: rim highlights on every capsule's top edge, soft contact shadows below all of them, zero hard speculars. The ink halo is a bleed into the paper, not a second light source.

**Deliberate avoidances:** the dark deep-sea register (held by the sibling trawl), amber as a primary accent (same reason), and the blue/indigo ramp the corpus census records as the template default across 271 chromatic hits. Also cut: a sub-legible diegetic engraved date on the seal. It is sanctioned garnish under Tahoe grammar #8 and it was the most subject-true detail available, since the skill's entire job is the `updated:` stamp; it went in the remove-one-accessory pass for costing rubric #12 in exchange for a detail that is gone by 128px.

**Audit: 11/12, zero failures on the non-negotiable 1–4.** Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the squircle as a *clip*, no baked corners or shadow. Glyph 640×536 optically centred on 512/512 with 192px side margins and 244px top and bottom, so the focal sits at 62.5% of tile width inside the 55–65% band and the safe zone is genuinely comfortable. Silhouette and the 16px squint were verified on real renders, not imagined: the frosted core survives inside the seal at 16px. Point deducted at #7 figure-ground, and it was found by rendering a grayscale proof rather than assuming a pass; desaturated, the vermilion resolves *lighter* than the graphite lines, so the value hierarchy inverts and the focal stops being the darkest element. The mark still reads, because the seal is the fattest and widest row and carries a knocked-out core, but under Tinted the figure-ground argument rests entirely on shape.

**#10 by construction:** four layers, `#bg` / `#mid` / `#fg` / `#highlight`, mapping 1:1 onto Icon Composer. Identity rides on shape and value; colour is the last ten percent.

**v1 → v2:** the first pass ran a milder line-length variance (470/530/504/408, a 1.31× range) and an ink halo at roughly half opacity. On the render it read closer to an equalizer than a page of text, and the signature's second half was invisible. v2 widened the range to 1.67× with a short trailing last line and lifted the halo. Both passes are on `audit.html` with real renders.

**Engines:** all three ran. Engine B (Arrow vector, `icon-engineB-arrow-4683c4.svg`) hard-failed #1 by baking its own corner mask with unequal radii, and drifted to an Aqua-era gloss sweep; it independently reached the same seal-overhang proportion, which confirmed that read. Engine C (GPT Image 2 raster, two takes, steered with the corpus porcelain exemplars) returned page-white with no cushion tile and a neon halo that smears the line separation by 16px. C2's frosted core rendered convincingly enough to justify carrying the seal's hue into the master's `frostGrad` lower half, and C1's wider line-length variance drove the v2 width pass. Neither raster ships: a flat pre-masked raster re-creates the corpus's #1 failure mode.
