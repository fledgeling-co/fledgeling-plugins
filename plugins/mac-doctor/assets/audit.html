A · icon.svghand-authored layered SVG, built by build_icon.py — SHIPS 11 / 12 Ships. Passes all four non-negotiables, and after a late composition change it now carries the subject better than any other take. The ember was originally a wedge detached outside the ring, meaning "a piece came out". Inline it says what the tool actually reports: three quantities at once, dark for used, ember for just reclaimed, and the remaining hole for free. That is also the conventional gauge idiom, so it reads without being learned. The segment sits centred in the hole with clearance either side, on butt caps and carries only its own edge catch, since an inline element that casts a float shadow reads as sitting on top rather than in the track. Geometry and material are named constants in build_icon.py, so it stays editable and can be made variant-aware. The ring is also heavier than the early builds, R 280 W 152 against 292 108, because a thin ring reads as a progress spinner and loses its divisions below 32px. Loses one point on 11: a ring gauge is legible but conventional.
C · icon-engineC-clean.pngGPT Image 2, corpus-referenced raster, deframed and remasked — material reference 10 / 12 Kept as the material reference, not shipped. Its gel is still the richest here: volumetric body, strong rim, authored translucency. Its disqualifying frame did turn out to be croppable (inner tile x 78-948, y 66-953, aspect 0.981), and for a while it shipped on that basis. It lost the place on composition rather than material: its ember is a solid triangle baked outside the ring, so it cannot express the inline three-part read, and being pixels it cannot be changed to. Also fails check 10 outright, since a baked raster cannot adapt to Dark, Clear or Tinted.
B · icon-engineB-arrow.svgArrow 1.1 vector take 6 / 12 Real vector and cleanly editable, but the geometry is wrong in the way that matters. The ring is nearly complete with only a small notch, so it does not read as a gauge that is nearly full, which is the entire concept. A second stray arc sits at the lower right with a visible seam. The ground is flat with no vignette or rim light, so the tile reads as a print rather than a cushion, and it supplies its own rounded-rectangle corners instead of the family superellipse. Nothing salvaged.
Recommendation: ship A (icon.svg). The raster won the material judgment and held the shipping slot for a while once its baked frame proved croppable, but composition decided it: only the authored vector could move the ember inline, and inline is what makes the mark state used, reclaimed and free at once instead of only "a piece came out". Editability is the second reason, and the one that matters over time. Known liabilities to revisit: the master is still less volumetric than C at 1024, and closing that means a bounded fidelity run against a raster regenerated with the inline composition rather than the current one. Check 10, variant robustness, remains untested; nothing has been rendered against Dark, Clear or Tinted, and a warm porcelain ground is exactly the kind that collapses under Tinted. The ring gauge is conventional, which is the lost rubric point.
