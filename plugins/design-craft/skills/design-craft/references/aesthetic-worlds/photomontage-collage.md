# Photomontage / collage — the poster composite

- **Family:** hand, archive and play
- **Best for:** music, fashion, culture and campaign pages, festivals, editorial features, launches that must not look like the last startup landing page
- **Touchstones:** Hannah Höch's photomontage; punk zines in the Jamie Reid tradition
- **Source:** [Kittl 2027 trends](https://www.kittl.com/blogs/graphic-design-trends-2027/) (collage, mixed media); [Canva 2027 "Rebel by Design"](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/canva-2027-design-trends/); [@adriankuleszo, 19 Nov 2026](https://x.com/adriankuleszo/status/2123316620437139880) (posters, composites and composed brand photography as the answer to the identical landing page); all accessed 2026-11-22

## Tokens

- **Palette:** photography carries the colour; one or two spot inks (a fluorescent or a primary) for type blocks and marks; black.
- **Type:** two or three display families chosen by one written rule ("nouns cut from a condensed grotesk, verbs in a fat serif"), plus one quiet text face at 16–18px for anything a visitor has to read.
- **Scale:** live display text stays under the `SKILL.md` §8 ceiling of 6rem. Type larger than that belongs inside the composite image, with the same words in live text beside it.
- **Spacing:** off-grid placement inside the composite; a strict grid in the reading sections beneath it.
- **Shape:** edges come from the photographs themselves (masked cut-outs, real torn paper from scans). No uniform radius anywhere.
- **Motion:** layers may drift on scroll by small, different amounts (under 24px of parallax) with a still composition under `prefers-reduced-motion`; or no motion at all.

## Signature moves

1. One composite per view, built from real photography: cut-outs with real edges overlapping at mixed scale.
2. Type laid both over and under image layers, with one deliberate collision per view — a word crossing a face, a headline cropped by an object.
3. Brand photography composed for the layout rather than taken from stock.
4. The collage is the cover, not the whole book: reading sections below return to a calm grid so the page stays usable.

## Avoid

Stock cut-out images with drop shadows; collage used as a sticker sprinkle over a product grid; live text over a busy image below 4.5:1 (set it on a solid block instead); and a half-committed version, which reads as broken rather than bold.

## Do not use when

The surface is Operate; every claim has to stay legible as plain text for regulatory review; or there is no real imagery and no image backend (`generate-images.md` Phase 3).

## Subject-mining prompt

What printed matter does this audience already collect — flyers, covers, packaging, setlists, receipts? Which two images would they cut out and pin up, and which single word would they paste across them?
