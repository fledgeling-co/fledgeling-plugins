# Y2K retrofuturist - chrome, glass, and early-web optimism

- **Family:** genre and material
- **Best for:** nostalgia-coded products, music, fashion, games, playful campaigns, 1999 to 2005 references
- **Touchstones:** iMac G3 advertising, Windows Aero, aqua interfaces, early web culture
- **Source:** [garden-skills recipe](https://github.com/ConardLi/garden-skills/blob/aaf9a82f5efd73e87cc0998edc398e75bfc35901/skills/web-design-engineer/references/style-recipes/y2k-retrofuturism.md) (snapshot `aaf9a82f`)

## Tokens

- **Palette:** ice blue or chrome ground, cyan-to-magenta iridescence, one acid pop, high-contrast ink.
- **Type:** extended or rounded display face with friendly humanist body text; digital numerals only when functional.
- **Scale:** packed 4 / 8 / 16 / 24px rhythm with a few oversized display moments.
- **Shape:** 16 / 24 / 32px and pill controls when the object is genuinely a bubble or capsule.
- **Shadow:** glossy highlight and coloured depth are part of the world, not generic elevation.
- **Motion:** bouncy spring, lens sweep, or one floating object. Reduced-motion becomes a still chrome composition.

## Signature moves

1. A physical glass or chrome object is the hero, not a normal card with a gradient behind it.
2. Specular highlights explain the material.
3. A digital readout or early-web control carries a real state.
4. One nostalgic interaction is repeated as a system, not sprinkled everywhere.

## Avoid

Do not add chrome to a normal SaaS page, use a retro label without a retro subject, or let effects hide the primary action and readable copy.

## Do not use when

The brand has no nostalgic claim, the audience is enterprise and trust-first, or long-form reading is the primary task.

## Subject-mining prompt

Which obsolete interface, material, or hardware object does this audience actually remember? Use that memory to choose the interaction, not just the colours.
