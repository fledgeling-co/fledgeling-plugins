# Apothecary object - Aesop / tactile restraint

- **Family:** quiet and human
- **Best for:** physical goods, hospitality, skincare, restaurants, object-led premium work
- **Touchstones:** Aesop product pages and store windows, the amber bottle
- **Source:** [garden-skills recipe](https://github.com/ConardLi/garden-skills/blob/aaf9a82f5efd73e87cc0998edc398e75bfc35901/skills/web-design-engineer/references/style-recipes/aesop.md) (snapshot `aaf9a82f`)

## Tokens

- **Palette:** mineral ground, dark ink, one botanical or amber signal. Keep the palette narrow.
- **Type:** a transitional serif for the reading voice; a plain sans for labels and controls.
- **Scale:** 16 to 18px body, generous leading, 10 to 11px letter-spaced labels.
- **Spacing:** 8 / 16 / 24 / 40 / 72 / 120px.
- **Shape:** square edges, no shadow. Material comes from the photographed object.
- **Motion:** slow fades and drawer movement around 450ms. Never spring-bounce.

## Signature moves

1. The object is photographed on a real surface with one supporting prop.
2. Product writing carries the hierarchy rather than feature-card copy.
3. Asymmetric image and long-copy blocks keep the product from becoming a tile.
4. Labels name a real collection or ritual, not a decorative section number.

## Avoid

Do not fake paper, stone, cloth, or grain in CSS when a real asset is the point. Do not add blue UI accents, loud buttons, lifestyle stock people, or pop-up interruptions without a task reason.

## Do not use when

The product is digital-first, comparison-led, playful, or used by a reader who needs rapid scanning.

## Subject-mining prompt

Which physical object, surface, ritual, or ingredient does the audience already associate with the product? Photograph or generate that object; do not draw its texture as chrome.
