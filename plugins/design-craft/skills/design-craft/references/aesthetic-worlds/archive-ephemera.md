# Archive / ephemera — the collection as the page

- **Family:** hand, archive and play
- **Best for:** museums and cultural institutions, heritage brands, retrospectives and anniversaries, research collections, personal archive and journaling tools
- **Touchstones:** Letterform Archive's online collection; museum collection-catalogue pages
- **Source:** [Canva 2027 Design Trends, "Archive Era"](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/canva-2027-design-trends/) (accessed 2026-11-22: "keepsake" searches +123%, "junk journal" +249%); `evidence.md` §12

## Tokens

- **Palette:** a true off-white ground, or the scanned stock's own colour only where real scans sit on it; near-black ink; one stamp or registration colour (a red or a blue) reserved for what the archivist added: dates, catalogue numbers, annotations.
- **Type:** an archival serif or a typewriter face for catalogue data; a plain label grotesk for interface text. Tabular figures for every number and date.
- **Scale:** 15–17px body; captions 12–13px tracked at 0.04em; catalogue numbers may run large as a typographic object.
- **Spacing:** 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 / 64px. Generous margins around each object, an 8px gap between an object and its caption.
- **Shape:** zero radius on scans, because they are rectangles of paper; hairline rules in catalogue tables; any shadow comes from the photographed edge, never from CSS.
- **Motion:** slow fades or none. An object can open into a detail view or zoom on request; it never floats.

## Signature moves

1. The scans are the material: tickets, letters, labels and photographs shot or scanned at 600 dpi with their edges, dust and folds kept.
2. Every object carries a catalogue line (number, date, source, dimensions), so provenance is part of the design.
3. A finding aid, an index or timeline, lets the visitor move through the collection by date, type or place.
4. The stamp colour separates what the archivist added from what the object itself carries.

## Avoid

CSS paper textures, fake masking tape, coffee-ring images, a typewriter webfont laid over an ordinary product layout, and catalogue numbers invented and presented as real. Without real objects this world is a costume: use the honest placeholder from `SKILL.md` §6 and say the page needs its scans before it can ship.

## Do not use when

The subject has no archive and the brief cannot supply one; the surface is Operate and speed matters more than browsing; or the category is one where an aged look reads as unmaintained (payments, health records, security).

## Subject-mining prompt

What does this subject keep — receipts, tickets, letters, packaging, prototypes, logs? Name three objects a visitor would recognise as belonging only to this subject, and write the catalogue line for each.
