# Tactile editorial - Stripe Press / books as objects

- **Family:** quiet and human
- **Best for:** publishing, longform features, books, ideas, considered cultural products
- **Touchstones:** Stripe Press, cloth-bound books, foil-stamped covers
- **Source:** [garden-skills recipe](https://github.com/ConardLi/garden-skills/blob/aaf9a82f5efd73e87cc0998edc398e75bfc35901/skills/web-design-engineer/references/style-recipes/stripe-press.md) (snapshot `aaf9a82f`)

## Tokens

- **Palette:** warm neutral ground, ink, one chapter or book accent, one hairline tone.
- **Type:** display serif plus body serif; a small humanist sans is utility only.
- **Scale:** body 18 to 19px with 1.65 line-height; display italic at 1.5 to 2 times body size.
- **Spacing:** 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 96px.
- **Shape:** zero radius. A real book or object can carry a soft shadow; content cards do not.
- **Motion:** slow crossfades; an object may rotate gently, never as a constant effect.

## Signature moves

1. The real physical object is the hero evidence.
2. A quote hangs into a wide margin rather than becoming a card.
3. One foil or chapter colour changes by context, never all at once.
4. Section breaks use material or a considered image, not a generic gradient.

## Avoid

Do not use card grids for books, sans-serif body copy, cold neutral grey, or a cream palette without a real editorial subject. Do not copy a publisher's mark or cover.

## Do not use when

The audience will not read longform, the offer is a quick comparison, or the subject has no object or publication logic.

## Subject-mining prompt

What is the artefact the visitor would keep, cite, shelve, or return to? Make that artefact the compositional authority.
