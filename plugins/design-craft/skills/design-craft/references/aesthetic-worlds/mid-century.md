# Mid-century geometry - Paul Rand / Saul Bass

- **Family:** genre and material
- **Best for:** cultural identity, film, publishing, heritage products, poster-like campaign pages
- **Touchstones:** Paul Rand identities, Saul Bass title sequences, jazz-album sleeves
- **Source:** [garden-skills recipe](https://github.com/ConardLi/garden-skills/blob/aaf9a82f5efd73e87cc0998edc398e75bfc35901/skills/web-design-engineer/references/style-recipes/mid-century-modern.md) (snapshot `aaf9a82f`)

## Tokens

- **Palette:** three colours maximum: one bold ground or field, one muted support, ink.
- **Type:** geometric grotesk or slab display with a humanist text face.
- **Scale:** one oversized numeral or title, then a compact supporting ramp.
- **Spacing:** structured 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48px grid.
- **Shape:** zero radius, flat colour shapes, no shadow.
- **Motion:** one title-sequence-like entrance, then stillness.

## Signature moves

1. One geometric shape carries the primary visual idea.
2. Type sits inside, around, or against the shape rather than above a generic hero.
3. A single motif repeats across sections and earns recognition.
4. Texture is real print evidence or a restrained asset, not a random grain filter.

## Avoid

Do not use gradients, rounded product cards, more than three colours, or realistic photography as the lead when the subject has no object reason.

## Do not use when

The surface is feature-dense, operational, or needs a highly familiar product grammar.

## Subject-mining prompt

What single symbol, shape, or gesture can the audience recognise before reading the words? Make it structural, not a sticker.
