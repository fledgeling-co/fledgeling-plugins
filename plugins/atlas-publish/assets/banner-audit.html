Recommendation.

Ships.

The composition is derived from `build_icon.py` rather than from a sibling banner: the ground ramp hangs off the icon's own `KEY = (0.34, 0.26)`, and the graphite, vermilion, vignette and shadow values are the master's constants unchanged (`#454B57 / #2C313A / #161A20`, `#F2823C / #DE5A28 / #C9481C`, `#8B7F66`, `#6E6049`). The face is Instrument Sans, linked from Google Fonts, which is the plurality face across the set and therefore the closest thing this marketplace has to a set wordmark; the measured advance came back 1.1975 against the monospace control, so it loaded rather than falling back.

Two devices carry the icon's move onto the sheet. On the right, a queue of four graphite rails runs to a single vermilion gate and every one of them stops flush against it, with bare porcelain past it: the skill takes the open PRs to a sealed release and goes no further. In the wordmark, the hyphen of `atlas-publish` is poured as that same gate, at the master's own 62:294 bar proportion.

Legibility: the wordmark and both halves of the essence line hold at 900px and at 400px. At 200px the tagline is a texture, which is what 200px is.

Two constructions were built as their own elements rather than as CSS effects, because this engine accepts both and paints neither: the icon's contact shadow is a backing box rather than a `filter: drop-shadow`, and every rim light is a positioned child rather than an `inset` box-shadow. `render_banner.py` refuses a source containing either, so this is enforced rather than remembered.

Known liabilities: the rails read slightly heavier than the lockup at full size, and shifting the queue 68px left plus dropping their shadow opacity was a judgment call rather than a measurement. The vermilion gate sits directly on the porcelain in both devices and separates from the graphite by hue and hard edge rather than by luminance, which is the same liability the icon carries. Rows 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 of the sheet are one person's assessment, not a panel's.
