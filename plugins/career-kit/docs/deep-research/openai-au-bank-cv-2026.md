## 2.3 Twenty-plus years with one employer

### Best structure: grouped employer, independently readable roles

**Recommended approach [Medium—recommendation]:**

- One clear employer heading.
- Separate title and date range for each substantial role.
- Scope statement and achievements beneath each recent role.
- Approximately **10–12 years in meaningful detail**.
- Earlier relevant roles compressed into a career-progression section.
- Complete chronology retained in a private master record and supplied whenever an application or background-check form requests it.

For application forms and online profiles, enter each substantive position separately. On LinkedIn, correctly associated consecutive positions can be grouped automatically under the same employer. **[High—platform behaviour]** ([LinkedIn position-grouping help, checked 19 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552185/)). ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552185/))

### What to avoid

**[Medium—recommendations]**

- `Large Bank | 2003–Present | Product Owner` when she became a PO only recently.
- A functional CV that removes role dates and obscures progression.
- Describing every internal move as a promotion.
- Presenting concurrent secondments as consecutive permanent jobs.
- Replacing an official title with a more senior title.

For an obscure title, retain it and add a truthful translation:

> **Customer Solutions Lead — Product Owner, digital servicing**

### How to demonstrate adaptability

Use a short progression statement supported by the actual record:

> Progressed through **[number] roles across [functions]**, including **[number] promotions**, combining frontline operations, people leadership and agile product delivery.

Then prove breadth through changes in:

- customer segment or product;
- technology and delivery method;
- risk/accountability;
- team size;
- department and stakeholder group.

**[Medium—recommendation]** This is stronger than claiming “loyal and adaptable” without evidence.

### Age-bias management without concealment

AHRI/AHRC’s 2025 research confirms continuing age-related hiring concerns, including perceived salary expectations and “too much experience”. It does not establish that this candidate will face bias, or that hiding dates prevents it. **[High—research finding; Low—individual prediction]** ([AHRI/AHRC Older and Younger Workers Report, 2025](https://www.ahri.com.au/resources/hr-research/older-and-younger-workers-report-2025)). ([ahri.com.au](https://www.ahri.com.au/resources/hr-research/older-and-younger-workers-report-2025))

**Recommended response [Medium]:**

- Lead with current capability, not “23 years of loyal service”.
- Show recent technology learning and delivery examples.
- Remove school details and irrelevant early duties.
- Consider omitting old graduation years, while answering required forms accurately.
- Retain honest employment continuity.

SEEK’s July 2026 age-bias guidance similarly recommends concentrating on recent, relevant experience rather than decades-old junior roles. **[High—guidance]** ([SEEK, updated 27 July 2026](https://au.seek.com/career-advice/article/ask-sabina-how-to-combat-age-discrimination-in-your-job-search)). ([au.seek.com](https://au.seek.com/career-advice/article/ask-sabina-how-to-combat-age-discrimination-in-your-job-search))

### Implications for the candidate

**1. Separate product accountability from delivery coordination. [Medium—sample interpretation]**

For a PO/PM application, establish:

- What customer or business problem did she own?
- Which prioritisation decisions were hers?
- What product or journey improved?
- How did she measure value?
- How were risk and commercial trade-offs handled?

For a Delivery Lead application, establish:

- How many teams/workstreams?
- Which dependencies and blockers?
- What planning, governance and release responsibilities?
- What improved in flow, predictability or readiness?

**2. Seniority must be demonstrated through scope—not just tenure. [Medium—recommendation]**

Show cross-functional influence, portfolio/roadmap decisions, complexity, production accountability and measurable outcomes. Do not add “Senior” merely because she has long service.

**3. Distinguish direct people management from squad leadership. [Medium—recommendation]**

Use both where accurate:

> Directly managed **[N] employees**, including performance and development; led delivery across a **[N]-person cross-functional squad** without line-management authority over all members.

**4. Domain experience can be decisive. [Medium—sample interpretation]**

Judo asks for lending-product experience; ING’s transaction-banking role asks for payments/cash-management understanding. An excellent generic PO CV may still be weak for these specific portfolios. Use genuine domain breadth to select suitable vacancies, not to claim expertise in every banking product.

### Recommended writing workflow

**[Medium—recommendations]**

1. Build a factual evidence inventory herself.
2. Use approved AI tools to reorganise or shorten—not manufacture experience.
3. Replace generic language with product, customer, team and decision specifics.
4. Check every metric, certification and ownership claim.
5. Read the document aloud: could she explain each sentence naturally?
6. Keep confidential customer, employee and commercially sensitive information out of public AI tools.
7. Follow disclosure or no-AI instructions where an employer provides them.

Avoid prose such as:

> “Visionary, results-driven transformation leader leveraging cutting-edge synergies…”

Prefer:

> “Product Owner and people leader with experience simplifying banking operations and delivering digital-service improvements.”

### Manager and referee etiquette

**[Medium—recommended sequence, subject to employer policy]**

5. Frame the move as development and enterprise contribution—not escape.

**All lengths below are analyst recommendations—Medium confidence, not bank rules.**

```text

City, State | Personal mobile | Personal email | LinkedIn URL
Australian work-rights statement, if useful and accurate

One role family, with 1–2 relevant differentiators.

- Advertised capabilities she genuinely possesses
- Relevant tools/frameworks
```

# 6. Before/after achievement rewrites

**All numbers below are fictional illustrations. They are not candidate claims or suggested targets. Recommendation confidence: Medium.**

| Vague duty | Stronger, evidence-bearing version |
|---|---|
| **“Managed the product backlog.”** | “Reprioritised a 120-item servicing backlog using customer impact, control obligations and squad capacity; reduced median feature lead time from 20 to 12 business days over two quarters.” |
| **“Improved customer experience.”** | “Led discovery and delivery of a simplified digital application journey, increasing completion from 58% to 72% over six months while maintaining the agreed verification controls.” |
| **“Worked with stakeholders to deliver projects.”** | “Aligned Operations, Technology and Risk on dependencies across three workstreams; delivered the servicing release to the agreed quarter-end milestone and completed operational-readiness sign-off before launch.” |
| **“Responsible for compliance.”** | “Coordinated remediation of 18 overdue control actions across six critical-operation processes, closing all actions within the 12-week programme scope and retaining evidence for independent review.” |
| **“Managed a team.”** | “Directly managed 14 operations employees; introduced weekly coaching and workload reviews, improving SLA attainment from 84% to 96% in six months while reducing processing rework by 30%.” |
| **“Developed staff.”** | “Built role-based learning plans and coached three employees into internal promotions; reduced new-starter time to agreed proficiency from 12 to eight weeks.” |
| **“Improved staff engagement and retention.”** | “Introduced structured development conversations and clearer workload allocation for a 20-person team; annual voluntary turnover fell from 18% to 10%, alongside improvement in the team’s engagement measure.” |
| **“Supported change management.”** | “Led readiness, communications and role-based training for 120 colleagues during a workflow rollout; increased verified active use from 55% to 88% within eight weeks of launch.” |

### Measurement discipline

**[Medium—recommendations]**

- State the baseline, result, timeframe and population.
- Distinguish **percentage points** from relative percentage change.
- Separate realised savings from forecast benefits.
- Describe the contribution accurately: *owned, led, coordinated, contributed*.
- Where several factors influenced an outcome, avoid claiming sole causation.
- If confidential, use approved rounded values, ranges or percentages.
- If no outcome data exists, quantify scope and delivery evidence rather than inventing a result.

A useful evidence record is:

> **Problem → personal decision/action → scope → result → timeframe → source of verification.**

## A. ATS/AI-safe formatting

- [ ] One column; conventional section headings.
- [ ] Contact details and substantive content in the body.
- [ ] No tables/text boxes arranging career history.
- [ ] No graphics, skill bars or scanned pages.
- [ ] DOCX master and selectable-text PDF available.
- [ ] File type/size follows the actual portal.
- [ ] Recent roles have separate titles and month/year dates.
- [ ] Copy-and-paste reading order checked.
- [ ] Parsed application fields checked and corrected.
- [ ] Exact keywords supported by examples—not repeated blocks.
- [ ] No invisible text, copied job description or AI instructions.
- [ ] Final document proofread by a person.

- [ ] Correct employee/internal portal used.
- [ ] Current mobility policy and eligibility checked.
- [ ] Manager-notification timing clarified.
- [ ] Receiving department’s priorities addressed.
- [ ] Cross-department contribution made explicit.
- [ ] Internal jargon understandable outside the current team.
- [ ] Internal referees asked for consent.
- [ ] No assumption that tenure guarantees an interview.
- [ ] Handover and learning needs considered.
- [ ] Probity/check requirements confirmed for the new role.

- [ ] One primary role family targeted.
- [ ] Employer, titles and dates independently understandable.

- [ ] Most relevant recent achievements prominent.

- [ ] Confidential information removed.
- [ ] Work rights and screening answers accurate.
- [ ] Old internal-only CV not uploaded accidentally.
- [ ] Referees ready, but details withheld unless requested.

- [ ] Employer, role dates, qualifications and seniority agree.

# 8. Knowledge gaps and what could not be verified

1. **Named-bank AI configurations—Low confidence.** Public ATS evidence does not reveal enabled ranking modules, weights, knockout settings, prompts or recruiter overrides. No verified bank-specific “pass score” was found.

2. **Eightfold, Phenom and HiredScore deployments—unconfirmed.** Available secondary references and indirect signals were insufficient to establish current Australian-bank deployment or the processing of these role families.

3. **Macquarie’s underlying current ATS vendor—unconfirmed.** The bank-linked portal was identifiable, but accessible primary material did not conclusively establish the vendor. Its published AI-assistance policy was verifiable.

4. **Internal workflows—partly private.** Manager approvals, notification timing, performance eligibility, priority/redeployment rules and internal screening configurations must be checked with the current employer.

5. **Advertisement coverage—limited.** The sample is current/recent and includes SEEK, LinkedIn and employer postings, but is not comprehensive. It underrepresents ordinary operational Team Manager roles and does not establish 2025–2026 market-wide frequency. Several advertised group roles are not vacancies in the banking legal entity itself.

6. **AI-authorship detection—unverified.** Survey attitudes do not demonstrate reliable detection. No independent, bank-specific detector validation was found.

7. **Changing ownership and systems.** Older PageUp/Greenhouse references were not treated as current where present application links differed. Beem’s current recruitment system and precise hiring entity require fresh vacancy-level verification.

## Recommended order of work this month

**[Medium—recommendation]**

1. **Build the evidence inventory:** roles, promotions, direct-report numbers, product scope and 12–15 defensible achievements.
2. **Create the internal-target CV first:** one real vacancy, not a hypothetical all-purpose transfer.
3. **Produce the three-page external master and focused variants.**
4. **Update LinkedIn and SEEK together**, keeping the same factual chronology.
5. **Test one complete application**, including parsed fields, before applying more broadly.

**The central strategy is not to “beat AI”. It is to make her progression, relevant authority and demonstrated results difficult for either software or a hiring manager to misunderstand.**
