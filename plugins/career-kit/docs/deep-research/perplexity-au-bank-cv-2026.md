# Optimising CV, LinkedIn and Seek Profiles for Product and Delivery Roles in Australian Banking, 2025–2026

For a senior Australian candidate with more than twenty years at a single large financial-services organisation, the most effective CV, LinkedIn and Seek profiles in late 2026 will balance two imperatives: first, being technically “ATS- and AI-readable” for systems such as Workday and SAP SuccessFactors so they are parsed, scored and surfaced correctly; and second, persuading human hiring managers in major banks that a long tenure reflects progression, breadth and contemporary product, agile and leadership capability rather than stagnation. Drawing on evidence from Australian recruitment guidance, ATS vendor documentation, banking careers sites and age-discrimination research, the optimal approach is a three-page, achievements-focused CV with clean, text-only formatting; explicit alignment to agile product and risk/compliance vocabularies used in Australian banking; clear signalling of work rights and background-check readiness; and strongly updated narratives for the last ten to twelve years, framed as a series of promotions and internal moves within the one employer. ### 1.2 How Workday, SAP SuccessFactors and Similar ATS Parse and Rank Resumes

Understanding how ATS parse and rank resumes is crucial to designing documents that will be correctly interpreted. Workday’s resume parsing process has been explicitly tested in practice: when a candidate uploads a resume to a Workday-powered job page, Workday converts the file into plain text, strips away layout, tables and headers, and then searches for patterns it recognises—employers, job titles, start and end dates, degrees, and skills—dropping each into a corresponding field on the application form.[12] The recruiter primarily reads this form rather than your original PDF, which may remain attached but unopened for many applications.[12] Confidence: High, based on direct testing of Workday’s behaviour.[12]

The testing described in the Workday-focused article shows that most core information—job titles, employers, dates and skills—parsed correctly for cleanly formatted resumes, and that even a two-column layout was interpreted acceptably.[12] However, certain formatting choices caused problems. For example, when projects were listed alongside jobs without clear separation, Workday treated all four entries as work experience and asked for a company name on each.[12] Loose or creative phrasing of degrees led Workday to misclassify the field of study, reading a “Bachelor’s in Computer Science” as a “Bachelor of Arts” because it could not clearly parse the text.[12] These issues underscore the need for plain, standard headings (such as “Work Experience” and “Education”) and consistent job-structure patterns. Confidence: High for these findings, which come from empirical testing.[12]

The article also clarifies that Workday can read both DOCX and text-based PDFs, but not scanned or image-only PDFs; if you cannot highlight text in your file, the parser has nothing to read.[12] It furthermore advises that content should not be placed in images, charts, graphs or tables, as these are not reliably parsed, and recommends avoiding icons instead of words like “phone” and “email.”[12] Contact details should be placed as plain text at the top of the document, and the resume saved as a text-based PDF or Word file.[12] Confidence: High for these technical constraints, again based on direct testing.[12]

SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting behaves similarly in its handling of resumes. The official procedure for completing a candidate profile and submitting an application explains that when a resume is uploaded—whether from a local device, Dropbox or Google Drive—information from the resume is automatically parsed into the designated candidate profile fields.[18][19] After uploading, the candidate is asked to fill mandatory fields marked with a red asterisk, and can complete background sections such as Outside Work Experience, Formal Education, Language Skills and Geographic Mobility.[18][19] This suggests that SuccessFactors, like Workday, performs a structured extraction of resume data, creates a candidate record, and then requires manual completion or correction of any missing or mis-parsed elements. Confidence: High, as this is directly from vendor documentation.[18][19]

SuccessFactors also integrates prescreening questions and job-specific information fields, including country-specific sections, into the application process.[18][19] Candidates may be asked to answer yes/no or multiple-choice questions that act as knockout filters (for example, regarding work rights, necessary certifications or willingness to relocate), and must complete additional fields such as employment information and postal codes before submitting.[18][19] While the documentation does not explicitly detail ranking algorithms, it makes clear that the system uses a combination of parsed resume data and structured form responses to present candidate profiles to recruiters. Confidence: High about prescreening questions and structured data; Medium about how these are algorithmically ranked, which is not fully described.[18][19]

Australian recruitment guidance for 2025 highlights similar concerns around ATS parsing. Randstad advises candidates to use a clean format, avoid images, tables or fancy fonts, and include keywords from the job description.[14] It notes that many resumes never reach a human reader because they fail to pass ATS filters, and recommends including specific metrics, such as “increased sales by 30% in six months,” to stand out.[14] Combined with Workday and SuccessFactors documentation, this reinforces that ATS systems rely heavily on recognizable text patterns and keywords, and may not interpret creative layouts or unconventional phrasing reliably.[12][14][18][19] Confidence: High for the general ATS-friendly recommendations.[14]

With respect to ranking mechanisms, ATS historically relied on keyword matching and rule-based scoring. For example, they might assign points when a candidate’s resume includes specific required skills, job titles or degrees, and filter out resumes lacking mandatory terms or failing knockout questions. Modern ATS, particularly when integrated with AI-powered matching tools, are increasingly capable of semantic matching—recognising that “Product Owner” and “Product Manager” or “fraud management” and “financial crime” are conceptually related even if exact terms differ. However, the provided sources do not detail the semantic algorithms used by Workday or SuccessFactors in 2025–2026. Based on global trends and vendor announcements outside these sources, it is reasonable to infer that large banks may use AI-enhanced matching, sometimes drawing on platforms like Eightfold or Phenom, but this remains an inference in the absence of explicit citations. Confidence: Medium that semantic matching occurs; Low regarding specific tools deployed by the named banks.

## 2. Australian CV Conventions in 2026

### 2.1 Length, Structure and Section Order

Australian recruiters, including large firms like Randstad, continue to emphasise concise, skills-focused CVs for the local market. Guidance for Australian job seekers in 2025 describes employer expectations of CVs that are usually one to two pages in length, emphasise skills and achievements, and list work experience in reverse chronological order.[14][15] For early-career candidates, these norms apply strictly: a two-page maximum, minimal detail on minor roles, and heavy emphasis on transferable skills and any local volunteering or certifications.[15] Confidence: High, based on explicit guidance from Australian recruiters.[14][15]

However, these general conventions must be interpreted in the context of senior candidates. A candidate with more than twenty years at one large financial institution, including people management, product ownership and delivery leadership, simply cannot convey relevant depth in one or two pages without compromising clarity or omitting important roles. In Australian practice for senior roles—particularly managerial and specialist positions—it is common and acceptable to use a three-page CV, provided it is tightly written, free of redundancy, and focused on achievements rather than exhaustive detail. This practice is not directly documented in the provided sources, but aligns with recruiter and hiring manager expectations observed across Australian banking and professional services. Confidence: Medium; this is an inference grounded in professional experience.

For your CV, the recommended structure is therefore three pages, arranged in a standard section order that ATS and humans both recognise. A typical order would be: name and contact details; a short professional summary; a key skills and expertise section, tailored to product, delivery and banking; a career history section (reverse chronological, emphasising the last ten to twelve years); education and certifications; and optionally additional sections such as professional memberships or community involvement if directly relevant. Workday guidance emphasises the importance of standard headings like “Work Experience” and “Education,” and of listing your latest job first; following this pattern ensures ATS can parse and categorise your entries effectively.[12] Confidence: High that this section order and reverse chronological structure support ATS parsing and recruiter expectations.[12][14]

The career history section should devote most space to your last ten to twelve years, which likely encompasses your product ownership, delivery and leadership roles. Earlier roles can be summarised in fewer lines, sometimes grouped under a heading such as “Earlier roles at [Bank Name]” with compressed descriptions. This approach both recognises your long tenure and reduces potential age-bias impacts by limiting detail on very early roles while emphasising progression and breadth. Confidence: Medium; this strategy is grounded in professional advice and age-discrimination research but not explicitly documented as a standard.[17]

### 2.2 Personal Details: What to Include and What to Leave Out

Australian CV conventions strongly discourage including personal details that are irrelevant to job performance or potentially discriminatory. Randstad’s guidance for newcomers to the Australian market explicitly notes that employers expect resumes without photos, dates of birth or marital status, and emphasises achievements rather than duties.[15] It also advises listing work experience in reverse chronological order and focusing on skills.[15] Confidence: High, based on explicit recruiter advice.[15]

You should therefore omit a headshot, date of birth, marital status, and detailed home address. A city and state (for example, “Sydney, NSW”) are sufficient to signal location without exposing sensitive details. Your contact section should include your full name, a professional email address, a mobile number formatted without the international prefix (Workday testing indicated that phone numbers with a “+1” prefix were mis-parsed), and a link to your LinkedIn profile.[12][15] Confidence: High that omitting photos and sensitive personal data aligns with Australian practice; High that using plain-text contact details, including a phone number without country code, aids ATS parsing.[12][15]

Regarding referees, Australian convention has historically been to state “Referees available on request” rather than listing names and contact details on the CV itself, particularly when applying externally. This protects your referees’ privacy and allows you to tailor which referees to provide for each role. Internal applications sometimes list one or two internal referees, as these may be well-known to the hiring manager and add credibility. However, even internally, you can simply note that referees are available upon request and mention in your cover letter that you can provide internal references from specific leaders if helpful. This practice is widely observed in Australian recruitment, though not explicitly documented in the provided sources. Confidence: Medium; inference based on professional practice.

Work-rights and citizenship are more important in Australian CVs than in some other markets, especially for banks dealing with regulatory and security requirements. Randstad recommends that newcomers to Australia add a line stating their eligibility to work, such as “Eligible to work full-time on [Visa Type] (valid until 2026),” to remove doubt for employers.[15] For an Australian citizen or permanent resident applying to banks, a line such as “Australian citizen, unrestricted right to work” or “Permanent resident, unrestricted right to work in Australia” can be included in the professional summary or a separate “Work rights” section. This clarifies a key ATS prescreening criterion and helps you pass knockout filters related to work eligibility.[15][18][19] Confidence: High that explicitly stating work rights is beneficial, given recruiter guidance and prescreening questions in ATS.[15][18][19]

Banks often require background checks, including police checks, employment and education verification, and in some cases credit checks, before finalising offers. While they rarely expect you to declare your background-check status on a CV, you can subtly signal readiness by noting in your summary or additional information that you have successfully completed background checks in your current banking role, or that you are accustomed to operating under strict compliance frameworks. This is more about reassuring hiring managers than influencing ATS, and should not be overemphasised. Confidence: Medium; inference based on banking hiring processes rather than explicit documentation.

Finally, Australian CVs should use Australian spelling and terminology. This means “organisation” rather than “organization,” “behaviour” rather than “behavior,” and references to “CV” more than “résumé” in local communications. ATS systems are not sensitive to spelling variants in the same way humans are, but bank recruiters may prefer candidates who adopt local language conventions, especially for roles requiring strong written communication. Confidence: Medium; inference based on linguistic norms and recruiter expectations.

### 2.3 Career Summary / Profile and Tone

A short career summary or professional profile at the top of the CV is now a common and effective feature for Australian candidates, particularly for senior roles. Recruiter guidance emphasises that your resume and cover letter must present a cohesive story about your qualifications and that tailored content is crucial.[16] A summary allows you to immediately position yourself as a “Senior Product Owner and People Leader in Australian Banking” or similar, and to highlight your core domains—such as agile delivery, customer outcomes, fraud and scams, APRA compliance and digital transformation—in a few lines. Confidence: High that a tailored summary improves recruiter and hiring manager comprehension.[14][16]

The summary should be written in a human, specific voice, not generic corporate jargon. Given concerns about AI-generated CVs, a summary that reads as highly generic (for example, “Results-oriented leader with strong communication skills and a proven track record of success”) may not be persuasive even if it passes ATS filters. Instead, aim for wording that reflects your actual context, such as “Senior Product Owner in Australian retail banking with 8+ years leading cross-functional squads across payments, fraud prevention and customer remediation, and more than 15 years’ experience managing teams in contact centre and operations environments.” This specificity helps human readers, and AI summarisation tools, quickly identify your relevance. Confidence: Medium to High; inference based on recruiter feedback and the need to differentiate in AI-assisted reading.

Tone should be professional but not stilted. Australian banking hiring managers tend to respond well to straightforward, honest language that emphasises outcomes and collaboration. Overly florid or buzzword-heavy wording can appear insincere, and may be more easily flagged as AI-generated. There is no evidence in the provided sources that banks use AI-detection tools to penalise such text, but anecdotal recruiter feedback suggests they are increasingly skeptical of generic phrasing. Confidence: Medium; inference from professional practice.

### 2.4 Comparison with US Conventions and Implications

Because many online CV templates and advice columns are US-based, it is important to distinguish Australian practice from American norms. ## 12. Formatting and Alignment Checklists (Narrative Guidance)

### 12.1 ATS/AI-Safe Formatting

To ensure ATS and AI systems can fully parse and interpret your CV, the following formatting principles should be applied. Use a standard, text-based Word or PDF format, ensuring that all content is selectable and not embedded in images. Place contact details at the top as plain text, avoiding icons or graphical representations of phone or email.[12] Use standard headings such as “Professional Summary,” “Key Skills,” “Work Experience” and “Education,” and structure each job entry with title, employer, location (optional), dates and achievement paragraphs.[12][18][19] Avoid placing key content inside tables, text boxes, columns or images; while Workday tested a two-column layout successfully, simpler structures are more reliably parsed.[12][14] Confidence: High, based on Workday and recruiter guidance.[12][14]

Ensure date formats are consistent and clear, such as “Jan 2020 – Present” or “2015–2020,” rather than vague phrases like “for five years.” ATS systems look for recognizable date patterns to calculate tenure.[12] Do not group jobs by skills; list them chronologically. Avoid overly creative naming of headings (for example, “My Journey” instead of “Work Experience”), as ATS may not interpret unconventional headings correctly.[12] Confidence: High; Workday guidance explicitly advises standard headings and date-based job lists.[12]

### 12.2 Internal-Application Version

Your internal CV can make use of internal program names and acronyms, but should still be ATS-friendly. It may be slightly shorter, as internal recruiters and managers have access to broader performance and HR data. Emphasise internal achievements that relate to the target department and explicitly note cross-functional collaborations within the bank. You can include references to internal awards, recognition and participation in internal leadership programs, as these may carry weight internally. Ensure that your internal CV is updated within the bank’s talent system, such as SuccessFactors, if applicable, and that your internal profile matches your CV’s content.[13][18][19] Confidence: Medium; inference based on SuccessFactors and internal mobility practices.[13][18][19]

### 12.3 External-Application Version

Your external CV should be slightly more explanatory in references to internal programs, using generic descriptors alongside internal names. It should also be tailored to each bank’s culture and language as far as possible, based on public information. For example, if ANZ emphasises “customer journeys” and “tribes,” use those terms where appropriate; if CBA emphasises “customer obsession,” align your wording. The core ATS-friendly formatting remains the same, but your summary and skills sections should be customised with the bank’s keywords. Confidence: Medium; inference based on external hiring practices.

### 12.4 LinkedIn and Seek Alignment

Your LinkedIn and Seek profiles must align with your CV in titles, dates and core narrative, but adapt tone and length to each platform. LinkedIn can host more detail in experience entries, including additional metrics and projects not fully covered in the CV. Ensure that job titles match exactly across platforms to avoid confusion; for example, do not use “Product Owner” on CV and “Product Manager” on LinkedIn for the same role unless that reflects real title differences.
