2. Typography: Typefaces, Point Sizes, Emphasis, Capitalisation, Colour, and Bullets Australian recruiter and government guidance prioritises ubiquitous, high-legibility system fonts over decorative or condensed faces (High Confidence) . Across sans-serif options, Calibri and Arial
 are co-recommended by Tier (b) SEEK , Tier (b) Hays , contracted provider APM , Tier (a) DEWR Job Jumpstart (Arial named for cover letters
 ), and DEWR as reported by MyPerfectResume ([SECONDARY: promotional], reporting Calibri or Arial at 11 pt)
 . Tier (b) SEEK's font guide additionally endorses sans-serifs Helvetica, Verdana, and Trebuchet MS
, alongside serif typefaces Georgia (noted for screen clarity), Cambria, Garamond, Book Antiqua, and Times New Roman
—while cautioning that sans-serif fonts are generally preferred  and Times New Roman can appear 
"a little old-fashioned or traditional" . Up to two complementary fonts (headings vs body) are permissible, though one family maximises visual consistency . Even in late 2026, Australian recruiter lists have not formally updated to name Microsoft 365's newer default Aptos, which carries nearly identical character-width metrics (0.49 vs 0.47
 width factor) to Calibri. Published point-size envelopes converge tightly on 11 pt for body copy and 14–16 pt
 for section headings (High Confidence). Tier (c) Flinders University specifies 11-point ; Tier (b) Michael Page sets an absolute minimum of 
10-point (no smaller than 10-point font) ; Tier (b) Hays specifies 10 or 12pt ; Tier (b) SEEK recommends 
10 to 12 point body copy in its font guide  and 11–12pt in its general resume and cover-letter guides
 ; and Tier (b) Robert Half's US guide concurs on 11- or 12-point  (note that Tier (a) Style Manual's ≥14 pt
 rule applies solely to specialist Easy Read cognitive-disability publications, explicitly contrasted against "Standard font size"
 for plain language ). For section headings, Tier (b) SEEK specifies 14 to 16 points
 , while Tier (a) Australian Government Style Manual restricts hierarchy to 3 or 4 heading levels at the most using inbuilt heading styles (
Heading 1, Heading 2) with visibly distinct weights and sizes .

What is the current state, and what is the strongest supporting evidence for it? The current state of corporate Australian résumé design in late 2026 is a machine-first, single-column A4 document architecture (2–3 pages) governed by enterprise ATS ingestion mechanics and agency .docx
 workflows rather than graphic-design templates (High Confidence) . Primary employer documentation confirms that CommBank routes banking candidates through Workday with pre-submission summary verification  and Victorian Government agencies route applicants through dedicated ATS portals . Official vendor documentation from Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, and Greenhouse establishes that columned layouts, tables, text boxes, header/footer-trapped contact details, and image-based PDFs cause parsing failures or dropped fields, while Workday's explicit exclusion of Skills and 
Languages from auto-fill forces candidates to embed keywords directly inside chronological role bullets rather than graphic skill bars . Simultaneously, live 2026 Australian job postings from Robert Half across operational risk, programme management, and human resources require applicants to upload CVs in Microsoft Word (.doc or .docx)
 , reinforcing SEEK's assessment that .docx remains the most universally compatible ATS file format alongside non-image .pdf files
 . Underneath these technical rules, Australian anti-bias and accessibility norms across Tier (a) APSC, Tier (a) Style Manual, Tier (b) SEEK, and Tier (c) University of Sydney firmly prohibit photos, age, gender, and marital status while requiring multi-page depth (2–3 pages corporate; 2–6 pages APS) and WCAG 2.2-aligned sentence-case hierarchy
 . What are the contrasting viewpoints or competing evidence? Four substantive conflicts divide published Australian career guidance (High Confidence). First, visual two-column Page 1 space packing clashes with linear ATS parsing: SEEK's visual guide argues that two columns on Page 1 make optimal use of prime screen real estate  and Study Australia promotes Canva and Kickresume.com templates

: DEWR Job Jumpstart instructs candidates to place their name, phone, and email on every page , whereas Greenhouse documents failed parses when primary contact details sit inside document headers or footers .
Third, .docx agency mandates compete with .pdf layout preservation: Robert Half's Australian job ads mandate
