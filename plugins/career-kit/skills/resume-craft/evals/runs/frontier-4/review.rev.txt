## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 Screening technology: what the evidence actually establishes

There are four different operations to prepare for:

| Operation | What it does | Candidate implication |
|---|---|---|
| Parsing | Extracts employer, title, dates, education, skills and contact fields. | Make these explicit and correct extraction errors. |
| Eligibility screening | Uses answers about matters such as work rights, location or required experience. | Answer accurately; CV keywords cannot compensate for an ineligible answer. |
| Search, matching and prioritisation | Finds candidates or orders applications against recruiter criteria. | Use recognisable terminology and show where and when the skill was used. |
| Human selection | Evaluates evidence, judgement, motivation and fit, potentially assisted by summaries. | Give a hiring manager concrete decisions and outcomes to discuss. |

**Confidence: High for the documented functions; Medium for this combined model.** Greenhouse describes field extraction; NAB describes role-specific questions; LinkedIn describes criteria-based evaluation and human decisions. [Greenhouse, 2 March 2026](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse); [NAB, undated; accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.nab.com.au/about-us/careers/apply-for-job); [LinkedIn FAQ, accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a7109304).

**An ATS logo does not establish which AI features are enabled.** These are documented vendor capabilities, not confirmed deployments at every customer:

| Product | Verified capability | Limit on the claim |
|---|---|---|
| Workday HiredScore | August 2026 release describes an LLM evaluating individual qualifications against parsed CV content, with fit/gap explanations. | Does not prove CBA or ING enabled HiredScore. |
| Oracle Recruiting | Generative-AI ratings and summaries can use parsed education, experience and skills; recruiters can filter by ratings. | Does not prove Westpac or AMP enabled this feature. |
| SAP SuccessFactors | Configurable CV parsing, including Textkernel; 2H 2026 documentation describes AI-assisted skills matching. | Does not prove ANZ or Bendigo use AI matching. |
| LinkedIn Hiring Assistant | Uses hiring criteria, profiles, CVs, screening responses and recruiting notes to summarise qualification matches. | An additional licensed capability, not something every LinkedIn advertiser uses. |

**Confidence: High for capabilities; bank activation unconfirmed.** [Workday, 5 August 2026](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html); [Oracle, undated; accessed 20 November 2026](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/talent-management/farqa/evaluate-candidate-applications-using-ai-matching-ratings.html); [SAP parsing documentation, undated](https://help.sap.com/docs/successfactors-recruiting/setting-up-and-maintaining-sap-successfactors-recruiting/working-with-resume-parsing) and [SAP 2H 2026](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_SUCCESSFACTORS_RECRUITING/8477193265ea4172a1dda118505ca631/8134bbc02518434a8ef1c28f3fd2c7a1.html?locale=en-ES); [LinkedIn FAQ, accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a7109304).

**Formatting recommendation — Medium, based on direct parser guidance:** use one column, ordinary bullets, descriptive headings and a consistent date style such as “Jan 2022 – Present”. Put name, telephone, email and LinkedIn URL in the main body. Avoid skills bars, logos, text boxes and tables for career history. A page number may sit in a footer because losing it would not lose evidence.

This is risk reduction, not a claim that every two-column CV fails. Greenhouse specifically lists these layouts as possible causes of partial or unsuccessful parsing; it also says the file remains attached if parsing fails. [Greenhouse, 2 March 2026](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

**DOCX versus PDF:** SEEK calls DOCX often the safest option but also accepts text-based PDF and prioritises the employer's instructions. There is no comparative bank-specific test here proving one universally wins. Keep both. If both are accepted without guidance, I would upload a simple DOCX and check the extracted fields; use a text PDF when requested or when direct human circulation benefits from fixed pagination. **Medium — recommendation.** [SEEK, 29 October 2026](https://au.seek.com/career-advice/article/tips-for-creating-an-ats-friendly-resume).

**Keywords and semantic matching coexist.** LinkedIn offers title/skills filters, while its AI tools assess qualifications in context. Write “Product Owner” in full, followed by “PO” if useful; do the same for genuine specialist terms. Preserve official employment titles and add a truthful functional explanation where an internal title is obscure. **High for platform functions; Medium for writing recommendation.** [LinkedIn filters, undated; accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428?lang=en); [LinkedIn AI agents, accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a7437598/how-linkedin-uses-ai-agents-to-connect-job-seekers-and-hirers).

**Knockout questions deserve a separate check.** Platforms support screening questions, but this research cannot identify each bank's automatic rejection rules. Treat work rights, office attendance, location, required experience and mandatory credentials as substantive questions. Count actual PO experience, not total employment tenure. **High for question functionality; Medium for this checklist; exact knockout logic unconfirmed.** [LinkedIn screening questions, accessed 20 November 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a410766).
