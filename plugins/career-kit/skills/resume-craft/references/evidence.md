# Evidence: where each rule comes from

Two kinds of source, kept apart because they are different kinds of claim.

**R, the research.** Six reports on how Australian banks screen résumés in 2026, in
`../../../docs/deep-research/`, cited by letter: **C** Codex, **A** OpenAI, **P** Perplexity,
**O** Claude Opus, **G** Gemini. They describe one market in one month. A rule marked R is as
good as the reports' own sources, and where they marked a recommendation as their inference,
this file says so.

**C, the corrections.** Things one person and the family member relaying for her said about
drafts in a single engagement on 20 November 2026, recorded in career-kit's
`references/evidence.md`. A rule marked with a correction is a failure that happened once and
was fixed, not a finding about résumés in general. They are here because each was caught by a
person reading copy that had already passed every fact and consistency check.

Where the two disagree, the table says which the skill follows. The person's own stated rules
outrank both.

## Inferences of this skill's own

Nothing quoted supports these directly; they follow from the rules above.

- **Draft the experience first, the skills second, the summary last.** The summary and the
  skills are tested against the experience, so they are written once it exists.
- **Order of cuts** in `voice.md`. Derived from "recent roles count most" and "do not cut to a
  word count".
- **The seven things to check in another market** in `tailoring.md`. The research covers one
  market; the list is the set of defaults that are plainly local.

## Held loosely

- No report could see which ranking or AI features any employer has switched on, or any score
  threshold. Everything about ranking describes what a tool can do.
- No controlled test compares file formats or layouts on these employers' systems.
- Both counted ad samples are small (7 to 16 ads) and skewed to three banks.
- The corrections come from one person's résumé in one industry. A different person may want
  the implied subject, a count of years, or one résumé per role, and their rule wins.
