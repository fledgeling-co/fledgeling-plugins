# The question form — asking in a way that gets answered

The person is doing this around a job. A form they can finish on a phone in fifteen minutes
returns answers; a list of twenty open questions in a chat message returns three.

## A form, or questions in the conversation

Use the form when there are more than about five questions, or when the person will answer on
their own time. For five or fewer, ask in the conversation as multiple choice with a free-text
option, where the runtime offers a question tool: quote the line each answer will complete,
offer three or four options built only from what is already known, and include one that leaves
the line as it is. Save the answers verbatim as a file, the same as a form round.

## Shape

One HTML file, generated by `scripts/build_form.py` from a JSON spec. No network, no
dependencies, answers in `localStorage`, Copy and Download at the bottom, a running count of
how many questions the person has looked at.

```bash
python3 scripts/build_form.py --spec questions.json --out questions.html \
    --title "Jane Doe — résumé questions" --key jane-resume-v1
```

## Four things that make a question answerable

**A suggested default, marked and sourced.** Where their documents already imply an answer,
pre-select it, label it `suggested`, and say where it came from ("from your LinkedIn export").
The person is then confirming rather than composing, which is a different and much smaller
task.

**Tap-to-fill examples under every free-text field.** Three to five, each a complete sentence
they could send as written, built from what you already know about their work. A blank textarea
is the question people skip.

**A "why this is being asked" line.** One sentence, with the research citation. "Three of the
five ads ask for discovery from evidence" tells them what kind of answer helps.

**Multiple choice wherever the answer is genuinely a choice.** Especially for the privacy
questions, where the options are the real decision: internal only, softened for external,
remove everywhere.

## Writing an example that is safe to select

An example will come back as the answer, so write it as something that could be true of this
person given what you already know, never as a figure or an outcome you invented. "Access data
showed a role held permissions almost nobody used" is safe because it describes a shape of
work; "reduced access incidents by 40%" is not, because selecting it would put a fabricated
number into their résumé.

Where a question needs a number, ask for the number in a text field and give no example.

## Count what was looked at, not what holds a value

A form whose suggested defaults are pre-selected reads "15 of 15 answered" before anyone opens
it, which is what the source session's third form did. `build_form.py` counts a question once the
person has tapped something in it, and exports an untouched default as
`[suggested default, not touched]`.

Four strengths of answer come back, strongest first: typed, example edited, example selected
unchanged, default not touched. All four are answers. Before printing a figure or an outcome that
rests on the last, ask the person to confirm it.

Fast forms invite agreement. In that third form eleven of fifteen optional notes were one
unchanged example saying, in effect, "happy with your suggestion". Do not offer an example that
only agrees; it carries no information and it will be tapped.

## Checking you understood their feedback

A person's own notes on a draft are the best input an engagement gets, and the easiest to
misread. "Remove the specific product" had two readings; "uplifted the SLAs through negotiation"
could mean she led the negotiation or did the uplift inside one; "this was branch" corrected a
setting the drafts had carried for days. Check the reading before rewriting anything.

- **One question per note.** Quote the note in the question's "why" line, state your reading,
  and offer the plausible readings as options, with "Something else (say in the note)" and a
  notes box. Pre-select nothing: an untouched default would read as agreement.
- **Offer no tap-to-fill examples.** On a check page every example is either an invented fact or
  a line that only agrees.
- **Where a verb could mean either, split the work into parts.** "I supported the negotiations,
  and the uplift to the service levels was my work" and "I reviewed the cause and the change
  was made by others on my recommendation" were each chosen over a single verb.
- **Ask the noun of every number and whether two pieces of work are one.** People or seats; the
  same program seen from two roles, or two programs.
- **Where a note points at an old document**, such as "review my original résumé", offer its
  lines back word for word as a tick list. The person took three of four back.
- **Put the rules you drew from the notes back as a tick list.** A ticked rule becomes a
  standing decision for every section and document. An unticked one is your reading and is not
  recorded as theirs. In the source session the person ticked four of seven.
- **End with two questions:** which other lines do not represent their work, and whether they
  want wording suggestions, backed by research on how hiring managers read, for the lines they
  flag.

A note with no tick is an answer, and for wording questions it was the usual one: both summary
wording questions came back as "Something else" with the person's own sentence. Every one of 25
questions was answered.

The page saves as the person goes, and people download it before they finish. Treat the latest
download as superseding an earlier one, and compare the two before reading either.

## When the tick and the text disagree

One answer ticked "drop this claim" and wrote a note that supported the claim. Do not pick a
reading. Ask one follow-up that states both, and until it is answered take the more cautious of
the two in the document and list it as open.

## An unchanged example is an answer

The form marks a verbatim example as `[example text, unchanged]` in the copied output. That tag
exists so you can see which answers were selected, not so you can discount them. In the source
session fifteen answers came back as unchanged examples and were treated as unverified; the
person had selected them because they were accurate, and their relay said so. Ask before
treating a selection as a non-answer.

It is often the least they did, though. In the source session an unchanged example, "missed
service levels were picked up sooner", stood in the résumé for two rounds; the person's own
words, when she wrote some, were "from red to green across a six-month performance improvement
plan". In a later round, show example-text answers back and invite the person's own words,
results first.

## Placeholders and questions are the same list

Every red placeholder in a draft is one question, and every question says which line it will
complete. Ask what each bare fact was for as well as for what is missing outright: what an
award recognised, what happened after a change went ahead, what was different once a team was
reorganised. Those answers turn a statement that something happened into the reason it
mattered.

## After the answers come back

Save the raw text verbatim as a file in the project, unedited. That file is the citation for
every figure that enters a document afterwards, and `scripts/facts_lint.py` checks outputs
against it.

## Rounds

Two or three rounds is normal, so say so in the first form. The first collects facts. The second
collects the details the interview guide and the stories need, and what the first left open. A
third often comes from a panel reading the finished documents: in the source session it raised
thirteen things only the person could settle, such as whether three bullets described one piece
of work, and whether an outcome she had described as expected had since happened.

In a later round:

- Quote the person's earlier answer and ask them to confirm or correct it. Eight of fifteen
  questions in the third form did this, and it came back within the hour.
- Ask nothing the answers files or the notes already settle. Check before sending.
- Say on each question which line of which document depends on it.
- Save each round's answers as its own file, and add that file to the project's list of fact
  sources.
- Build the form only from a settled panel result. The third form's question count went from nine
  to thirteen to fifteen while a late lane's answer was being folded in.

After incorporating a round, run a panel on the incorporation (`panels.md`).
