# improve-skill — icon notes

**Direction: "The Honed Edge".** The studio's *Tahoe Gel-Glass* sub-register (a), porcelain cushion tile carrying a coloured gel object, crossed with device bank **#16** (the icon performs the verb) and **#5** (dual-function primitive). The subject is a pipeline that takes a skill which already exists and makes it measurably better, so the tile is not a background: it is the workpiece. A worn plane iron lies mid-pass on a rising diagonal, and the surface behind it is visibly brighter and truer than the surface still to come.

Runner-up, and the near-neighbour worth naming: **Direction 7, Diagonal Tool**, which the catalogue calls the single most template-worn move in the corpus. This icon enters that territory deliberately rather than by reflex, and inverts it. In Direction 7 a tool crosses an inert field and the tool is the identity; here the tool has *changed* the field, and the identity is the change. Also considered and dropped: Direction 8 *Instrument Emblem*, because improve-skill has no gauge or chart to quote; its in-product artifact is an eval table, and a table is failure mode #4.

**Glyph (subject-mined, device bank #16 + #5 + #20):** one plane iron, 640 × 168 at 33°, occupying 61.4% of the tile. Crisp corners on the honed edge because it is sharpened, worn rounding and a shallow sag on the back because it is used. Underneath it, one family of grain lines runs along the travel direction and **crosses the boundary** — torn and broken above it, continuous and fine below it. That continuity is what makes the split read as one surface in two states rather than as two different materials, which is the whole argument: same skill, better.

**Signature:** the before-and-after boundary *is* the diagonal, and the one vermilion hone line *is* that boundary. A single shape does three jobs: the tool's cutting edge, the division between the two states, and the icon's only accent. **Risk taken:** walking into the corpus's most worn composition on purpose, on the bet that inverting it is stronger than avoiding it.

**Palette:** two families only. Warm-neutral porcelain (`#DCD8CD→#B2AB9B` un-planed, `#FFFFFE→#F7F2E7` trued) and achromatic graphite (`#4B5159→#101215`), with one vermilion reserved strictly for the honed edge (`#C4341A`/`#F2542D`/`#FF7C46`). One soft top-left light plus the sanctioned second source, an emissive interior; here the hone itself, spilling onto the surface it has just cut.

**Audit — the shipping take is C2 at 7/12; the highest-scoring take is A at 11/12.** Those are different takes, and the gap is the user's deliberate call: C2 won the material comparison by a distance, and material was judged to matter more here than sheet score. The scores stand as audited rather than being revised to fit the decision. Full scores, verdicts and liabilities: `audit.html`.

**Take A (hand-authored layered SVG, `icon.svg`, generated by `build_icon.py`)** scored 11/12 with zero failures on the non-negotiable 1–4. Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the squircle as a *clip*, no baked corners or shadow. Checks measured rather than eyeballed: focal 61.4% of tile, optical centre (506, 490), safe-zone margins L192 R204 T245 B290, figure-ground 4.82:1 against the un-planed side and 7.41:1 against the trued side, the two fields 1.54:1 apart. Its deducted point is #10: under an aggressive monochrome tint that collapses mid-tones, the blade and the un-planed field can flatten to one value. It stays on disk as the editable route back.

**What shipping C2 costs, stated plainly.** It is a flat pre-masked raster, so **#10 hard-fails**: identity is a colour relationship with nothing layered behind it, and it will not survive Dark, Clear or Tinted rendering. That is the corpus's single most common failure and this icon now has it. Worse for the concept specifically: sampled on the masked 1024, C2's un-planed side measures L 0.426 and its trued side L 0.363, so the side that is meant to be brighter and truer is in fact slightly *darker*, because a warm bloom sits on the rough side and a vignette sits on the trued one. The before-and-after therefore reads as a change of texture rather than as an improvement. The banner compensates by carrying a wider split in its own ground, and by running the tile's own cut on out across the surface. A hard specular top-left is legacy-era drag (#5, #9), the focal sits at roughly half the tile and low (#2), and the file cannot be edited: any future change is a new generation and a new audit, not a diff.

**Three engines, all four takes on the sheet.** Engine A took 11/12. Engine C raster took 9/12 (C1) and 7/12 (C2). Engine B (Arrow vector) came last at 5/12 with hard fails on silhouette and 16px: the blade forked into a wishbone and the vermilion hone line, the entire signature, was absent. Its steeper, flatter diagonal was the one thing worth salvaging, and the shipped C2's 40° cut sits in that same steeper family.

**Cut at round 3 of the vector master:** a curled shaving peeling off the back edge, the physical evidence of what came away. It was authored twice and read as a coat hanger both times, so the remove-one-accessory pass took it, leaving two objects: surface and blade. The shipped C2 render keeps its shaving, and in a photographic material it does work, which is part of why it won the look.

**Register note:** five of the six sibling plugins share this porcelain ground, which makes it the marketplace's house register rather than a borrowed one (trawl owns the dark deep-sea register). Differentiation is carried entirely by the split, which no sibling uses, and by the fact that this is the only icon here whose tile is the thing being acted on rather than the stage the subject stands on.
