# improve-skill — icon notes

**Direction: "The Honed Edge".** The studio's *Tahoe Gel-Glass* sub-register (a), porcelain cushion tile carrying a coloured gel object, crossed with device bank **#16** (the icon performs the verb) and **#5** (dual-function primitive). The subject is a pipeline that takes a skill which already exists and makes it measurably better, so the tile is not a background: it is the workpiece. A worn plane iron lies mid-pass on a rising diagonal, and the surface behind it is visibly brighter and truer than the surface still to come.

Runner-up, and the near-neighbour worth naming: **Direction 7, Diagonal Tool**, which the catalogue calls the single most template-worn move in the corpus. This icon enters that territory deliberately rather than by reflex, and inverts it. In Direction 7 a tool crosses an inert field and the tool is the identity; here the tool has *changed* the field, and the identity is the change. Also considered and dropped: Direction 8 *Instrument Emblem*, because improve-skill has no gauge or chart to quote; its in-product artifact is an eval table, and a table is failure mode #4.

**Glyph (subject-mined, device bank #16 + #5 + #20):** one plane iron and the shaving it took off. The iron is authored as a real extruded solid — a 640 × 204 top face at 33° lifted clear of the ground, with a front face dropping back down to it — occupying 61.5% of the tile. Since round 6 that lift is *pitched*: 48px at the leading end where the iron is buried in the timber, 132px at the trailing end, so the front face is a wedge and the block rides nose-down at a working angle rather than sitting level. Crisp corners on the honed edge because it is sharpened, worn rounding and a shallow sag on the back because it is used. Underneath it, one family of grain lines runs along the travel direction and **crosses the boundary** — torn and broken above it, continuous and fine below it. That continuity is what makes the split read as one surface in two states rather than as two different materials, which is the whole argument: same skill, better. Rising off the iron's top rear edge is the shaving — a ribbon swept along the blade's own axis through an open hook of 0.78 of a turn, 285 × 282 against the blade's 630 × 634, so the blade stays the hero.

**Signature:** the before-and-after boundary *is* the diagonal, and the one vermilion hone line *is* that boundary. Since the round-4 rebuild a single shape does four jobs: the tool's cutting edge, the division between the two states, the icon's only accent, and the line where the solid meets the ground. **Risk taken:** walking into the corpus's most worn composition on purpose, on the bet that inverting it is stronger than avoiding it.

**Palette:** two families only. Warm-neutral porcelain (`#D8D2C4→#ABA391` un-planed, `#FFFDF6→#F1EAD9` trued) and achromatic graphite (`#5D636B→#181B20`), with one vermilion reserved strictly for the honed edge (`#C4341A`/`#FA6231`/`#FF9159`). One soft top-left light plus the sanctioned second source, an emissive interior; here the hone itself, spilling onto the surface it has just cut and lighting the underside of the iron's front face.

**Audit — A ships at 11/12.** `icon.png`, `icon-256.png` and `icon-128.png` all render from `icon.svg`. The one deduction is at #4, and it is the price of the shaving; see below. Full scores, verdicts and evidence: `audit.html`.

## Round 4 — the material rebuild, and what it fixed

For three rounds the sheet carried a split: the highest-scoring take was the vector master A, and the take the user chose on look was the raster C2. The split is now closed, and it closed by **rebuilding A rather than re-scoring anything**. The brief was to pull the vector toward the best of both rasters — C2's whetstone mass, both rasters' warm textured ground, a genuinely emissive hone, a deep soft contact shadow — while fixing the flaw the rasters shipped with.

**What materially changed.** The flat bar became a solid. It is now an extruded body: the top face is lifted 88px clear of the ground and a front face drops back to it, with a chamfer highlight between the two faces and a rim light along the worn back. The geometry is derived, not drawn twice — the outline is flattened to a polyline in the blade's local frame, the lower silhouette chain is swept straight down, and the ground contact of that sweep *is* local y=0, so the solid physically cannot drift out of register with the boundary or the hone. Both ground planes gained warm mottle pushed through a heavy blur over the boundary-crossing grain. The hone became real light: a heavily blurred spill on the trued plane, a warm bounce running up the front face, and a hot core line. The shadow became a deep soft cast plus a tight contact occlusion.

**What got fixed.** Polarity — the flaw C2 shipped with. The trued side now measures **L 0.920 against the un-planed side's L 0.746, +0.174 the right way round**, where C2 measured 0.653 against 0.696, **−0.043 inverted**. The side that is meant to be better now reads better. Measured by `measure.py` on the real render, every iteration, not eyeballed.

**The #10 point, earned back on evidence.** Every previous round deducted a point at variant robustness: under an aggressive monochrome tint that collapses mid-tones, the blade and the un-planed field flattened to one value and the cutting edge was lost. Rendered side by side with the old master under both a mono and a dark tint, the old one still collapses and the rebuilt solid holds. The reason is structural: identity is now a **form** relationship — a lit solid casting a shadow onto a two-state ground — rather than a colour relationship. The raw field ratio under that tint is a modest 1.69:1; what carries the read is the chamfer, the rim light and the cast shadow, none of which a tint can remove.

**Measured on the shipped renders:** focal 61.5% of tile, optical centre (494, 457), safe-zone margins L179 R216 T140 B250 — re-measured after the round-8 depth change (they read 58.7%, (508, 479), L207 T184 through rounds 6 and 7). Figure-ground was 4.67:1 against the un-planed side and 7.56:1 against the trued side with the fields 1.62:1 apart when this was first taken at round 4, and the pitch leaves all three unchanged within measurement noise (see round 6 below). Four real layers named `#bg` / `#mid` / `#fg` / `#highlight`, mapping 1:1 onto Icon Composer. Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the squircle as a *clip*, no baked corners or shadow. Edit `build_icon.py`, never `icon.svg`.

**The shaving curl, cut three times, and what made the fourth work.** Three rounds failed because a spiral **outline** is the wrong model. An outline is a closed curve with a hole in it — a shell — and no amount of redrawing it makes it material. What ships is a swept **surface**: one cross-section curve (a nearly straight tail leaving the blade, easing tangentially into a loose open hook) swept along the blade's own axis by the ribbon's width, then cut into 96 bands, each shaded by its real facing angle to the single top-left light. Bands whose outer face turns toward the viewer are lit; the ones on the far side are seen from the *inside*, through the open end of the roll, so they take the shadow family plus a transmitted lift where the outer face is in light. The free end tapers in opacity, because that is the thinnest, most-curled material and the ground has to show through it.

Two things were measured off C2 rather than invented. **Its cross-section is a circle seen obliquely**, so the section here is compressed to 0.54 along the roll's axis; drawn as a true circle the mouth traces a full ellipse and the whole thing reads as a capped tin can. And **its curl is not a pale shape on a dark ground** — its lit top measures L 0.576 against ground of L 0.635 right beside it, falling to L 0.27 at the bottom, so it reads by form-shading and hairline cut edges, never by a value jump. That is the trap all three failed attempts fell into. This one measures **1.25:1 against the un-planed ground it was shaved from, against an internal modelling range of 3.66:1** — nearly all of its legibility is its own shading. Staying under a full turn matters twice: past one turn the swept ribbon closes into a tube and reads as a roll of tape, and an open hook is what *partially unrolled* actually looks like.

**What it costs, honestly: one point, at #4.** Rendered at 16px beside the no-curl master, the blade's dark diagonal, the vermilion hone and the two-state split are all completely intact — identity is not damaged, which is why this is a deduction and not a hard fail — but the curl contributes a small low-information tan patch above-left of the blade where the previous master had clean empty ground. It simplifies to a soft pale note, as intended, and it is still noise. It repays that at #11: the tile now carries the physical evidence that the plane cut. Polarity is unharmed and marginally better at **+0.184** (trued L 0.920, un-planed L 0.737), up from +0.174, and the blade and both fields are geometrically untouched so figure-ground is unchanged. `SHAVING=0` in `build_icon.py` rebuilds the round-4 two-object tile.

## Round 6 — the pitch: why it still didn't look like planing

The whole brief was one sentence: *"It doesn't look like wood is being planed because the angle of the block is flat unlike C2."* That was right, and the diagnosis was cheap once stated. Round 4 lifted the top face by **one constant rise**, so the top face was a parallel copy of the footprint and the front face was a band of even height. The iron had mass and no attitude: a bar lying on the boards, not a tool taking a shaving off them.

**What was measured off C2 rather than invented.** Its ground/hone line runs **38.9°**, its top-face shoulder runs **41.9°**, and its front face grows from about **55px deep at the near end to 90px at the far end**. The front face is a *wedge*, not a band, and that wedge is the entire tell — it says the block is riding nose-down, buried in the timber at the leading end and lifted behind. (Two independent detectors on C2 bracket the taper at 1.6:1 to 2.6:1 and the shoulder-vs-ground offset at +3.0° to +5.8°; the shipped value sits inside both.)

**What changed, and only this.** The lift is now **linear in local x**: `RISE_NEAR = 48` at the leading end, `RISE_FAR = 132` at the trailing end, **2.75:1**. Because a linear lift stays affine, the lifted top face is still **one matrix** — `MATRIX_TOP`, a screen-vertical shear of the blade's own frame — so the stone texture, the top-face gradient, the grind striations and the rim light all ride it with no second transform and no chance of drifting off the metal. The chains and faces are now derived by *index* so each top-face point drops to its own footprint point rather than to a constant offset. Top-face edges come out at **38.9° against the hone's 33°, +5.9° of pitch**, matching C2's shoulder-versus-ground offset. The front-face gradient is re-anchored by *absolute distance from the cutting edge* rather than by fraction of the rise, so the hone's falloff is identical whether the wedge is 48px deep or 132px there.

**What deliberately did not move.** The footprint, the cutting edge and the before/after boundary all live at local y=0 and the shear leaves local y=0 fixed, so the signature line cannot drift — and the banner's derived split needed no re-derivation. `ANGLE` stays 33° and `EDGE_MID` stays (543, 604) for the same reason: changing them is a different edit class and would kink the banner. The curl construction is untouched; its base is now held in the blade's *local* frame (`CURL_BASE_L = (289, 130)`) and mapped through `to_top`, so the tail's exit point rides the pitch instead of floating off the metal. Verified on a 4× crop of the junction — the tail still plunges behind the worn back edge exactly as it did before.

## Round 7 — the material round: the scene has no cool light in it

Instrumented round against C2 on the create-mac-icon fidelity harness (`loop-runs/r00-baseline/` -> `loop-runs/r01/`), with the fixture's declared envelope `--max-paths 3000 --max-bytes 350000` — the swept ribbon's 96 bands are construction, not path soup. The brief was the shape of the baseline numbers: 16px and 32px were already close to C2 (0.76 / 0.69) and 1024/256/128 were not (0.36 / 0.35 / 0.36). Composition had converged; material had not.

**What was measured off C2, not assumed.** Five readings, each taken in C2's *own* hone frame (its line fits 38.93 deg from (392,681) to (670,456)) so the two icons could be compared at matching heights above the cutting edge rather than at matching canvas pixels.

1. **The dark end's hue is inverted.** C2's darkest 1% of block reads rgb **(0.155,0.132,0.119) — R>G>B, warm**; the master's read **(0.096,0.108,0.128) — B>G>R, cool**. Saturation *magnitude* was almost identical (0.236 vs 0.246), so a saturation check passes and a range check passes; only sampling the dark end's actual channel order catches it. This is the create-mac-icon "check the dark end's hue" finding, arriving with the sign flipped: not desaturated, mis-hued. Every stop of `topFace` ran #2E3238 -> #5D636B with B ten points above R, and the rendered top face measured a cool cast (sat 0.13-0.15, B>R) at *every* point of *every* cross-section. The comment above that gradient had said "warm-leaning graphite, not blue steel" since round 4; the constants never said it.
2. **The hone was a tube, not a cut.** Sampled along the line itself, C2 holds L 0.95-0.98 for the leading 30% of the blade and then falls off a cliff — 0.91 / 0.74 / 0.53 / 0.30 — extinguished into the ground by about 62% along. The master measured a **dead-flat L 0.89 for all 640px**. C2 is physically right here for the icon's own reason: the glow is the cut happening, and the cut is where the iron is buried in the timber at the leading end.
3. **The front face had no along-length shading.** At 15px above the cutting edge the master read a flat L 0.281 end to end; C2 runs 0.274 -> 0.138 over the same span, i.e. it halves. Same physical fact as (2), applied to the surface the hone lights.
4. **The chamfer was a pinstripe where C2 has a trough.** Across three cross-sections, C2's top/front junction is the **darkest** part of the whole section over the leading two thirds (0.245 -> 0.209 -> 0.201 going up through it) and only becomes a rolled highlight near the trailing end, at +18% and warm. The master carried a 4.4px #848E9C stroke at 0.56 — cool, and +72% over the face beside it.
5. **The ground's brightest point was in the wrong corner.** Measured corners: C2 TL 0.869 / TR 0.509 / BL 0.556 / BR 0.562 — one key, up and left, every corner away from it at 0.6-0.65x. The master: TL 0.780 / TR 0.678 / **BL 0.932** / BR 0.848. Its brightest ground sat in the corner *furthest from its own light*. That is a single-light violation in the master, not merely a mismatch with C2 — which is why fixing it is a rubric gain and a score gain at once.

**What changed, all of it material.** One class, `build_icon.py` only. `topFace` re-hued to warm-neutral graphite with **the luminance of every stop held to within 0.01**, so the cast moves and the modelling does not. `frontFace` re-hued warm and its floor lifted off black (L 0.106-0.128 -> 0.15-0.23) — the master's ground is *brighter* than C2's, so its bounce should be stronger, not weaker. A new `honeFall` mask carries C2's measured along-length falloff onto every layer the hone owns at once — wide bloom, glow, core, specular — so they cannot drift apart, plus `frontFall` on the face it lights and `seatRamp` for the seat's own occlusion (C2's ground reads 0.66x its far field under the lit leading end and 0.41x under the trailing end; the master was a flat 0.59-0.60x). The pinstripe became a soft warm occlusion trough plus a roll that ramps in toward the trailing end. Rim light, grind striations and the stone mottle all re-hued off blue. Fields and vignette re-aimed onto the key.

**Deliberately not followed: C2's register.** Its two ground fields sit at 0.65 and 0.65 — C2 has essentially no polarity, which is why it fails #11. Roughly two thirds of the remaining `lum_delta` is the master's trued field standing 0.26 L above C2's, and closing that would delete the icon's signature. Only the part of it with a *physical* reason — the falloff away from the light — was taken. This is the Ledgerline rule: fade where no boundary lives, never across one.

**Reusable recipe — "single-emitter falloff mask".** The hone change is the transferable one, and it generalises past this icon. Where an object is lit by an *emitter it carries* (a glowing edge, a screen bezel, a filament, a heated tip), author the emitter's decay **once** as a `linearGradient` of white with varying `stop-opacity` along the emitter's own axis, wrap it in a `<mask>` over the full canvas, and apply that one mask to the group holding *every* layer the emitter owns — bloom, glow, core, specular, and the spill it throws on the ground. Cost is one gradient and one mask; the payoff is that the emitter's falloff, the shading of the surfaces it lights, and the ground's brightness beneath it can never drift out of agreement, because they are one number. Give it a floor rather than letting it reach zero whenever the emitter is also carrying identity at small sizes.

## Round 8 — the coarse-structure round: the curl was the wrong solid

`loop-runs/r04/`, measured against `loop-runs/r01/`. Edit class: coarse structure — silhouette, centring, object scale, attitude, major colour fields. The human's review named the curled flake twice as the priority and noted it had not changed across rounds; that is squarely this class, so it was the round's whole job. The same review's other two items — the left side's lighting and texture, and re-applying the r02 fibre — are fine material work and were deliberately left for the round that owns them.

**Where the gap actually was.** The candidate's curl silhouette was isolated exactly by differencing a `SHAVING=0` render against a `SHAVING=1` one: bbox x[183,467] y[232,483], 284×251, centroid (313,361). Tracing C2's curl by gradient failed outright — the flake is only about 1.25:1 against the ground it sits on, so the board's texture swamped every edge filter. What worked instead was **fitting circles through three points read off each rim arc** and drawing the solved circles back onto the reference to check them (`loop-runs/r04/fitcheck.png`, near-exact):

    near rim   centre (294, 253)   R 115     residual < 2px over a 240px arc
    far  rim   centre (359, 186)   R 121     offset d = (+65, -67), |d| = 93.3, bearing 45.9 deg

Three facts fall out of that fit, and the master disagreed with all three.

1. **Both rims are circles.** Under orthographic projection a 3D circle projects to a circle only when its plane is parallel to the image plane, so C2's roll axis points essentially at the viewer and there is **no in-plane foreshortening of the section left to draw**. `CURL_FORE` had been compressing it by 0.54; it is 1.00.
2. **The roll is a hoop, not a pipe.** Centre-to-centre 93px against R 115 is 0.81:1 — diameter to length 2.5:1. The master was built at 106/78 = 1.36:1 and read as a length of pipe lying on the boards. `CURL_R` 78 -> 115, `CURL_SWEEP` 106 -> 93.
3. **The roll has tipped off the edge it came from.** The rim offset subtends 45.9 deg, not the blade's 33. A new `CURL_TILT = 12.9 deg` rotates the sweep axis alone (`TUX,TUY`) and leaves the blade frame untouched.

Fourth, read off the crop rather than fitted: C2's ribbon leaves the blade as a **~40px stub off the roll's lower flank**. The master's `CURL_PHI0 = -24 deg` entered on the upper-right flank and needed a ~150px straight chute that C2 does not have; it is now +34 deg, on the flank that faces the blade.

**Positioned relative to the blade, not copied as canvas pixels.** The master's blade is 1.38× C2's in screen terms (689.5px back edge against 500px) and sits lower-right, so C2's canvas coordinates are meaningless here. C2's roll clears its own blade's back edge by 53px; `CURL_C` was sited at (308,278) so this one clears **this** blade's back edge by 50. `CURL_BASE_L` moved to local y 182 to hold the same 22-unit inset inside the back edge after `BLADE_THICK` went to 204.

**One correction worth recording.** A first attempt also flipped the sweep sign, on the belief that the roll receded the wrong way; the render came out as an opaque disc. Deriving the ruled-strip normal properly — N = T x S, so N_z = -((radial) · (projected sweep)) — showed the original code was already self-consistent provided the array named `far` is in fact the *nearer* rim. The sweep sign was restored and only the measured tilt kept. The visibility test was rewritten from `(n · U) > 0` to `(n · SU) < 0` so it tracks the tilted sweep axis; the two are identical at zero tilt.

**Not this round's edit, but in this round's class:** `BLADE_THICK` 152 -> 204 arrived externally mid-round, justified in its own comment as the mean of 204/190/218 measured on C2's top/front shoulder over the leading two thirds. It was kept and built on, and it moves the solid from 58.7% to 61.5% of the tile.

**Reusable construction — "fit the rims, then read the solid off the fit".** For any rolled, coiled or tubular form being matched to a raster reference where edge detection fails on low contrast: do not trace the silhouette. Read three points off each visible rim arc, solve the circle through them, and draw the solved circles back over the reference to verify. The two circles then hand you the whole solid for free — **equal radii and circular projection means the axis faces the viewer, so foreshortening is 1.0; centre separation over radius is the length-to-diameter ratio; and the bearing of the centre offset is the axis attitude, which is generally *not* the parent object's axis.** Three numbers, one verifiable drawing, and no assumption about what the material "probably" looks like.

## Round 8, second track — coarse structure: the iron was a bar, not a block

Two coarse-structure tracks ran on this fixture at the same time, which is why there are two round-8 entries. The section above is the curl fit; this one is the blade's depth. Each saw the other's constant arrive "externally" mid-round. The shipped `icon.svg` (md5 `d675ed0d776e`) is **both** edits together, and the gate below was run on that combined file; this entry also isolates what the depth change does on its own, built from `loop-runs/r04/_before/` in a scratch directory (`loop-runs/r04/attrib.py`), because a shared score.json cannot otherwise be attributed. Same harness, same declared envelope `--max-paths 3000 --max-bytes 350000`.

**What was measured off C2, not assumed.** Round 7 read C2 in its own hone frame; this round did the same and asked one question — how deep is its top face? The tempting subtraction is wrong. C2's silhouette back edge sits at a stable ly 248–260, but that figure carries the front face's lift with it, and the master's wedge (48→132) is deeper than C2's (55→90), so no single depth matches the apparent back edge along the whole length. The number to trust is **(back edge) − (top/front shoulder)** on the same cross-section, because the rise cancels out of the subtraction. Taken on three sections over the leading two thirds — the span where round 7 established the shoulder is a readable trough rather than the rolled highlight it becomes at the trailing end — it reads **204 / 190 / 218, mean 204**. The master's top face was **152**: 4.2:1 in plan against C2's 3.1:1. The iron was a slim bar wearing a wedge, and no amount of material work fixes a proportion.

**What changed, and only this.** `BLADE_THICK = 152 → 204`. Everything that consumes it already derived from it — the outline, the stone texture's local-y range, the `topFace` gradient's `y2`, the grind striations — so one constant moved and the solid, its texture and its shading all followed. One latent registration hazard fell out and was closed in the same edit: `CURL_BASE_L` held a hard-coded local y of 130, which was 22 units inside a 152-deep face and would have sat in the *middle* of a 204-deep one, so the shaving would have emerged from the top of the iron instead of over its worn back. It is `(289, BLADE_THICK - 22)` — derived, so a future change of face depth cannot break it. Checked on a 3× crop of the junction, not assumed. (The other track re-wrote it as the literal `182`, the same value; the derived form was restored and the render is byte-identical, so nothing about the artwork turns on this — only the next depth change does.)

**What deliberately did not move, and why the obvious edit was refused.** `ANGLE` stays 33°, `EDGE_MID` stays (543, 604), `BLADE_LEN` stays 640, `RISE_NEAR/FAR` stay 48/132. C2's hone line fits **38.92°** against the master's 33°, and a raster registration probe — warping the candidate onto the reference — said rotating to 38.9° was the single largest available gain (1024 ssim 0.6506→0.6667, edge_f1 0.048→0.111). **Built for real, `ANGLE = 38.9` scored −0.0299 net: a rejection.** The probe lied because the warp rotates the squircle, the vignette and the ground fields with the blade and leaves gaps behind; it does not model what changing `ANGLE` actually does. Every candidate after that was a real build scored by the harness. Shortening to C2's ~562 also hurt, and cost focal size (58.7% → 52.4%). Both were also banner-breaking: the split at `banner-src.html` is derived from A's 33° boundary and would need re-deriving.

**204 rather than the peak.** The composite keeps climbing past the measurement — 212 scores +0.1557 against 204's +0.1298 on the same v1 sweep — and 204 ships anyway, because 204 is what C2 measures and 212 is outside the bracket the three sections support. Tuning the constant against the composite without a physical reason is how this loop breaks.

**Scored, not asserted.** The harness moved to `METRIC_VERSION = 2` mid-round (edge_f1 now excludes the squircle rim) and refused a mixed comparison, correctly; the r01 baseline was re-scored under v2 and every number below is v2. The round-7 table above is v1 and is not comparable to it.

The gate and the rubric agree this round — no disagreement to report — and the 16px squint row in `loop-runs/r04/cmp-r04.png` shows the dark diagonal carrying more mass than before, closer to C2's. One honest mismatch remains and is now *more* visible, not less: the master's blade is longer than C2's, so its bbox (630 × 634) overshoots C2's (567 × 536) even as its filled area moves toward it (9.2% → 10.9% of tile against C2's 13.6%). Length is a separate edit class and scored worse when tried.

**Reusable recipe — "measure depth where the rise cancels".** For any icon whose subject is an extruded solid seen in three-quarter view and lifted or pitched off its ground, do not take the object's depth from its silhouette: the far silhouette edge carries the lift, so it changes with attitude and gives a different answer at every station along the object. Take it instead as **(far silhouette edge) − (near face's shoulder)** on a single cross-section perpendicular to the object's own axis, sampled in the object's own frame rather than in canvas pixels, and average over the span where the shoulder is a *trough* rather than a highlight. The subtraction is attitude-independent, which is exactly what makes it comparable between two renders that do not share a pitch. Cost is one perpendicular-ray sampler (`loop-runs/r04/an9.py`, ~40 lines of numpy); the payoff is a depth constant you can defend against the score rather than fit to it.

## Round 9 — the material round: the top face was a lighter grey than the stone it was made of

**What was measured off C2.** Region means were the wrong instrument here — the master's blade is ~1.38× C2's and sits elsewhere, so any mask built from our geometry mixes material error with structural non-overlap. The round was decided on a **cross-section profile instead**: perpendicular to *each block's own hone*, at five stations along the length, fifteen depths from below the cutting edge up to the back silhouette (`loop-runs/r06/work/an10.py`). Read that way the answer is unambiguous and it is not where the residual map points. Our **front face matches C2 to within 0.05 over its entire body** (depths 20–56px, −0.05 to +0.03 at every station). Our **top face runs +0.05 to +0.14 too light at every station** (depths 72–190px). Face means: ours 0.338, C2's 0.243. Its across-depth ramp was overdone too — ours climbed +0.101 from hone edge to back edge where C2's climbs +0.068 — and the top/front junction stepped **+0.176** on ours against C2's **−0.018**, i.e. C2 has no step there at all over the leading two thirds. The darkest 3% of our top face was rgb (0.210, 0.209, 0.187), **sat 0.110**; C2's is (0.173, 0.145, 0.131), **sat 0.240**. The face was desaturating into shadow — the exact fault round 1 caught on the ground, still living on this one face.

**Getting the reference's hone line right, and the near-miss that followed.** The split line was first fitted on "warm and bright" pixels and caught the timber, giving 41.7° — wrong diagonal, and every face split taken from it was wrong. Refitted on **chroma** (`R − (G+B)/2`, top 0.5%, one outlier-trim pass): **y = −0.8026x + 991.2, 38.75°**, residual sd 7.2px, and validated by running the identical method on our own render, which returned **33.05°** against the build's 33.00°. On the corrected line, region means then claimed C2's front face (0.340) was *lighter* than its top face (0.243) — an inverted per-face separation, and nearly the edit this round was built on. It is an artifact: the extended straight hone cuts through the trailing-right part of C2's block, so "below the line" collects top-face and edge pixels. Re-measured by perpendicular distance above each block's own hone *inside the block mask*, C2's front face runs 0.28 → 0.16 and the separation is the ordinary one. **Measure inside the mask, not against an extended line.**

**What changed, and why the ground did not.** One axis: the top face's own material. The four `topFace` stops are the old ramp under **L′ = 0.245 + 0.70·(L − 0.338)** — the affine that lands the rendered face on 0.215 → 0.283 across depth, which are C2's own two numbers — with the offset-0 stop set to C2's *measured* darkest colour rather than a desaturated grey. Every additive layer on that face was scaled with it, because at a fixed opacity each reads as a **bigger** step once the base drops 0.09: `topSheen` 0.13 → 0.06, the two grind striations 0.16/0.09 → 0.08/0.045, the back rim light 0.52 → 0.30. The rim's new opacity is not tuned: C2's top face peaks at 0.389 in its brightest 3% and 0.30 is the value that puts our rim on 0.389.

The ground was deliberately left alone, and that is the round's real decision. The **contrast budget** made this a live conflict: the master's 32/16 `self_contrast` sat at 0.553 against a 0.520 floor, and r05 was rejected outright for spending it. Tracing where the percentiles come from settled it — at 16px **p90 is 100% trued ground** and **p10 is 54% top face / 46% front face**. Darkening the ground therefore pays for its own convergence out of p90, which is how r05 died; darkening the **block** lowers p10 and *buys* contrast. Two ground hypotheses were also measured and rejected before they cost a round: C2's contact profile around its own block runs 0.727× far-field at 0–15px and ours already runs 0.738× at 0–20px, so **no contact-shadow or AO work was warranted**; and C2's trued ground (0.610) is *darker* than its rough (0.642), so converging its ground polarity would invert the icon's signature and break rubric #11.

**Budgeted before a constant moved.** `loop-runs/r06/work/sim.py` applies the proposed transform to the rendered 1024 PNG and recomputes `fidelity.py`'s own metrics, with the baseline printed both simulated and real as calibration (agreement within 0.006 at every size). Target mean 0.245 / squash 0.70 predicted **+0.0515 net** with `self_contrast` *rising* to 0.64/0.63; the built file scores **+0.0547**. The measured value and the best-scoring value coincide — the composite is flat from 0.245 to 0.225 (+0.0515 vs +0.0514) — so the constant is C2's number and the plateau is a check on it, not its source.

**What it cost.** Nothing measurable, which is unusual and worth stating plainly. Polarity holds at **+0.143** (trued 0.878, un-planed 0.736) by `measure.py icon.png 33.0 543 604 640` — the fixture's invariant is intact, trued side brighter, and it is untouched at three decimal places because no ground constant moved. Figure-ground **improves**: the block's L drops 0.307 → 0.241, so at 128px it reads **3.48:1** against the trued field (was 2.73:1) and **2.86:1** against the un-planed (was 2.25:1). Gate and rubric agree; no disagreement to report. Rendered check: the face now reads as dark stone taking a grazing light rather than moulded plastic under a spotlight (`loop-runs/r06/work/junction-2up.png`, `after-3up.png`). One target is short and is left short: the along-length swing is **1.32:1** against C2's **1.17:1**. Taking the sheen further would close it, but the sheen *is* the single-emitter read, and the remaining swing is honest falloff from a key that is genuinely off the trailing end.

**Reusable recipe — "find out which pixels own your percentiles".** When a gate constrains a global statistic — a self-contrast floor, a mean, a histogram spread — and the round's candidate edits all move luminance, spend ten lines finding out *which regions supply the percentiles the gate reads*, before choosing what to edit. Mask each region, take the pixels at or beyond each percentile, and report the region's share of them: `sel = (g >= np.percentile(g, 90)); {name: (m & sel).sum() / sel.sum()}`. The result partitions your candidate edits into ones that spend the budget and ones that pay into it, and the partition is frequently the opposite of intuition — here the bright ground owned p90 outright while the subject owned p10, so darkening the *subject* raised the very statistic that darkening the *ground* had destroyed a round earlier. Cost is one masked percentile pass; the payoff is not discovering the conflict after the render.

**One thing worth recording about the run itself.** Between this round's score and its final re-score, `build_icon.py` acquired ~120 lines it was not given — an `feDiffuseLighting` surface-relief system (`fibreRelief`, `pitRelief`, an `AMP_LAW` amplitude table and a derived `fibreRamp` mask), authored by something other than this round and on top of its output: its own `PIT_SCALE` comment reads "the face already carries 6.38% once ROUND 8 darkened it", i.e. it was written against this round's darkened face. It was caught because the structure counts moved — 12 gradients / 11 filters / 310,230 bytes became 13 / 13 / 314,933 — and the net fell from +0.0547 to **+0.0269** with it. The shipped file is this round's four edits on the `_before` snapshot and nothing else; the foreign version is kept intact at `loop-runs/r06/work/build_icon.foreign-relief.py` rather than discarded, since the relief idea is a reasonable one and the measurement it cites is real. It is simply not this round's class and was not this round's to score. **Verify the structure counts, not just the composite** — a rebuild that quietly gains filters is invisible in the gate verdict, which said ACCEPT both times.
