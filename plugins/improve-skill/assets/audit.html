The previous master scored 11/12 and still read flat — a slab with a line under it. It is now a real extruded solid: a top face lifted 88px clear of the ground and a front face dropping back to it, so the iron has the mass the raster take won on. The ground contact of that front face is local y=0, which is the before/after boundary, which is the vermilion hone, so the signature shape now does four jobs instead of three. Both ground planes carry blurred warm mottle plus the grain family that crosses the boundary; the hone is emissive for real, spilling onto the plane it just cut and lighting the underside of the front face; the solid sits in a deep soft cast shadow plus a tight contact occlusion. Checks re-measured on the round-4 renders (superseded where round 6 restates them below): focal 58.7% of tile, optical centre (508, 500), margins L207 R216 T226 B250, figure-ground 4.67:1 against the un-planed side and 7.56:1 against the trued side, the two fields 1.62:1 apart. The concept flaw the rasters carried is fixed and measured: the trued side reads L 0.920 against the un-planed side's L 0.746, a +0.174 delta the right way round, where C2 measured −0.043 inverted. The point previously deducted at #10 is now earned back on evidence, not assertion: rendered under aggressive monochrome and dark tints side-by-side with the old master, the old one collapses — blade and un-planed field flatten to one value and the cutting edge is lost — while the rebuilt solid holds, because its identity is now a form relationship (a lit solid casting a shadow onto a two-state ground) rather than a colour relationship. The raw field ratio under that tint is a modest 1.69:1; what carries the read is the chamfer highlight between the two faces, the rim light on the worn back, and the cast shadow, none of which a tint can remove.

Round 5 — the shaving, and the point it costs. The curl is now on the tile at the fourth attempt, and it took a change of model rather than a change of drawing. The three that failed were all a spiral outline: a closed curve with a hole in it, which is a shell. This one is a swept surface — one cross-section curve, a nearly straight tail leaving the blade easing into a loose open hook of 0.78 of a turn, swept along the blade's own axis by the ribbon's width, then cut into 96 bands each shaded by its real facing angle to the single top-left light. Bands whose outer face turns toward the viewer are lit; the ones on the far side are seen from the inside through the open end of the roll, so they take the shadow family plus a transmitted lift where the outer face is in light. The free end tapers in opacity because that is the thinnest, most-curled material. Two things were read off C2 rather than invented: its cross-section is a circle seen obliquely, so it is compressed to 0.54 along the roll's axis — a true circle draws a full ellipse at the mouth and the whole thing reads as a capped tin can; and its curl is not a pale shape on a dark ground, measuring L 0.576 at its lit top against ground of L 0.635 right beside it and falling to L 0.27 at the bottom. It reads by form-shading and hairline edges, never by a value jump. This one holds to that: 1.25:1 against the un-planed ground it was shaved from, against an internal modelling range of 3.66:1. Staying under one full turn matters twice — past it the ribbon closes into a tube and reads as a roll of tape, and the open hook is what “partially unrolled” actually looks like. Polarity is unharmed and marginally better: trued L 0.920 against un-planed L 0.737, +0.184, up from +0.174. The blade and both fields are geometrically untouched, so figure-ground is unchanged. The deduction is at #4, and it is the honest cost of the addition. Rendered 16px against the no-curl master, the blade's dark diagonal, the vermilion hone and the two-state split are all completely intact — identity is not damaged, which is why this is a deduction and not a hard fail — but the curl contributes a small low-information tan patch above-left of the blade where the previous master had immaculate empty ground. It is a soft pale note, as intended, and it is still noise. The curl repays that at #11: the tile now carries the physical evidence that the plane cut, which is the one thing the two-object version never had.

Round 6 — the pitch, and why the tile still didn't read as a cut. The user's whole note was that it doesn't look like wood is being planed, because the angle of the block is flat unlike C2 — and that was exactly right. Round 4 lifted the top face by one constant rise, so the top face was a parallel copy of the footprint and the front face was a band of even height: an iron lying on the boards, not one taking a shaving off them. The mass was there and the attitude wasn't. What was measured off C2 rather than invented: its ground/hone line runs 38.9°, its top-face shoulder runs 41.9°, and its front face grows from about 55px deep at the near end to 90px at the far end — a wedge, not a band. That is the whole tell. What changed: the lift is now linear in local x — 48px at the leading end, 132px at the trailing end, 2.75:1 — so the front face pinches almost shut where the iron is buried in the timber and opens out behind it. Because a linear lift stays affine, the lifted top face is still one matrix, a shear of the blade's own frame, and the stone texture, the top-face gradient, the grind striations and the rim light all ride it without a second transform. The top-face edges come out at 38.9° against the hone's 33°, +5.9° of pitch, matching C2's shoulder-versus-ground offset. The footprint, the cutting edge and the before/after boundary all live at local y=0 and are untouched by the shear, so the signature cannot drift and the banner's derived split needed no re-derivation. Scored, not asserted: against C2 on the create-mac-icon fidelity harness the composite improves at every size — 1024 0.3504→0.3623, 256 0.3334→0.3495, 128 0.3436→0.3567, 32 0.6747→0.6898, 16 0.7500→0.7595 — and the Pareto gate ACCEPTs at +0.0657 net with no size regressing. Polarity is untouched at +0.177 (trued L 0.920, un-planed L 0.743), against +0.178 for the flat build measured the same way. Figure-ground is unchanged within noise: the same sampler reads 6.20:1 against the un-planed side after the pitch versus 6.34:1 before, and the two fields sit 1.75:1 apart either way. What it costs: the trailing end now stands 42px taller, so the top safe-zone margin goes 226→184 (L207 R216 B250 unchanged) and the optical centre lifts to (508, 479); the focal is still 58.7% of the tile. Not a deduction — 184px is still 18% of the canvas — but it is the budget spent. One latent bug fell out of the same edit: the top-face gradient was still anchored to the un-lifted blade frame, so it had been 88px out of register with the face it filled since round 4. It is now on MATRIX_TOP with the face. Score holds at 11/12 — the #4 deduction is the curl's small-size noise and the pitch neither worsens nor repairs it; at 32px and 16px the dark diagonal, the vermilion hone and the two-state split are all intact, and the wedge is still legible at 32.

Shipping: A (icon.svg) at 11/12, by user direction. icon.png, icon-256.png and icon-128.png all render from it. The earlier split — highest-scoring take A, shipping take C2 — is closed, and it closed by rebuilding A rather than by re-scoring anything. The instruction was to pull the vector toward the best of both rasters: C2's whetstone mass, C1 and C2's warm textured ground, a genuinely emissive hone, a deep soft contact shadow — while fixing the flaw both rasters shipped with. What materially changed: the flat bar became an extruded solid (top face lifted clear of the ground, front face dropping back to it, chamfer between them); both ground planes gained blurred warm mottle over the boundary-crossing grain; the hone became real light, with a heavily blurred spill on the trued plane and a warm bounce up the front face; the shadow became a deep soft cast plus a tight contact occlusion. What got fixed: polarity. The trued side now measures L 0.920 against the un-planed side's L 0.746, +0.174 the right way round, where C2 measured −0.043 inverted. What finally landed on the fourth attempt: the shaving curl, and it landed by abandoning the construction rather than redrawing it. Three rounds drew a spiral outline, which is a shell; this is a ribbon surface, swept along the blade's axis and banded, with every band's tone derived from its real facing angle to the one light. The tile now carries three objects — surface, blade and the material the blade took off. It is not free: the score moves 12/12 to 11/12, deducted at #4, because the 16px render now carries a small low-information patch where it used to carry clean ground. Identity at 16px is intact and was verified against the no-curl master side by side, which is why it is a deduction and not a hard fail. What round 6 then fixed: the attitude. Round 4 gave the iron mass but lifted its top face by one constant rise, so it sat dead level — a bar on the boards rather than a tool mid-cut, which is exactly what the user reported. The lift is now linear along the blade: 48px at the leading end, 132px at the trailing end, so the front face is a wedge and the block rides nose-down at C2's working angle, +5.9° of pitch against the hone line. Scored on the create-mac-icon fidelity harness against C2, the composite improves at every size and the gate ACCEPTs at +0.0657 net. Consequence for the banner: its ground split was derived from C2's 40° cut, so it was re-derived on A's 33° boundary and now runs continuous through the placed icon to within 0.1px — verified at 2× on the junction crop, not assumed. What ships now carries none of the raster liabilities: it is layered vector on four named planes, editable as a diff through build_icon.py, and it survives tinting.

Rubric: the 12-point icon audit from icon-directions.md (mask discipline, grid, silhouette, 16px identity, single light model, palette discipline, figure-ground, depth coherence, era fit, variant robustness, personality, no-text) — checks 1–4 non-negotiable, delivery bar ≥10/12. Raster takes audited on their squircle-masked 1024 versions. Render commands: rsvg-convert -w {1024,256,64,32} for SVGs, the same via an embedded-image wrapper for rasters.

The shared spec all four takes were built from

  • Direction: Tahoe gel-glass sub-register (a), porcelain plus gel object, crossed with device bank #16 (the icon performs the verb) and #5 (dual-function primitive). Runner-up and near-neighbour: Direction 7, Diagonal Tool, which the catalogue names the most template-worn move in the corpus. This enters that territory deliberately and inverts it: in Direction 7 the tool crosses an inert field and the tool is the identity. Here the tool has changed the field, and the identity is the change.
  • Glyph: the whole tile is the workpiece. A worn plane iron lies mid-pass on a rising diagonal — a real extruded solid, 640 × 152 top face at 33° lifted clear of the ground with a front face dropping back to it, crisp corners on the honed edge and worn rounding with a shallow sag on the back. Since round 6 the lift is pitched: 48px at the leading end where the iron is buried in the timber, 132px at the trailing end, so the front face is a wedge and the block rides nose-down at a working angle instead of sitting level. Rising off its top rear edge, the shaving: a ribbon 106 wide swept along the blade's own axis through an open hook of 0.78 of a turn, occupying a 262 × 250 bbox against the blade's 601 × 590, so the blade stays the hero.
  • Signature: the before-and-after boundary is the diagonal, and the one vermilion hone line is that boundary. One shape doing four jobs since the round-4 rebuild: the tool's cutting edge, the division between two states, the icon's only accent, and the line where the solid meets the ground.
  • Light: one soft top-left source, plus the sanctioned second source, an emissive interior; here the hone itself, spilling onto the surface it has just cut.
  • Palette: two families. Warm-neutral porcelain (#DCD8CD→#B2AB9B un-planed, #FFFFFE→#F7F2E7 trued), achromatic graphite (#4B5159→#101215), and one vermilion reserved strictly for the honed edge (#C4341A/#F2542D/#FF7C46).
  • #10 layer plan: bg the two-state cushion tile; mid the grain and the hone's spill; fg the blade, the carrying shape; highlight the hone line and rim light.

Failure-mode anti-checklist

  • Template genericism: clear. No stock gel glyph, no saturated gradient tile.
  • Diagonal-tool default: entered knowingly, not by reflex, and inverted so the tool is not the identity. Stated as the run's one aesthetic risk.
  • White-on-hue with no translucency cues: not applicable, porcelain register.
  • Tone-on-tone collapse: measured, not assumed. 4.67:1 against the un-planed side, 7.56:1 against the trued side, fields 1.62:1 apart.
  • Metaphor pile-up: three objects on the tile, and the third earned its place on the fourth attempt: surface, blade, and the shaving the blade took off. The three cut attempts were all spiral outlines — twice an annulus that read as a coat hanger, once a tapering spiral that read as a snail shell. What ships is a swept ribbon surface, which is a different object, not a better drawing of the same one. No fourth object is proposed.
  • Sibling non-differentiation: five of the six siblings share this porcelain register, which is the marketplace's house ground rather than a borrowed one. Differentiation is carried by the split, which no sibling uses.
  • Flat pre-masked raster: avoided. The shipping master is layered vector on four named planes and was verified against real monochrome and dark tint renders; the raster takes are reference alternates only.
  • Baked text: none.
