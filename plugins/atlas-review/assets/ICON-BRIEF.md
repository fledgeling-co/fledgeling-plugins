## The skill this icon is for

`atlas-review` takes the open pull requests on the Atlas monorepo from arrival to
reviewed, fixed, merged, pushed and reported. Its sibling `atlas-publish` begins
where it ends.

**The defining claim is that a check which cannot fail is not a check.** Three
quarters of machine-written tests that compile are vacuous, and reading one is
measured as poor at catching that, because a reader treats a mock as a valid
fixture. So the skill does not read a test to decide whether it works. It breaks
the code the test covers and watches the test go red. Twenty-one checks that
could not fail were found this way in one session, and not one was found by
running the tests.

The break is not damage. The break is the evidence.

## The chosen concept: the stroke you broke to prove it

Same letterform, same material, same accent as the sibling, saying the opposite
thing with them.

Take the capital **A** of the Atlas script wordmark, lifted verbatim from the
brand mark, and re-pour it as the sibling does: a soft-extruded graphite gel
monoline on warm porcelain, the same weight and the same optical sit.

Then cut **one clean fracture** across the stroke, at a single point, square to
the stroke's own direction. Not a tear and not erosion: a deliberate, surgical
gap with two flat faces, as though a scalpel took a slice out. The vermilion of
the sibling's gate shows in the gap, filling the void rather than outlining it,
so the break reads as lit from inside rather than as missing material.

The letterform continues cleanly on both sides. The stroke holds. That is the
whole idea: the mark survives the cut, and the cut is the only reason you know it
holds.

## What the composition must do

- **The gap is the subject.** It sits at optical centre; the letterform is
  arranged around it, not the other way round.
- **One break only.** Two reads as damage. One reads as a measurement.
- **The faces are flat and parallel.** A jagged break says something broke. A cut
  says somebody did it on purpose and is watching what happens.
- **Legible at 32px.** The A survives, and the gap survives as a bright interruption
  in a dark stroke. If the gap closes up at small sizes, widen it rather than
  thinning the stroke.

## Rejected, and why, so they are not re-proposed

- **A magnifying glass over code.** The whole thesis is that looking does not
  work. An icon of looking would state the opposite of the skill.
- **A tick, a shield, or a checklist.** Generic to every review tool, and this one
  is defined by distrusting a passing check rather than by producing one.
- **Two strokes side by side, before and after.** Reads as a comparison, and the
  point is that one thing is tested rather than that two things differ.
- **A crack with jagged edges.** Says breakage. The cut must say intent.
