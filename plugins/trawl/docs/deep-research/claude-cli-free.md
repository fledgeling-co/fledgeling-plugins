# Maximizing Idea Diversity and Quality in LLM-Based Divergent Ideation: Evidence Report

## Executive Summary

- **(High Confidence)** LLM ideation homogenization is real, large, and measured at three levels: within one model's samples (~95% of 4,000 sampled research ideas were semantic duplicates at cosine ≥0.8 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)), across users of the same model (94% concept overlap in ChatGPT-assisted toy design; nine independent users produced the identical name "Build-a-Breeze Castle" [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x)), and *across model families* — LLM outputs are more similar to other LLMs' outputs than humans' are to each other, so swapping models does not restore diversity [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361).
- **(High Confidence)** The strongest single prompt-level mitigation is a generate-then-differentiate chain-of-thought pass ("modify the ideas to make them bolder and more different; no two the same"), which cut pooled cosine similarity from 0.377 to 0.255 — near the human-group benchmark of 0.243 — while personas gave only modest gains (0.348–0.368) and similarity-feedback loops actively made diversity *worse* (0.432, worst of 35 strategies tested) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727).
- **(High Confidence)** Temperature is not a creativity dial: it correlates weakly with novelty and moderately with incoherence [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00492). The measured alternative is Verbalized Sampling — asking the model to output a distribution of k responses with probabilities and sampling the tails — yielding 1.6–2.1× diversity with no quality loss on aligned models [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171).
- **(High Confidence)** For divergent tasks specifically, multi-agent structure beats single-agent iteration: single-agent self-refinement suffers documented Degeneration-of-Thought [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118), and role-played multi-agent discussion scored 4.44 originality on the Alternative Uses Test vs 3.47 for a single agent and 3.73 for debate [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06373). But for convergent/factual tasks, debate does not reliably beat cheap self-consistency at matched cost [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371).
- **(High Confidence)** The strongest anti-convergence mechanisms in the literature are population structures, not conversation: MAP-Elites archives with LLM feedback (QDAIF, ICLR 2024) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13032) and FunSearch's island model — independent islands, prompts built from k=2 same-island samples, worst half of islands killed and reseeded every 4 hours — which produced publishable mathematics [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6).
- **(High Confidence)** LLM judges are unreliable for direct 1–10 novelty/feasibility scoring: scores cluster centrally [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01724), position bias flips verdicts (GPT-4 only 65% order-consistent) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), and the Stanford ideation study abandoned direct scoring for a pairwise Swiss tournament (71.4% accuracy) after finding direct score prediction uncalibrated [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109). A panel of three small heterogeneous judges beat one GPT-4 judge at ~1/7 the cost [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796).
- **(High Confidence)** Novelty and feasibility scores mean less than they appear: when 43 experts actually executed AI-generated vs human ideas, the AI ideas' novelty and effectiveness ratings collapsed (−1.98 vs −0.63 for human ideas) and the rankings flipped [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803). Human raters show a novelty–feasibility trade-off (r=−0.42); LLM judges show none, indicating they don't model the tension [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08901).
- **(Medium Confidence)** RLHF/alignment is a root cause, not just a correlate, of the diversity loss: alignment reduced the "Creativity Index" of models by ~30% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265), RLHF measurably trades diversity for generalization [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452), and the mechanism is traced to typicality bias in preference data that persists even under a perfect reward model [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171).

## Detailed Findings

### Q1a. Measured homogenization / mode collapse of LLM idea generation, and mitigations

**The phenomenon.** Doshi & Hauser's 293-writer experiment (Science Advances, July 2024) found GPT-4 story ideas raised individual novelty +5.4–8.1% and usefulness up to +9.0%, but raised between-story cosine similarity +8.9–10.7% versus human-only writing — individual gain, collective loss [science.org](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). High confidence. Anderson, Shah & Kreminski's 36-participant controlled study (ACM Creativity & Cognition 2024, 1,271 ideas) found ChatGPT users produced more and more-detailed ideas that were measurably more homogeneous *across users* (d=0.47, p=.038) than users of a non-LLM creativity tool [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536). High confidence. Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch's Nature Human Behaviour reanalysis (May 2025) confirmed the pattern across five experiments, with the 94% concept-overlap result [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x); Lee & Chung's published reply accepts the group-diversity finding [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02195-5). High confidence.

Within-model saturation is quantified by Si, Yang & Hashimoto (ICLR 2025): over-generating 4,000 seed ideas per topic with a Claude-3.5-Sonnet agent, semantic dedup (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, cosine threshold 0.8 chosen by manual inspection) left only ~5% unique ideas, with new-batch uniqueness decaying toward a plateau — "just sample more" hits a hard wall [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109). High confidence. Wenger & Kenett (Jan 2025) show the homogeneity is cross-model, controlling for response structure [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361). High confidence for the claim's existence; effect sizes were verified only at summary level.

**Root cause.** Kirk et al. (ICLR 2024) established that RLHF substantially reduces per-input and cross-input output diversity relative to SFT — the canonical generalization–diversity trade-off [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452). The Creativity Index work (ICLR 2025 oral) found professional authors' scores ~66.2% higher than LLMs' and that alignment reduces a model's Creativity Index ~30% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265). Verbalized Sampling (Zhang et al., Oct 2025) traces the mechanism to *typicality bias* in human preference annotations, showing formally it persists even with a perfect reward model [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171). High confidence.

**Mitigations, ranked by measured effect:**

1. **Generate-then-differentiate CoT.** Wharton's 35-strategy benchmark (GPT-4, 1,000 ideas/strategy): best prompt = generate 100 titles, then explicitly "modify the ideas as needed to make them bolder and more different. No two ideas should be the same," then elaborate. Pool cosine similarity 0.255 vs base 0.377 (human MBA groups: 0.243); estimated reachable unique-idea space grew from ~3,700 to ~4,700 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727). High confidence.
2. **Verbalized Sampling.** 1.6–2.1× diversity in creative writing, +25.7% human ratings, recovering 66.8% of base-model diversity; training-free and orthogonal to temperature [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171). High confidence on headline numbers. <CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>At least one follow-up analysis found VS approximately equal to (or below) a plain "generate all N in one response" baseline — meaning batch generation in a single context is itself a measured diversity technique, and VS's marginal gain over it is contested (per the practitioner-research sweep; specific rebuttal paper not independently re-verified). Sources: arXiv:2510.01171 vs. follow-up analysis noted in agent findings.</CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>
3. **Persona/frame diversity — real but modest, with conditions.** Best persona in the Wharton benchmark: 0.348 vs base 0.377; celebrity personas (Steve Jobs, 0.368) no better than generic creative ones [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727). A 2025/26 factorial audit across 7 models finds persona *breadth* beats persona depth — one-sentence occupation personas capture most of the gain, while generic "be diverse" instructions and temperature tuning do approximately nothing [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20429v1). Feng, Hélie & Panchal (Design Science, Dec 2025; GPT-4o/Gemini-1.5/Llama-3.1): any multi-persona prompting beats none; **parallel personas in separate sessions ≈ sequential switching; all-personas-in-one-prompt is worst** [cambridge.org](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/enhancing-design-concept-diversity-multipersona-prompting-strategies-for-large-language-models/3B346E253508337A4EE899499BE49D9B). High confidence. Wan & Kalman (2026) show 10 diverse GenAI personas preserved collective story diversity in a direct extension of Doshi & Hauser — homogenization stems from *uniform* deployment, not GenAI per se [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13868). Medium-High. Counterweight: Zheng et al. (162 personas × 4 model families × 2,410 factual questions) found personas give no accuracy benefit — personas are a diversity lever, not a quality lever [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054). High confidence.
4. **Semantic de-duplication.** A de facto standard has emerged: sentence-embedding cosine with threshold ~0.8 (Si et al. use all-MiniLM-L6-v2 at 0.8; the Wharton study uses Universal Sentence Encoder with ≥0.8 = duplicate) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)[arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727). Note this is a convention set by manual inspection, not a validated constant; other pipelines calibrate to 0.87 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27905) or use 0.7 against retrieved prior art [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20844). High confidence on the practice, Medium on any specific threshold.
5. **Sampling parameters.** Temperature: weak novelty correlation, moderate incoherence correlation (Peeperkorn et al., ICCC 2024) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00492). High confidence. Min-p sampling claimed quality+diversity wins at high temperature (ICLR 2025 oral) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082) but a 2025 rebuttal reruns the evidence and finds it statistically indistinguishable from baselines [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13681). <CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>Min-p's superiority: original authors claim human-preferred diversity/quality gains; "Min-p, Max Exaggeration" disputes both the statistics and the human eval. Treat as unresolved; Low-Medium confidence.</CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>

**What measurably fails:** telling the model the cosine similarity of existing ideas (0.432, worst strategy), seeding with previous top ideas (0.403), pasting in HBR/Stanford brainstorming best-practice articles (0.387–0.392, worse than baseline), emotional appeals/tipping/threats (~no effect) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727). High confidence.

### Q1b. Structured multi-agent ideation architectures

**Tree/Graph of Thoughts.** ToT's dramatic gains are on verifiable search problems (Game of 24: 74% vs 4% CoT, GPT-4) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601); its creative-writing gain is modest (GPT-4-judged coherency 7.56 vs 6.93; humans preferred ToT 41/100 vs 21) at ~5,500 tokens/problem. High confidence for puzzles, Medium for ideation value. GoT beats ToT ~62% on sorting error at 31% lower cost, but all its tasks are decomposable-verifiable — no ideation evidence exists; its relevance to brainstorming is the architectural idea of *aggregating* branches [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09687). High on results, Low on transfer.

**Debate.** Du et al. (ICML 2024): 3 agents × 2 rounds lifts arithmetic 67%→82%, GSM8K 77%→85% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325). Liang et al. (EMNLP 2024) documents **Degeneration-of-Thought**: once confident, a self-reflecting LLM cannot generate novel thoughts even when wrong; their two-debater+judge fix works with *modest* disagreement and an adaptive stopping rule, and warns the judge is biased when debaters use different LLMs [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118). High confidence on the phenomenon. The critical literature is equally firm: Smit et al. (ICML 2024) found debate does not reliably beat self-consistency at matched inference cost and is hypersensitive to hyperparameters (tuned "agreement modulation" can rescue it) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371); Zhang et al. (Feb 2025) found 5 MAD frameworks failing to reliably beat CoT/self-consistency across 9 benchmarks, with **model heterogeneity the one lever that helps** [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788). High confidence. The reconciliation: debate's documented value is convergent (error-correction); its divergent value runs through avoiding DoT, and discussion formats beat adversarial debate for originality.

**Multi-agent discussion for creativity.** LLM Discussion (2024): three-phase (initiate → discuss → converge) role-play with 4 agents × 5 rounds scored AUT originality 4.44 vs LLM-debate 3.73, brainstorm-then-select 3.84, zero-shot CoT 3.52, single agent 3.47 (1–5 scale); human eval over 1,406 responses confirmed with Kendall's τ=0.52 human–LLM originality correlation [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06373). Medium-High confidence (single base model, not a major-venue camera-ready). A 2025 design study finds cohort size, interaction depth, and persona heterogeneity each independently enrich idea diversity [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08350). Medium confidence.

**Quality-diversity and evolutionary approaches — the strongest anti-convergence evidence.** QDAIF (ICLR 2024) runs MAP-Elites where the LLM is both mutation operator and quality/diversity evaluator; it beat non-QD baselines on coverage×quality across opinion writing, stories, and poetry, with human raters confirming the elite sets were more diverse and higher quality [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13032). High confidence (Medium on absolute effect sizes — partly self-judged). FunSearch (Nature, Dec 2023) is the existence proof at the frontier: LLM proposal + programmatic evaluator + island model produced new mathematics (cap-set records, bin-packing heuristics). Its anti-convergence machinery is concrete: independent islands; prompts sample k=2 programs from within one island; within-island clustering by score signature favoring shorter programs; **every 4 hours, the worst half of islands are killed and reseeded from survivors' best** [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6). AlphaEvolve (2025) generalized this to an evolutionary coding agent that found a 48-multiplication 4×4 complex matrix-multiply (beating Strassen's 49 after 56 years, independently verified; caveat: commutative-ring methods like Waksman's 46 predate it) and improved ~20% of 50+ open math problems [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131). High for verified math, Medium for self-reported infrastructure claims. EvoPrompt (ICLR 2024) shows population-based LLM variation beats single-trajectory refinement (up to +25% on Big-Bench Hard) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532). High confidence.

<INFERENCE from="Feng et al. parallel≈sequential>collective; Zhang et al. 2025 heterogeneity finding; Wharton hybrid-pooling recommendation; FunSearch island isolation">For a 5-agent brainstorming skill, the evidence pattern supports: run frames in *isolated parallel* contexts (not one shared conversation), make the frames maximally heterogeneous (breadth over depth), do not share intermediate ideas between agents during divergence (sharing = the failed similarity-feedback/seeding strategies + anchoring), and pool only at the end (low inter-pool overlap is where hybrid prompting's gain comes from).</INFERENCE>

### Q1c. Scoring and convergence

**Judge biases (quantified).** Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2023): position bias — GPT-4 only 65.0% consistent under answer-order swap (30% first-position biased), GPT-3.5 46.2%, Claude-v1 23.8%; verbosity attack failure 91.3% (Claude-v1, GPT-3.5) vs 8.7% (GPT-4); self-enhancement ~+10% (GPT-4) to ~+25% (Claude-v1) win-rate inflation, flagged as underpowered; GPT-4↔human agreement 85%, above human-human 81% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685). High confidence. Stureborg et al. (2024): familiarity (low-perplexity) bias, skewed and clustered score distributions, anchoring across multi-attribute rubrics [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01724). High on findings. CALM (ICLR 2025) formalizes 12 bias types measured via perturbation attacks; frontier judges retain significant residual bias [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736). High on framework.

**Direct scoring vs pairwise.** Si et al. found LLMs "poorly calibrated when asked directly to predict final scores," abandoned rubric scoring for a Claude-3.5-Sonnet **pairwise Swiss tournament** that reached 71.4% accuracy predicting ICLR acceptance between submission pairs [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109). A 2026 axiomatic benchmark against ~25,139 expert novelty ratings found *every* automated proxy near chance — n-gram novelty, embedding distance, perplexity, NLI, GNNs, reward models — with the best retrieval-augmented judge capping at r≈0.35 and plain LLM-as-judge worst due to central-tendency collapse ("near-identical mediocre ratings") [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15145). Medium-High (recent preprint). Embedding-distance novelty correlates with experts at only r≈0.29–0.37 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08901). Medium. Humans show a novelty–feasibility trade-off r=−0.42; LLM judges show ~0 correlation between the two dimensions [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08901). Medium confidence, but load-bearing: an LLM scoring both dimensions is not modeling the tension that makes the trade-off informative.

**Panels beat single judges.** PoLL (Cohere, 2024): a jury of three small heterogeneous judges (Command R + GPT-3.5 + Haiku, vote-pooled) beat a single GPT-4 judge on human correlation (Cohen's κ 0.763 vs 0.627 on KILT-NQ) at ~1/7–1/8 the cost, and reduced self-preference because judge families are disjoint; single-judge choice drastically changed model rankings [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796). High confidence.

**Feasibility scores overstate.** The Ideation–Execution Gap study (Jun 2025): 43 experts executed randomly assigned ideas (>100h each); AI ideas' pre-execution novelty advantage (5.78 vs 4.91) reversed after execution — AI ideas dropped −1.05 novelty, −1.76 excitement, −1.88 effectiveness while human ideas were roughly flat; overall gap −1.98 vs −0.63, p<0.01 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803). High confidence. Sakana's AI Scientist illustrates the failure mode in deployed systems: an external evaluation found its 1–10 interestingness/novelty/feasibility scores "arbitrarily assigned and unused," with systematic positivity bias, while its retrieval-based novelty check (Semantic Scholar, up to 10 queries × 10 results) was the functional component [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14297v1). High confidence.

**Cluster-then-select in practice.** Published ideation pipelines prefer threshold dedup + tournament over k-means-style clustering: Si et al. (cosine 0.8 → Swiss tournament); TrustResearcher (weighted rubric novelty .30/feasibility .25/clarity .20/impact .25; LLM-merge pairs at Jaccard>0.85; reject at BGE-M3 cosine >0.7 vs literature) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20844); GraphEval decomposes ideas into viewpoint-nodes and propagates labels for +14% F1 over judge baselines [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12600). <MISSING_DATA>No primary ideation paper was found using explicit k-means/HDBSCAN cluster-then-pick-winner; the pattern is standard in data curation (e.g., SemDedup) but unvalidated for idea selection specifically. What would be needed: a controlled comparison of threshold-dedup+tournament vs cluster-then-select on idea pools with human ground truth.</MISSING_DATA>

### Q1d. Creativity benchmarks and metrics

Psychometric results are consistently flattering to LLMs: GPT-4 top-1% on TTCT originality/fluency (blind-scored by Scholastic Testing Service) [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2713374523000249); GPT-4 beats 96% of humans on the Divergent Association Task under greedy decoding [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11158); GPT-4 "robustly more creative" than 151 humans on AUT/Consequences/DAT [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53303-w); chatbots beat humans *on average* on AUT, but best humans match or exceed best AI [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-40858-3); against 100,000 humans, GPT-4 sits significantly above the human mean on DAT while **top humans surpass all LLMs** [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13012). All High confidence.

Product-based and contamination-aware evaluations reverse the picture: on the expert-designed Torrance Test of Creative Writing, LLM stories passed 3–10× fewer tests than professional writers' stories, and LLM-as-judge did not correlate with the experts (CHI 2024) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14556); TTCT/AUT items are public and likely contaminated [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07175); CreativityIndex puts professional authors ~66.2% above LLMs on non-reconstructable text [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265). High confidence. NoveltyBench (CMU, 2025) measures distinct semantic equivalence classes across repeated generations: all 20 frontier models less diverse than human writers, larger models within a family often *less* diverse than smaller ones, and >90% of 67 recent benchmark papers evaluate only single generations — diversity is systematically unmeasured by mainstream evals [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05228). High confidence.

Useful metrics for a pipeline: DSI (mean cosine distance between contextual embeddings across a narrative) explains up to 72% of variance in human creativity ratings [ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10615993/); distinct-n [aclanthology.org](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1014/) and self-BLEU [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01886) remain standard lexical-diversity measures (note the documented bug in the original self-BLEU implementation). Trajectory: creativity appears to have plateaued or regressed in newer aligned models — GPT-4o (2025) scored *worse* than GPT-4 (2023) on AUT [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2713374525000202) (Medium), consistent with GPT-4-turbo declining vs GPT-4 on DAT [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13012) (High).

### Q1e. Practical prompt-level findings (2024–2026)

Beyond the Wharton ranking (Q1a), the verified practitioner-relevant findings:

- **Volume in one response.** Ask for many ideas in a single generation rather than one-at-a-time; this is both Mollick's core tactic [oneusefulthing.org](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/how-to-use-ai-to-generate-ideas) and the baseline that Verbalized Sampling was measured against. High confidence.
- **Hybrid pooling.** Between-strategy idea overlap is low, so combining pools generated under *different* prompts is explicitly recommended over perfecting one prompt [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727). High confidence.
- **Sequencing to avoid anchoring.** Humans exposed to AI examples early fixate: N=60 experiment showed higher fixation, fewer ideas, less variety, lower originality with AI image examples [dl.acm.org](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3613904.3642919); AI-augmented brainwriting works when humans commit ideas *before* seeing LLM additions [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14978). Conversely, *passive* exposure to AI ideas in a dynamic collective setting increased group diversity without changing individual creativity [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13481). High confidence.
- **Official vendor guidance is absent.** Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI docs/cookbooks contain a dedicated ideation-diversity guide as of Aug 2026 (verified absence). High confidence.
- <INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE>"Ban the first N obvious answers" as an isolated, measured technique: no study isolates this variable. The closest verified evidence is the Wharton CoT differentiate step (helped) versus seeding with prior top ideas and similarity feedback (hurt) — suggesting instructional differentiation works but idea-content feedback backfires. Likewise, no credible measured study of SCAMPER/random-word/oblique-strategies stimuli with LLMs was found; only SEO content.</INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE>

### Q2. Current state and strongest supporting evidence

The field has converged (2024–2026) on a consistent account: aligned LLMs are individually strong, collectively narrow ideators. The strongest evidence chain is: mechanism (typicality bias survives perfect reward models [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171); RLHF diversity loss, ICLR 2024 [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452)) → within-model saturation (5% unique at 4,000 samples [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)) → cross-user homogenization (Science Advances, NHB, C&C — three peer-reviewed venues agreeing [science.org](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)) → cross-model homogeneity [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361). The best-evidenced countermeasures are architectural (QD archives, islands, isolated parallel frames, panel judging, embedding dedup) rather than parametric (temperature) or motivational (threats, tips, "be creative").

### Q3. Contrasting viewpoints and competing evidence

Three live disputes, presented with both positions: (1) **Multi-agent value** — LLM Discussion and DoT argue structure creates divergence a single agent cannot [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118); Smit et al. and Zhang et al. show debate ≤ self-consistency at matched cost on convergent tasks [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371). Resolution in the data: the disagreement dissolves once task type (divergent vs convergent) is conditioned on. (2) **Min-p sampling** — ICLR 2025 oral vs a direct statistical rebuttal [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13681); unresolved. (3) **LLM creativity level** — psychometric top-percentile results vs product-based expert evaluations showing 3–10× deficits [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14556); contamination and construct-validity critiques favor the product-based reading. (4) **Personas** — no accuracy benefit on factual tasks [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054) vs measured diversity benefit in ideation [cambridge.org](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/enhancing-design-concept-diversity-multipersona-prompting-strategies-for-large-language-models/3B346E253508337A4EE899499BE49D9B); these are answers to different questions.

### Q4. What changed recently, and the trajectory

2023–2024 established the homogenization problem (Doshi & Hauser; Anderson; Kirk) and the flattering psychometrics. 2024–2025 delivered the systematic prompt benchmark (Wharton, Jan 2024), the pre-registered ideation ground truth (Si et al., Sep 2024), the judge-bias formalizations (CALM, PoLL), and diversity-native benchmarks (NoveltyBench). 2025–2026's turn is toward mechanism and correction: typicality bias + Verbalized Sampling (Oct 2025 → ICML 2026), the Ideation–Execution Gap (Jun 2025) deflating novelty scores, evolutionary agents at the frontier (AlphaEvolve, May 2025), and evidence that raw model creativity has plateaued while alignment pressure grows [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2713374525000202). <INFERENCE from="NoveltyBench larger-models-less-diverse finding; GPT-4o<GPT-4 AUT result; CreativityIndex −30% alignment effect">Trajectory: diversity engineering is migrating from model choice to harness design — newer/larger aligned models cannot be assumed more divergent, so the returns to architectural mitigations (the subject of this report) are rising, not falling.</INFERENCE>

## Knowledge Gaps

**Caused by absence of studies:** (a) No study isolates "ban the first N obvious answers" as a variable — the technique is plausible by analogy to the Wharton differentiate step but unmeasured. (b) No measured study of SCAMPER/oblique-strategies/random-word stimuli with LLMs. (c) No controlled comparison of cluster-then-select vs threshold-dedup+tournament for idea convergence. (d) No dedicated vendor (Anthropic/OpenAI) guidance on ideation diversity exists to defer to.

**Caused by contested evidence:** (e) Min-p sampling's benefit (oral-accepted paper vs statistical rebuttal). (f) Verbalized Sampling's marginal gain over plain batch generation.

**Caused by measurement limits:** (g) Automated novelty scoring has a validated ceiling of r≈0.35 against experts — every pipeline's novelty score, including LLM-judged, sits below a modest ceiling; effect sizes for Wenger & Kenett and Wan & Kalman were verified only at abstract level. (h) Most creativity findings are GPT-3.5/GPT-4-era; only thin evidence (AUT plateau studies) covers 2025–2026 frontier models, and none covers Claude 4+ generations specifically.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Empirically benchmark the adhd skill's own pool diversity** using the Wharton protocol (embed all ideas, mean pairwise cosine + unique-idea estimate at 0.8 threshold) against a single-agent batch baseline — rationale: the literature shows intuitively-good designs (similarity feedback, idea seeding) *reduce* diversity, so the skill's frame/ban mechanics should be measured, not assumed (~a day of work with MiniLM embeddings).
2. **A/B the "ban first three obvious answers" step against the Wharton generate-then-differentiate wording** — rationale: the ban is unmeasured; the differentiate step is the best-verified prompt-level lever, and they may compose or conflict.
3. **Replace any direct 1–10 novelty/viability rubric with position-swapped pairwise tournament judged by ≥2 heterogeneous models** and re-test ranking stability — rationale: direct scores show central-tendency collapse and 71.4% pairwise accuracy is the best available; panels cost less than a frontier single judge (PoLL).
4. **Prototype a lightweight island/archive variant** (e.g., frames as islands; keep per-frame elites; one cross-pollination round only after independent divergence) and measure whether it beats one-shot fan-out — rationale: QD/island structures are the strongest-evidenced anti-convergence mechanism but their cost/benefit at 5-agent scale is unmeasured.
5. **Track the Verbalized-Sampling-vs-batch-generation dispute and the min-p rebuttal** through peer review (ICML 2026 camera-ready; rebuttal responses) — rationale: both directly determine the cheapest diversity lever worth embedding in each agent's prompt.
