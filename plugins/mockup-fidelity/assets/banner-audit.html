Recommendation.

Ships. First banner for this plugin, composed alongside its first icon rather than inherited from anything.

Every value is lifted from `build_icon.py`'s named constants: the ground gradient is GROUND_TOP through GROUND_BOT, the wordmark ink is GEL_DEEP, the body copy is GEL_MID, the dashed reference outline is SCRIBE, the slab reuses the GEL_TOP to GEL_BOT ramp, and the rule and sliver are ACCENT with its HI and LO stops. Nothing here was sampled off a sibling banner.

The right-hand device restates the icon's signature move at a size where it can be read: the reference scribed into the ground as a dashed outline, the implementation laid over it, misregistered by the same (100, 76) the icon uses, and the exposed sliver carrying the only chroma on the page. The annotation says `divergent · offset 100,76`, so the mark is labelled with what it is rather than left as decoration. That the sliver's width *is* the misregistration, rather than a symbol for it, is the whole argument of the skill.

The wordmark is Instrument Sans, and that is a deliberate choice about the set rather than about this banner. Nine of the family's twenty-seven banners already use it, making it the plurality face, and an audit of the set found twelve different display faces where `brand-and-docs.md` asks for "a set wordmark". A new banner adding a thirteenth would have made that worse. IBM Plex Mono carries the measurement annotation, which three siblings already use as their secondary.

Known liabilities. The annotation is small and low contrast by design, and it disappears entirely at the 400px catalogue-card width, so the device has to work as a shape alone at that size; it does, but the label is large-size only. The slab and the icon are the same object at two scales, which reads as deliberate at 900px and as slight repetition at 1600px. And rows 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 are one person's assessment rather than a panel's.

Two things caught by looking rather than by the script. The first render wrapped the annotation onto a second line and pushed it near the bottom edge, and `render_banner.py` reported `overflow: []` throughout, because the text wrapped inside its own box rather than escaping the frame. The device also sat close enough to the right edge to look cropped without being cropped. Both are exactly the class of defect the sheet exists for, and neither is mechanically detectable.
