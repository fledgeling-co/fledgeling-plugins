Ships. This is the re-ground of the periwinkle master, not a new design: the graticule lens and its knurled bezel were the good part of the previous icon and they survive intact. What moved is the palette, and the banner moved with it.

Every colour here is lifted from `build_icon.py`'s named constants rather than judged by eye, which is what rubric row 6 asks for. The ground gradient is GROUND_TOP through GROUND_BOT (`#F9F5EE` to `#DED7C5`), the background plane and the decorative lens ring are GRID_SOFT and BARREL_MID, the rule is ACCENT (`#DA5526`), and the two ink values are warm neutrals in the barrel's family. The three status chips keep their green and amber, because semantic colour is a separate axis from the brand accent and collapsing them would cost the reader the pass/advisory/inconclusive distinction the skill is actually about.

Known liabilities, in order. The body copy's second line ends close to the decorative lens on the right; it does not overlap and both are legible at 900px, but there is less air there than anywhere else in the set, and a longer essence line would collide. Rows 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 are one person's assessment rather than a panel's, so they carry the same caveat the icon's own sheet carries: nothing independent has preferred this composition. And the icon it sits beside is the palest of the family's warm group, which the icon sheet records as a deliberate trade against figure-ground; the banner inherits that quietness.

Worth recording why this banner existed in a wrong state at all. Its icon was rebuilt and the banner was not, so it displayed a violet icon on a violet ground beside a porcelain-and-ember master, at a correct 3200x1040 with a properly linked web font. Every mechanical check passed. The banner also referenced `icon-1024.png`, which the rebuild had renamed, so a re-render would have produced a hole rather than an error. Both are now checks: row 4 compares the icon's hash against the one recorded when the sheet was rendered, and row 2 resolves the reference.
