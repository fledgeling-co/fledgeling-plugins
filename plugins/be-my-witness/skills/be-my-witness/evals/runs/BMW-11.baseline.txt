No. A different fixture explains different *data* — other names, numbers, dates filling the same controls — but it doesn't explain a missing control. The primary call-to-action is part of the surface's structure, and the expectation names it specifically; structure should be invariant across fixtures. If the button were present with different label text or a different target, that would be the fixture talking. Its complete absence is either a real regression (the control isn't rendering) or a conditional-rendering path where this fixture's data legitimately suppresses the CTA — and if the latter, that's a finding to surface explicitly ("CTA hidden when X is empty — is that intended?"), not something to wave through as fixture noise. So: accept the legitimate data differences, but flag the missing CTA as a failure against the expectation until someone confirms the suppression is by design.
