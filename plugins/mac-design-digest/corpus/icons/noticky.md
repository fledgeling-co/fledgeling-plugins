## Signature devices
- **Glowing note on a black stage** `[GOLDEN-NUGGET]` — the note is not lit, it *emits*; a warm halo blooms into pure black. This is the "always on top / visible even in fullscreen / hidden when you share" promise rendered as light — the note is the one thing still glowing on a darkened screen. Subject-mined, not decorative: the glow *is* the product thesis.
- **Peeled corner + tilt** — a ~11° rotation with a curled top-left flap gives the flat object physicality and instantly reads as "sticky note / paper" rather than a generic yellow card. The curl is the cheapest, most legible "it's a note" signal and it survives further than the ruled lines.
- **Engraved ruled lines, not printed text** — three faint darker-gold rules suggest writing without a single letter, keeping the no-text discipline while communicating "you write on this."
- **Dark-field inversion of a bright subject** — sticky notes are conventionally shown as bright squares on light; putting the bright note on black is a committed direction (drenched-dark ground), and it's why the icon reads as a *tool that persists in the dark* rather than a generic Notes clone.

## Failures
- **#10 Variant robustness — FAIL.** This is a fixed Big-Sur-era raster, not layered Liquid Glass authored in Icon Composer. Its entire identity is hard-coded **yellow-on-black**: there is no background/foreground layer separation for the system to generate clear / tinted renders, and the meaning is colour-locked (the yellow *is* the sticky-note metaphor). Shipping in the Tahoe era it renders as a legacy icon rather than adapting to tinted/clear modes. Dark-mode is coincidentally fine (the field is already black), which is the only reason this isn't a harder break. Recorded honestly — the actionable finding for synthesis: a bright object on a self-lit black stage is expressive but does not participate in Liquid Glass variant generation.

**Soft passes** (pass, but flagged for synthesis):
- **#4 16px squint** — at menu-bar/Spotlight size the icon collapses to "yellow blob on black," which still reads as a sticky note / note app; but the peeled corner, the tilt, and all three ruled lines smear away. The high yellow-on-black luminance contrast is what carries it — the shape detail does not.
- **#9 Era coherence** — the devices (squircle, front-facing object, flat matte fill, short fold shadow) are pure Big-Sur unified, but two choices push against the era's defaults: the **pure-black field** (vs the era's light-at-top coloured sky-ramp) and the **emissive glow** (vs a short baked drop-shadow). Consistent within itself, so it passes — but it's a moody/self-lit dialect of Big Sur, adjacent to a **custom** reading, not the textbook coloured-field icon.

## Cross-icon / brand notes
- **Palette coherence with cover:** the cover shows the same golden-yellow note as the hero surface (matching `#F9D110`), a black+yellow wordmark (black rounded square containing a smaller yellow rounded square), and a Dock render of this exact icon. The app itself uses a *set* of note colours (yellow, pink `#F7C6D8`-ish, green `#C7E8A0`-ish in the screenshots); the icon commits to the **yellow** note as the brand-signature colour. Tight coherence — the icon is a faithful, literal miniature of the product (a sticky note), and the yellow is the one hue carried across icon, wordmark, and the hero note.
- **Subject communication:** unambiguous — the icon depicts its subject literally (a sticky note), the strongest form of icon-tells-you-what-it-does. The added meaning ("stays visible / on top / persists in the dark") is carried by the emissive-glow-on-black staging rather than by any glyph, which is the non-template move here.
- **Era honesty:** classified Big Sur unified on the front-facing flat matte object + fold-shadow reading; **custom (dark-stage)** is the honest adjacent reading given the black field and emissive glow. Not Liquid Glass — no translucency, refraction, specular glass edges, or Icon-Composer layer separation are present (see #10).
