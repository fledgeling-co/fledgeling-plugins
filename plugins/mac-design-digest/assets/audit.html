mac-design-digest icon — contact sheet & audit

Direction “The Stamped Value” · four takes across two engines · audited on real renders at 1024 / 128 / 64 / 48 / 32 / 16 css px from 2× retina sources, with the variant and silhouette reads captured rather than reasoned.
What actually happened here, in order, because this sheet records both a commission that was short of the pipeline's floor and a swap made against its own reading. The icon was built on 18 Aug 2026 and shipped with no contact sheet at all; a retrospective sheet was written on 19 Aug carrying one take against a floor of three, and saying so at the top. On 19 Aug the remaining engines were commissioned. Engine C ran — two GPT Image 2 rasters against four porcelain-register exemplars from create-mac-icon/references/corpus/apple-2026/ (apple-31 News, apple-26 Reminders, apple-28 Photos, apple-23 Safari), both on this page as C1 and C2 with their sources in this directory. Engine B refused. Arrow 1.1 returned “A positive credit balance is required for all requests, including BYOK” from the gateway, so there is no Arrow take and none is displayed. The skill's rule for an unavailable engine is to widen Engine A rather than to present one take as three, so a second hand-authored take was written from its own build script — a real fourth artifact, aimed squarely at the two checks the then-shipping master measurably lost. There is still no fidelity loop and no blind judge panel: the material was not scored numerically, because torch is absent on this machine and the gate refuses a tier that cannot see material. Every score below was read off the renders and the measurements beside them on 19 Aug.

Then the swap, later on 19 Aug, and it was the user's call rather than this sheet's. The inlaid graphite take now ships. It is the take this sheet first audited as A2, and it is rendered throughout as master because that id is what the sheet's own manifest means by “the take that ships”. The porcelain take that shipped from 18 Aug is preserved under its own name as icon-A1-porcelain.svg, built by build_icon_a1_porcelain.py, and is audited here as A1. Nothing was redrawn to make that happen: icon-src.svg is now the inlaid artwork byte-for-byte, icon.png, icon-256.png and icon-128.png were re-exported from it and are byte-identical to the renders this sheet was already displaying for that take, and every number in the tables below reproduced unchanged under the new labels. The swap moved no pixel; it moved which artwork the shipped filenames point at.

Then the seal moved, later still on 19 Aug, and that one did move pixels. SEAL_CX / SEAL_CY went from (692, 594) to (823, 739), so the seal straddles the plate's lower-right corner instead of sitting inset inside it, and the seal group left the plateclip group because inside it no centre anywhere could have broken the outline. That was liability (2) below and it is the edit the previous version of this sheet named as the next commission. Every score and every measurement recorded before the move is kept below as the before figure, and the three shipped rasters were re-exported from the new master and verified to reproduce byte-for-byte from it. Two rows improved, one row got worse, and the shelf collision this sheet also records did not move at all.

10 / 12loses 7, 10 A porcelain value-plate 680×536 standing above a porcelain ground, carrying three neutral marks — a thin label rule, one heavy figure bar, one thin footnote rule — with a twelve-lobed vermilion seal seated proud in a countersunk socket at its lower right. What it loses: #7, because the plate separates from its field by a hairline and a shadow rather than by value (1.16:1 from the constants, 1.07:1 measured); and #10, because that same relationship means the object dissolves the moment the render is reduced to luminance — captured this time rather than reasoned, in the grayscale strip below. Salvage worth taking into a master: the seal crossing the plate edge, taken on 19 Aug, and the arris radius on the socket wall, still outstanding.
C2 · icon-engineC-64e349-2.pngGPT Image 2 raster, same four references, same prompt
8 / 12loses 3, 7, 9, 10 — and 3 is non-negotiable The same object on an invented ground: a ceramic wall of grouted tiles that nothing in the brief asked for. It loses the same four checks for the same reasons and is worse inside each of them. #7 is 1.09:1, the weakest of the four. #9 fails twice over, because a grouted wall is a literal scene rather than the era's language, and because the seal casts nothing onto it, so the object hangs on the wall. And the grout is not merely decoration: it puts a second competing rectilinear structure in the tile, visible in the silhouette strip below as cross-lines that a luminance threshold cannot tell from the object, and it lifts the 16px noise floor — 0.194 against C1's 0.225 with a duller mark set. A useful negative result about Engine C: four same-register references transferred the material and the light faithfully and did not constrain the ground at all.

  • #2 is a note on three of the four and a deduction on none, but the shipping take's number moved and it moved the wrong way. A1 and C2 put 66.4% and 62% of tile width into the plate, C1 71%. The shipping take's plate is still 66.4%, and it was the whole object until the seal moved; now the seal reaches x=927 in the silhouette, so the object spans 756 of 1024 px, or 73.8%, against the catalogue's 55–65% composition constant. Its right margin is 97px where the plate's is 172px, which is still inside the tile's safe zone and is 44% less of it. Optical centring is unchanged and still true of the plate: its centre sits at y=500 against the tile's 512, a deliberate 12px lift, with identical 172px left and right margins — but that symmetry is now a property of the plate rather than of the object, and the object is what a viewer sees.
  • #3 was a weak pass on both hand-authored takes; it is now a real pass on the shipping take and still weak on A1. The silhouette strip below is the test — every authored fill and stroke forced to black with the ground switched off. A1 still comes back as a plain landscape rounded rectangle, because at r=112 centred on (692, 594) inside a plate whose edges are x=852 and y=768 the seal is inset by roughly 28px and never breaks the outline. The shipping take at (823, 739) does break it, and the numbers say by how much: the blackened mass now puts 21,163px outside the plate's rounded rectangle against 3,058 before, its bounding box runs to x=927 and y=843 against the plate's 852 and 768, and its IoU against that rounded rectangle falls from 0.9916 to 0.9447 — 0.9916 being the honest way to say "the silhouette was the rectangle, and the 3,058px were its outline stroke". An earlier version of this sheet claimed “a lobed disc breaking its lower-right corner” and withdrew the claim because the render said otherwise. The claim is now true of the shipping take, and the render below shows it rather than asserting it. C1 was the only take in the set that got the seal into its silhouette, and it was the fix to steal; it has been stolen.
  • #3 fails on both rasters for a structural reason, not a stylistic one. Their slab is a rounded square inset in a rounded square, so the tile's own silhouette is restated inside itself and the filled shape names nothing. A1 clears the same check only because its plate's 1.27 aspect distinguishes it from the tile it sits in, which is why its pass is a weak one; the shipping take now clears it on the seal as well as the aspect.
  • #7's failures are all on the object, never on the marks. A1's figure bar reads 6.85:1 against its plate and its seal body 3.33:1, both clear; it is the plate against the ground that measures 1.16:1. The shipping take inverts exactly that relationship, and the seal move gave its seal a second ground. Its inlays are 9.12:1 and 7.23:1 against the glaze, and its seal is the one number near the floor. Re-measured after the move, on the render rather than from the constants: the on-plate half of the wax reads 2.82:1 against the lit porcelain collar it is seated on, where before the move the visible collar was the whole ring including its shadowed inner edge and the same measurement read 2.67:1; and the 53.9% of the seal that now hangs off the plate reads 3.01:1 against the porcelain ground it lands on. From the constants the same two pairings are 3.19:1 against the collar and 2.88:1 against the ground's mid stop, with 2.86:1 against the glaze the seal never touches. So the overhang clears 3:1 measured and does not clear it from the constants, which it manages only because the overhanging half sits on the dark end of the wax ramp. All of these are load-bearing margins rather than comfortable ones, and the move did not change that. It changed which surface is the worst case.
  • #10 is a capture rather than a reasoned read, it does not go one way, and the seal move left every number in it untouched — which was checked rather than assumed. The sample boxes sit on the plate's lower-left quadrant and in the tile's left margin, both clear of the seal at either centre, so the #7 render figure and all three #10 figures reproduced to four decimal places after the move. Under grayscale and Tinted — which preserve luminance and discard hue, so whatever survives one survives the other — A1, C1 and C2 all collapse to about 1.08:1 and the shipping take holds at 9.08:1. Under Clear, where the ground layer is removed and the system supplies the field, the result reverses: A1's porcelain plate reads 15.40:1 on a dark desktop and 1.03:1 on a light one, the shipping take's graphite plate 1.59:1 on dark and 9.43:1 on light. Neither is unconditionally robust. The shipping take takes the point because Tinted is the named failure mode that 76% of the corpus loses and Clear-on-dark is not, and because A1 fails Default as well as Tinted while the shipping take only fails Clear-on-dark. Both rasters fail structurally: a flat single-layer raster has no ground layer to remove, so the Clear read cannot even be attempted.
  • #11 was a category read rather than a missing device, and the seal move is what changed it. The seal in a visible countersink is present in all four takes and it is distinct from its nearest sibling — clarify's device is three option cards with one drawn out of the stack, a vermilion selection dot and a margin note, checked in plugins/clarify/assets/build_icon.py, and carries no seal at all. What the shipping take used to lose was what the object is: dark landscape card, pale bars, disc at the right. With the seal straddling the corner and 53.9% of it hanging off the plate, that reading is broken by the object itself, because a payment card does not carry something notarised over its edge. The certificate reading A1 had is now available on the graphite plate, which is what the deduction was asking for, so the row is scored a pass. What survives of the card read, and it is a size effect worth stating: the overhang is 89px at 1024, which is 1.4px at 16. At 48px and up the seal visibly breaks the outline; at 16px it does not, and the tile there is still a dark landscape plate with pale bars and a warm dot near one corner. The row is scored on the object's identity at reading sizes, and #4 is the check that covers 16px.
  • Not measured, and therefore not claimed. The material was never scored: torch is absent on this machine, and the fidelity gate refuses a tier where the material metric cannot run rather than guessing. No fidelity loop ran and no blind judge panel saw any of these four, so every verdict here is one reader's, read off the renders on this page.

Rubric #3, the silhouette test

The two SVG takes are the real test: every authored fill and stroke forced to black with the ground switched off. The two rasters have no layer to isolate, so their figure is segmented by luminance at the midpoint between slab and ground — which is only possible at all because a threshold does not need the 3:1 that a human eye does, and the ragged edges and surviving grout are the same 1.08:1 failure seen from the other side.

Recommendation: ship the inlaid take (icon-src.svg), 12 / 12 with the seal moved out to the plate's corner — and record plainly that the take itself was the user's decision rather than a measurement. The user chose the inlaid take on 19 Aug 2026 against a recommendation to keep the porcelain master. The recommendation weighed #11 — A1's sealed-certificate read, which is the semantically correct one for a skill about a measured value carrying its provenance — above the two measured checks the inlaid take wins, and it lost. The seal move made later the same day is what settles that argument rather than trading it: the certificate read is now available on the graphite plate, so #11 no longer has to be spent to keep #7 and #10.

On the numbers the shipping take is the better marketplace tile. It closes both of A1's measured failures with margin (#7 from 1.16:1 to 8.24:1, #10 from 1.07:1 to 9.08:1 in grayscale) and it carries 0.333 of luminance spread at 16px where A1 carries 0.157 — below the family median, and the lowest of the four takes, at exactly the size a Finder row and a plugin tile are read. A1's identity is a hue relationship at 1.07:1; the shipping take's is a value relationship, and a value relationship is what survives Tinted.

What the seal move closed, and what it did not. Closed: the silhouette. Filled solid black with the ground off, the outline is now a landscape rounded rectangle with a twelve-lobed disc breaking its lower-right corner: 21,163px outside the plate's rounded rectangle against 3,058 before, IoU 0.9447 against 0.9916, and a bounding box that runs 75px past the plate in both axes. Closed: the card read. A payment card does not carry a wax seal over its edge, and 53.9% of this one hangs off the plate. Not closed, and it was the reason the move was commissioned: the shelf collision. shelf_check.py puts mac-design-digest against tui-craft at 0.912 after the move against 0.913 before it, still the highest of all 703 pairs in the 38-icon set, still above the 0.80 flag, and still the only undecided pair above it. Fifty-odd seal centres were measured across the whole lower-right quadrant of the tile and the best any of them reached was 0.901, at centres that put 76% to 90% of the seal off the plate and destroy the countersink relationship entirely. The metric is a 16x16 mean-centred luminance correlation, and at that resolution the seal is a handful of pixels against a graphite plate covering two-thirds of the tile: the number is about the mass and its ground, not about where the accent sits. It has not been added to the script's DECIDED list, because recording a real collision as a decision would be a false statement. Fixing it means changing the mass, which is a different commission from this one and needs a person to choose between redrawing this icon and replacing the measure.

Known liabilities on the shipping take, in the order a revision should take them. (1) The shelf collision with tui-craft at 0.912, above, and it is now the only open defect the rubric cannot see. Both are a dark landscape rounded rectangle on porcelain, centred, carrying pale horizontal bars and one vermilion mark below and right of centre. The prediction this sheet made before the take swap was a collision with proctor, on the grounds of a shared graphite-on-porcelain register, and the measurement does not support it: proctor is not in this take's top twelve matches at all, because proctor's mass is a cube on a bench and this one's is a landscape plate, and correlation follows where the mass sits rather than what value it is. (2) #2 got worse, and it is the cost of the fix. The object now spans 73.8% of tile width against 66.4% before and a 55–65% composition constant, with a 97px right margin where the plate's is 172px. Nothing is clipped and the tile's safe zone holds, but the tile is fuller than the catalogue asks for. (3) The seal's margins are still load-bearing. 2.82:1 against the lit collar measured, 3.01:1 against the porcelain ground, 3.19:1 and 2.88:1 respectively from the constants. Widen the collar or lift the wax ramp one step and none of them is a near miss any more. (4) At 16px the fix is only 1.4px wide. The 89px overhang scales to 1.4px at the size a Finder row and a marketplace tile are actually read, so the silhouette break that carries #3 and #11 is a reading-size property rather than a thumbnail one. #4 covers 16px on its own terms and passes at 0.333, but the object's identity at 16px is unchanged by this edit. (5) Engine B never ran — Arrow refused on the gateway credit balance — so this set has no independent vector take, and the widened Engine A is the skill's named substitute rather than an equivalent. (6) The material was never scored, torch being absent, and no blind panel judged any of it.

The named next step. The previous version of this sheet named the seal move, and that is done. What is left is the mass: the shelf number will not come down while this icon and tui-craft are both a dark landscape rounded rectangle centred on porcelain. The option not represented by any take here is A1's porcelain plate with the dark mass put inside it, as a graphite well holding the bright figure bar — which buys grayscale survival without inverting the object, and puts the mass somewhere no sibling puts it. The other salvage C1 earned and this take still has not taken is the arris radius on the socket wall.
