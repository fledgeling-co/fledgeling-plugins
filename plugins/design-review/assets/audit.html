A · icon.svghand-authored layered SVG, C2 glass overlay rebuilt in; SHIPS 11 / 12 Porcelain cushion tile carrying two planes of glass: a deeper slate slab behind, and a poured milky frost slab in front that is defined by its edge and its shadow rather than by its fill, so the rear plane reads through it as a darker step. Held inside the frost is a header rail and two columns, the right one sitting 44px low and 12px right of the rail and running past the margin, with a ghost of its true position still showing. The vermilion registration mark is set down on the break. Checks 1–4 pass on the real small renders: glyph box 650×536 optically centred at 512,512 with 187px side margins, and the mark still names itself at 16px after the crosshair detail has gone. Deduction is #3: taking C2's glass-overlay material brings back some of the stacked-documents read that the single-plate version had removed, held off only by the mark breaking the lower-right corner. Layers #bg/#mid/#fg/#highlight intact.
C2 · icon-c2.pngGPT Image 2 raster, corpus-referenced; material winner 9 / 12 The best material of the set by a distance: genuinely poured frosted glass, a rear slab glowing through the front one, real ground bounce in the white. It fails #1 twice over, baking both a corner radius and a drop shadow into the raster, which is why re-masking it leaves a halo. #10 fails by construction, since a flat raster has no layers to survive tinting. #3 is the design miss: two same-size offset slabs read as duplicate documents, not as one surface being checked. Its material was rebuilt into A rather than shipped, per the pipeline rule.
C1 · icon-c1.pngGPT Image 2 raster, three-column variant 8 / 12 Strongest figure-ground in the set, deep slate on near-white, and the three-column interior is the most legible layout of any take. Loses the same #1 and #10 as C2, the same stacked-cards #3, and additionally #11: every column sits true, so nothing in the artwork names what this skill actually does. A target on a tidy layout is a category glyph. The columns being wrong is the whole subject, and that is the lesson A took from it.
B · icon-engineB-arrow.svgArrow 1.1 vector take 6 / 12 The miss of the set. It baked its own rounded-corner tile and border, so under the system squircle it shows a second edge (#1 fail). The slabs grew header tabs and read as stacked folders (#3 fail). Composition sits high, leaving a bottom margin roughly twice the top (#2), the flat fills belong to a previous era (#9), and nothing in it is owned (#11). Salvaged into the master: nothing beyond confirming that same-size offset slabs read as a file stack, which pushed A toward one plate with its drift held inside. Kept on the sheet because a variation set that hides its losers is not an audit.
Recommendation: ship A (icon.svg). It is the only take that is Icon-Composer-ready as four real layers, clean on the non-negotiable checks 1–4 against masked small renders rather than against the 1024 artwork, and the only one whose artwork states the subject: a column off its shared rail, with the mark set on the break. C2 won the material comparison and that material has now been rebuilt into A as real layers, so the two read as one family. Known liabilities to revisit: two offset slabs carry some duplicate-documents read, and only the mark breaking the lower-right corner keeps the silhouette from being a plain stack; the frost slab sits close in value to the porcelain along its lower left, so at 16px the surface becomes a soft mass and the mark carries the identity alone; the ring-and-crosshair detail inside the disc is fully lost by 16px, leaving a circled mark rather than a registration mark; and the light Tinted variant is the weakest of the four, since flattening the vermilion to a system tint costs the one hue that separates instrument from surface.
Rubric: the 12-point icon audit from icon-directions.md (mask discipline, grid, silhouette, 16px identity, single light model, palette discipline, figure-ground, depth coherence, focal sizing, variant robustness, texture restraint, era fit); checks 1–4 non-negotiable, delivery bar ≥10/12. Every take, raster and vector alike, was clipped to a superellipse squircle before rendering, so takes that baked their own mask are judged on what the system would actually show. Render commands: `rsvg-convert -w {1024,256,64,32}` after squircle clipping.
