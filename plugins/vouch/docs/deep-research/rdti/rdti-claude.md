## Executive Summary

- **(High Confidence)** There is no list of eligible expense *types* in the R&DTI. Section 355-205 allows a notional deduction "to the extent that the expenditure ... is incurred on one or more R&D activities" that are registered [ITAA 1997 s 355-205(1)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-205&PiT=99991231235958). **No vendor on your schedule is inherently TRUE or FALSE.** The TRUE/FALSE column is a property of the link between that line and a specific registered activity, not of the supplier. A schedule that classifies by vendor name will not survive review. `<ENACTED>`

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Which software and SaaS operating expenses qualify?

The answer is structural rather than enumerative, and the structure has to be understood before any line can be marked.

**Two independent gates.** Eligibility is decided in two places by two agencies. AusIndustry / DISR (for Industry Innovation and Science Australia) decides whether an *activity* is a core or supporting R&D activity. The ATO decides whether *expenditure* is notionally deductible against those activities. The ATO is explicit that registration is not verification: "Registration of activities by DISR is not a verification of eligibility or an approval of the payment of a tax offset" [ATO, "Checklist for claiming R&D tax incentive"](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/helping-you-get-r-d-claims-right/checklist-for-claiming-r-d-tax-incentive). `<GUIDANCE>`

Since *GQHC and Commissioner of Taxation* [2024] AATA 409, the ATO can itself decide activity eligibility where the Board has made no binding Finding. The Tribunal held that findings by the Board "are clearly a pre-condition to the Commissioner being bound", and that absent them "the Commissioner has the power to assess or make decisions as to whether [the] Registered Activities consisted of eligible R&D activities as defined in Division 355" [ATO Decision Impact Statement, GQHC, published 12 April 2024](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/view.htm?docid=%22LIT%2FICD%2F2020%2F0826%2F00001%22). **(High Confidence)** `<ENACTED>` (judicial interpretation). Practically: a registration number does not insulate the expenditure schedule from an ATO activity-level challenge.

**The expenditure test.** Section 355-205(1) permits deduction of expenditure incurred during the year "to the extent that the expenditure ... is incurred on one or more R&D activities" that are (i) registered under s 27A of the IR&D Act and (ii) satisfy s 355-210 [ITAA 1997 s 355-205(1)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-205&PiT=99991231235958). Two operative phrases: *"to the extent that"* (mandating apportionment) and *"incurred on"* (mandating nexus). `<ENACTED>`

**The practical per-line test.** <INFERENCE from="s 355-205(1) 'to the extent that ... incurred on'; ATO 'would be incurred regardless of the R&D activities'; AusIndustry dominant-purpose criteria including 'whether your activity goes beyond activities you would have conducted if you did not conduct your core R&D activities'">For each of the 79 lines, the accountant should answer four questions in order, and the explanation column should record the answers: (1) Which *registered* core activity does this support? (2) Would this subscription exist, at this cost, if that core activity had not been conducted? (3) If it would exist but at lower cost or scope, what is the incremental or apportioned share, and on what measured basis? (4) What contemporaneous record ties this invoice to that activity? A line that cannot answer (1) is FALSE. A line that answers "yes, unchanged" to (2) is FALSE. A line that answers (3) without a measurement is unsupported, not merely uncertain.</INFERENCE>

### 2. Core vs supporting R&D activities under Division 355, and the dominant purpose test

**Core R&D activities (s 355-25(1))** are experimental activities:

> "(a) whose outcome cannot be known or determined in advance on the basis of current knowledge, information or experience, but can only be determined by applying a systematic progression of work that: (i) is based on principles of established science; and (ii) proceeds from hypothesis to experiment, observation and evaluation, and leads to logical conclusions; and (b) that are conducted for the purpose of generating new knowledge (including new knowledge in the form of new or improved materials, products, devices, processes or services)."

[ITAA 1997 s 355-25(1)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-25&PiT=99991231235958) **(High Confidence)** `<ENACTED>`

**Supporting R&D activities (s 355-30)** are "activities directly related to core R&D activities" (subs (1)). Subsection (2) imposes the additional gate:

> "However, if an activity: (a) is an activity referred to in subsection 355-25(2); or (b) produces goods or services; or (c) is directly related to producing goods or services; the activity is a supporting R&D activity only if it is undertaken for the dominant purpose of supporting core R&D activities."

[ITAA 1997 s 355-30(2)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-30&PiT=99991231235958) **(High Confidence)** `<ENACTED>`

**Dominant purpose, as administered.** AusIndustry defines it as "your prevailing or most influential purpose", and gives assessment criteria that map unusually well onto subscription spend. Activities *likely* to satisfy it "have no purpose other than to assist or facilitate a core R&D activity", or are ones where "the commercial benefit is insignificant". Activities *unlikely* to satisfy it are those that "produce a good or service that you do not use to conduct an experiment" or that "you would undertake if you did not conduct R&D". The decisive question offered is "whether your activity goes beyond activities you would have conducted if you did not conduct your core R&D activities" [AusIndustry, "Conducting supporting R&D activities for the R&DTI"](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/check-if-you-are-eligible-for-the-randd-tax-incentive/conducting-supporting-rd-activities-for-the-rdti). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

The consequential sentence for this claim is AusIndustry's statement that the goods-and-services limb captures inbound services: services "another entity provides to your business" [ibid.](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/check-if-you-are-eligible-for-the-randd-tax-incentive/conducting-supporting-rd-activities-for-the-rdti). A SaaS subscription is exactly that.

<CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>The dominant purpose test in s 355-30(2) is drafted as a test on *activities*, not on expenditure. AusIndustry's application of it to received services implies that "using service X" is itself the activity to be characterised. An alternative reading — common in adviser practice — treats the tool as a mere input to an activity already characterised elsewhere, so that only the s 355-205 "to the extent incurred on" test applies and dominant purpose never engages. The two readings converge in most cases because both ask what the spend is really for, but they diverge for a tool that is genuinely dual-use: the first reading can disqualify the line entirely, the second only apportions it. Positions: AusIndustry guidance (above) vs. no published contrary primary authority. Nature of the disagreement: the statutory unit of analysis.</CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE> **(Medium Confidence)**

**The software-specific exclusion.** Section 355-25(2)(h) excludes from *core* activities "developing, modifying or customising computer software for the dominant purpose of use by [the developer, a connected entity, or an affiliate] for their internal administration (including the internal administration of their business functions)" [ITAA 1997 s 355-25(2)(h)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-25&PiT=99991231235958). AusIndustry defines internal administration software as "payroll and accounting, invoicing, ordering, quality control reports and information management", "management information systems" and "enterprise resource planning" [AusIndustry, "Excluded R&D activities under the R&DTI"](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/check-if-you-are-eligible-for-the-randd-tax-incentive/excluded-rd-activities-under-the-rdti). For a company building software *for customers*, this exclusion generally does not bite on the product itself — AusIndustry's own example confirms software sold to other businesses is outside the exclusion. It does bite on internal tooling. `<ENACTED>` + `<GUIDANCE>`

**What AusIndustry says is routine in software.** The sector guide lists as generally ineligible for core status: "applying documented configuration options with known outcomes"; "integrating third party services or application programming interfaces by following vendor documentation"; "assembling user interfaces using known user interface components"; "building a dashboard ... by implementing established design and development patterns"; and "migrating data between systems using known tools" [AusIndustry, "Software development sector guide for the R&D Tax Incentive"](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/sector-guides-for-r-and-d-tax-incentive-applicants/software-development). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

The AI guidance goes further and names as unlikely-to-be-core: "implementing logging, alerts, dashboards, or routine performance checks using established methods and tools to confirm expected model behaviour"; "adjusting or fine-tuning parameters (e.g. learning rate) using established methods when it is known that the issue can be solved by parameter changes"; and "integrating known model outputs ... into applications or dashboards, using pre-defined logic, rules and interfaces" [AusIndustry, "Artificial intelligence-related activities and the R&DTI"](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/sector-guides-for-r-and-d-tax-incentive-applicants/software-development/artificial-intelligence-related-activities-and-the-rd-tax-incentive). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>` This is directly adverse to a naive Sentry/Axiom claim, and is dealt with in §4 below.

### 3. The "directly related" / nexus test applied to recurring subscriptions and cloud services

Neither the ATO nor AusIndustry has published guidance addressing SaaS subscriptions as a category. What exists is guidance on overheads, which is the closest analogue, plus TA 2017/5's specific criticisms of software claims.

**The ATO's positive statement.** Administrative costs and overheads — the ATO's examples are rent or lease payments, light and power, rates, cleaning, certain insurance — "may be incurred on R&D activities if there is a direct link between the R&D activities and the expenditure incurred" [ATO, "Keeping records and calculating your notional deductions", updated 23 March 2023](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/keeping-records-and-calculating-your-notional-deductions). So an overhead is not excluded in principle. **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

**The ATO's negative statement**, in the same paragraph and repeated on the exclusions page: "Ineligible expenses are those without sufficient link to the R&D activities, particularly if they relate to general company operations or marketing expenditure that would be incurred regardless of the R&D activities" [ATO, "Expenditure you can't claim", updated 13 May 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/amounts-you-can-claim/expenditure-you-cant-claim). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

The "incurred regardless" counterfactual is the workhorse. It is not a materiality test and not a proportionality test — it is a but-for test on the existence of the expenditure.

**TA 2017/5 on expenditure.** The Alert records four observed expenditure errors relevant here: that "expenditure incurred in acquiring, or in acquiring the right to use, technology cannot be claimed as a notional deduction"; that claims include amounts "not ... incurred on one or more eligible R&D activities; for example production costs of software sold to the market in the ordinary course of business"; that "expenditure is included as part of overall overhead expenses which does not relate to R&D activities"; and that "overhead expenses are apportioned using a method that allocates an unreasonably large amount to R&D" [TA 2017/5, "Claiming the R&D Tax Incentive for software development activities", 20 February 2017, amended 19 January 2024](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TPA%2FTA20175%2FNAT%2FATO). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

The second of those — production costs of software sold in the ordinary course of business — is the direct authority for splitting cloud and API spend between experimental and production workloads. Production hosting and production inference are the cost of running the commercial product, not the cost of the experiment.

### 5. Apportionment where a tool serves both R&D and ordinary business use

The ATO's guidance here is unusually specific and should be quoted into the schedule's methodology note.

**The governing principle.** "The words 'to the extent that' mean that a method of apportionment may be required if you cannot specifically identify the value of expenditure incurred on R&D versus non-R&D activities. There are many ways and methods you can use ... It is up to you to determine the most reasonable basis with the information you have at the time." [ATO, "Keeping records and calculating your notional deductions"](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/keeping-records-and-calculating-your-notional-deductions) **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

**No single method across types.** "It is not accepted that one apportionment method or formula will be suitable for all expenditure types you may have. You need to consider each expenditure type separately" [ibid.](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/keeping-records-and-calculating-your-notional-deductions). A flat percentage applied across all 79 lines is therefore non-compliant on its face, and is the exact pattern TA 2017/5 describes. **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

**Direct tracking is preferred over any formula.** "Preferably, your accounting and record keeping processes should enable the tracking of expenditure to your R&D activities on a 'real time' basis, so apportionment methodologies are only used in limited situations" [ibid.](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/keeping-records-and-calculating-your-notional-deductions). `<GUIDANCE>`

**Named methods, with the ATO's own reasoning:**

| Basis | ATO position | Fit for subscriptions |
|---|---|---|
| Direct attribution (separate account/project/instance) | Preferred; removes the need to apportion | Best available. Separate cloud projects, Sentry projects, GitHub repos, API keys per workload. |
| Employee hours on R&D ÷ total employee hours | Expressly endorsed for expenditure "incurred over a period of use (for example, utilities such as electricity, decline in value of R&D assets)" | The correct fallback for per-seat developer tools and AI assistants. Requires timesheets: "If you use project hours as a basis of apportionment for other expenditure types, you may need to retain timesheets for this purpose, even if you do not need them to help support the salary component of your claim." |
| Area of use | Endorsed for rates, land taxes, rent, lease costs | Not applicable. |
| R&D salary ÷ total salary | Only reasonable "where the expenditure is based on how much an employee earns (for example, payments of super, worker's compensation and payroll taxes)" | **Not appropriate for subscriptions.** The ATO rejects dollar-value bases for period-of-use spend because "employees within a company are unlikely to be remunerated consistently" and a dollar value "is unlikely to be representative of time spent." |
| R&D decline-in-value ÷ total decline-in-value | Expressly rejected as a general allocator | Not applicable. |

### 7. Record-keeping and substantiation

**Contemporaneity is a requirement, not a preference.** AusIndustry: "Records should be created at the time the activity is conducted." **Software-specific records AusIndustry accepts** — this list is the most useful single artefact for a software claimant, and it is unusually accommodating of ordinary engineering practice. For the unknown-outcome limb: "digital boards, workshop notes, screenshots of questions on technical forums, saved Q&A threads, issue-tracking systems, emails or screenshots of chat messages or dashboards, technical reports, research or meeting notes, and comparison tables or correspondence with experts". **The consequence of not having them.** In *Royal Wins Pty Ltd and Innovation and Science Australia* [2020] AATA 4320, the Tribunal affirmed that gaming-platform software activities were not core R&D activities, on the basis that there was no contemporaneous documentation establishing that a hypothesis had been formulated or tested; the reported reasoning records "a threshold difficulty for the Applicant is the absence of documentation or records" [Royal Wins Pty Ltd and Innovation and Science Australia 2020 AATA 4320](http://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/cases/cth/AATA/2020/4320.html). **(Medium Confidence — secondary-sourced)** `<ENACTED>` (judicial). **A first-order issue specific to this fact pattern.** The claim describes subscriptions "paid personally and reimbursed by" the company. ### 9. Overseas suppliers, the Overseas Finding, and where cloud sits

**The statutory condition is about the activity's location, not the supplier's.** Section 355-210(1) applies to an R&D activity where "(a) the R&D activity is conducted for the R&D entity solely within Australia", or, under (d), where it "is conducted for the R&D entity solely outside Australia" **and** "covered by a finding in force under paragraph 28C(1)(a) of the Industry Research and Development Act 1986" [ITAA 1997 s 355-210(1)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-210&PiT=99991231235958). **(High Confidence)** `<ENACTED>` Neither the supplier's residence, the data centre's location, nor the billing currency appears in the test.

**AusIndustry's gloss.** "A core R&D activity is conducted solely in Australia when **every part of its systematic progression of work is carried out in Australia**" [AusIndustry, "Apply for an overseas finding"](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/apply-for-an-overseas-finding). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

**The four conditions for an Overseas Finding**, which show how far removed this regime is from ordinary cloud procurement: the overseas activity must itself be a core or supporting R&D activity; there must be an Australian core activity that cannot be completed without it; the overseas activity must be incapable of being conducted solely in Australia for one of four listed reasons (access to a facility, expertise or equipment not available in Australia; contravention of the Biosecurity Act or quarantine law; access to a population of living things not available in Australia; access to geographical or geological features not available in Australia); and overseas expenditure must be less than the related Australian expenditure. AusIndustry adds: "**Financial reasons are insufficient**" [ibid.](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/apply-for-an-overseas-finding) **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>` + `<ENACTED>`

**The deadline is absolute.** "You must submit your overseas finding application before the end of the income year in which you conduct, or plan to conduct, your overseas R&D activities. The department **cannot accept late applications or grant extensions of time for overseas findings under any circumstances**" [ibid.](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/apply-for-an-overseas-finding) Average processing time 90 days. **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

<INFERENCE from="s 355-210(1)(a)'s focus on where the activity is conducted; AusIndustry's 'every part of its systematic progression of work is carried out in Australia'; the four allowable overseas reasons, all of which concern physical access to facilities, populations or geography rather than procurement">Where the hypothesis is formulated, the experiment designed and orchestrated, and the results observed and evaluated by people in Australia, the systematic progression of work is carried out in Australia. Remote infrastructure supplied by a non-resident vendor is an instrument used in that work, in the same way a leased instrument is, and does not convert the activity into one conducted outside Australia. On that reading, no Overseas Finding is required for AWS, Azure, Vercel, Fly.io, GitHub or LLM API spend, and the fact that these vendors are non-resident is irrelevant to eligibility.</INFERENCE> **(Medium Confidence)**

Why: no ATO ruling, AusIndustry guidance, or decided case addresses the location of an experiment executed on remote infrastructure. ### 10. GST treatment of notional deductions

**The rule.** "You cannot deduct under this Act a loss or outgoing you incur, to the extent that the loss or outgoing includes an amount relating to an input tax credit to which you are entitled or a decreasing adjustment that you have" [ITAA 1997 s 27-5](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F27-5&PiT=99991231235958). **(High Confidence)** `<ENACTED>`

**It applies to notional deductions.** The ATO lists Division 27 among the "limiting provisions" applied when working out notional deductions: "Although amounts treated as notional deductions are not actually deducted in calculating your taxable income, they are treated as a deduction when applying: a provision that prevents some or all of an amount being deducted ... Division 27 of the ITAA 1997, which is about the effect of input tax credits on deductions" [ATO, "Your notional deduction", updated 13 May 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/amounts-you-can-claim/your-notional-deduction). The claiming checklist repeats it: "If you are registered for GST, you will also need to take into account Division 27 of the ITAA 1997 when working out your R&D notional deductions" [ATO, "Checklist for claiming R&D tax incentive"](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/helping-you-get-r-d-claims-right/checklist-for-claiming-r-d-tax-incentive). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

**Answer:** for a GST-registered entity, notional deductions are **GST-exclusive to the extent an input tax credit is available**. The operative words are "to the extent that ... you are entitled" — the reduction tracks entitlement to the credit, not the presence of GST on the invoice.

<INFERENCE from="s 27-5's 'to the extent that ... an input tax credit to which you are entitled'; the fact that the named suppliers are predominantly non-resident">Three cases arise across the 79 lines and they should be handled separately, not by a blanket GST treatment: (a) supplier charges Australian GST and the entity holds a valid tax invoice in its own name — claim the GST-exclusive amount; (b) supplier charges no Australian GST (common for non-resident B2B supplies to a GST-registered recipient that has provided its ABN) — no ITC entitlement, so the full amount is the notional deduction; (c) supplier charges GST but the tax invoice is in an individual's name because the subscription was paid personally — ITC entitlement is doubtful, which in isolation *increases* the notional deduction but signals the deeper "who incurred it" problem in §7 above. Case (c) should not be quietly treated as case (b).</INFERENCE> **(Medium Confidence)**

### 11. Review and audit triggers, and where software claims have been denied

**The activity-side patterns that have been found ineligible**, per TA 2017/5: whole-of-project registration "without distinguishing eligible R&D activities from ineligible activities"; absence of a purpose of generating new knowledge; no hypothesis or experiment; "project management, commercial or economic risks ... mistaken for technical risks"; purchase and modification of off-the-shelf software; and supporting activities that "are not directly related to experimental activities or do not have a dominant purpose of supporting such activities" [ibid.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TPA%2FTA20175%2FNAT%2FATO) `<GUIDANCE>`

**Routine testing.** The TA 2017/5 addendum clarifies that a test which "does not set out to test causal relationships between technical variables to experimentally resolve an outcome that cannot be determined with existing knowledge or expertise" is not a core activity — the example given is "testing websites in operation by measuring the number of hits". The Alert lists bug testing, beta testing, system testing, requirements testing, UAT, data mapping and migration testing, and "testing the efficiency of different algorithms that are already known to work" as ineligible unless done as hypothesis-testing experiments, "however, [they] may be eligible as supporting R&D activities if they are directly related to core R&D activities" [TA 2017/5A Addendum, effective 20 February 2017](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TPA%2FTA20175A%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001). **(High Confidence)** `<GUIDANCE>`

### What is the current state, and the strongest supporting evidence for it?

The regime is stable, well-documented at the activity level, and under-specified at the expenditure level for modern software inputs. The statutory architecture has not changed materially since the 2020 rate reforms took effect for income years commencing 1 July 2021. The strongest evidence for the current state is the primary text of ss 355-25, 355-30, 355-100, 355-205, 355-210 and 355-225 (all quoted above from the ATO's own legislative database, current compilations), corroborated by ATO web guidance last updated 13 May 2026 and AusIndustry sector guidance that has been recently rewritten to address AI.

The notable feature is asymmetry of specificity. AusIndustry has produced detailed, current, engineering-literate guidance on *activities* — the software sector guide names agile methodologies, spike tickets, architecture decision records and dashboard screenshots. Neither agency has produced anything comparable on *expenditure*: the ATO's worked examples of claimable overheads remain "rent or lease payments, light and power, property rates and taxes, cleaning, certain types of insurance" [ATO, "Expenditure you can claim", updated 13 May 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive-and-concessions/research-and-development-tax-incentive/in-detail/amounts-you-can-claim/expenditure-you-can-claim), a list that predates the subscription economy entirely. **(High Confidence)**

**Enacted and affecting FY2026.** Act No 58 of 2026 inserted s 355-25(2)(i) and (j) (gambling and tobacco/vaping exclusions) and s 355-30(3)–(4), "effective 1 July 2026 and applicable in relation to income years starting on or after 1 July 2025" [ITAA 1997 s 355-25, history notes](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC%2F19970038%2F355-25&PiT=99991231235958). `<ENACTED>` **(High Confidence)**

**Announced, not law.** On 12 May 2026, as part of the 2026-27 Budget, the Government announced seven changes, all commencing **1 July 2028**: removing eligibility of supporting R&D activities; increasing the core offset rate by 4.5 percentage points; lowering the intensity premium threshold from 2% to 1.5%; restricting the refundable offset to a company's first 10 years of operation; raising the minimum expenditure threshold from $20,000 to $50,000; raising the refundable turnover threshold from $20 million to $50 million; and raising the maximum expenditure threshold from $150 million to $200 million.
