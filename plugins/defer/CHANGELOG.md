# Changelog

## 1.5.0 - 2026-09-09

GPT-6 Astra becomes the OpenAI-family spine at three thinking levels, and the thinking level stops being a dial on top of the routing decision and becomes the routing decision. Owner directive: astra at **low** is level with `gpt-5.6-sol` at high, `claude-fable-5` at medium and `grok-4.6` at high; astra at **medium** and **high** is frontier capacity for the hardest problems only; `gemini-3.8-flash-high` is the fast implementation lane once a spec or plan exists; grok and GLM are trusted to orchestrate; design work stays on Opus and Fable, authored at medium where a rough plan exists.

- **The frontier tiers are reserved mechanically, not asked for politely.** `codex-astra-medium` and `codex-astra-high` carry `frontier: True`, `allowed_lanes()` filters them out of every class, and `--hard` or `--task hard` is the only route to them. Prose reserving a lane a router can still reach is a lane a router reaches the moment it runs out of cheaper options. `--hard` is refused on `design`, `design-review` and `verification` by `NO_ESCALATION`: a harder design question is still a design question, and the answer to it is not another family.
- **Two new classes.** `orchestration` (astra at low, grok at high, GLM at high) and `hard` (the two frontier tiers, with opus as the in-family fail-back). `design` splits from `design-review`, because authoring at medium and judging at medium are not the same claim — the rule against cheapening a judgement class is unchanged, and `design-review` keeps xhigh and high.
- **A third evidence class, `peer`, for a claim a directive makes and a bench has not.** Astra has no row here or on DeepSWE at any effort, so `codex-astra-low` borrows `codex/gpt-5.6-sol@high`'s under `evidence: "peer"` with `peer_of` and `peer_source` naming whose row it is and who said so. It clamps exactly as `proxy` does — never drop-in, never a hard block — and `task_cost()` refuses to read a peer's cost, because "these two lanes produce comparable work" is not "these two lanes bill alike".
- **The cost tie-break abstains rather than ranking a guess.** Neither astra nor Gemini 3.8 has a published rate read into this file; both stand in at a predecessor's, named in `PLACEHOLDER_PRICES` and marked `price_evidence: "placeholder"`. A band containing one skips the cost stage entirely and breaks on preference order, and the route's reason says which stage was skipped. Ranking a measured $0.25 against a stand-in $14.00 would have sent every route away from the lanes the directive named, on the strength of a number nobody published.
- **A delivery penalty now names the model it was measured on.** The Gemini entry's 12 points were measured on 3.7 Flash; the lane runs 3.8. `applies_to_model` plus `delivery_penalty_for()` means the subtraction stops on its own and snaps back if the lane returns — rather than a predecessor's delivery record being charged to a model nobody measured, or the finding being silently dropped. The finding survives as that lane's `route_guard`, which is the honest shape for it: a condition on the brief rather than a number taken off a score.
- **`unmeasured` stops being reported as `outranked`.** A lane the bench scored behind and a lane nobody measured are different facts, and the route now prints them on separate lines. Collapsing them was a claim about evidence that did not exist.
- **`lane_run.sh` and `lane_probe.sh` no longer carry a copy of the policy.** Both build every argv and env from the registry through `lane_pick.argv_for()`. The case statement they replaced held its own five models and drifted the first time a lane moved, silently. `lane_probe.sh` gains `--frontier`, so the routine availability check does not spend the reserved tiers.
- **Four GPT-5.6 lanes retired into `DECLINED`** — `codex-terra` at high, max and medium, and `codex-luna-max` — plus `codex-sol` at medium. Nothing was found wanting: `codex-sol-high` survives only because the directive names sol@high as a peer of astra@low. `terra@max`'s brownfield row and `luna@max`'s $0.61 a task are called out as the two to re-read first if astra ever measures short.
- **Probed 2026-09-09, with a negative control.** `gpt-6-astra` answered at `low`, `medium`, `high` and `max` through `codex exec`, each echoing the requested effort; `-m gpt-6-bogus-xyz` failed at the API with a 400 and wrote **no output file**, which is what makes the four positives evidence rather than an echo of the flags. `max` is accepted and refused anyway, by `FORBIDDEN` — the directive defines three tiers, and a fourth nobody placed would be a routing decision made by an argument parser. Two CLI facts recorded: `--skip-git-repo-check` is required outside a trusted directory, and codex 0.153.4 now warns on an unknown model before the API refuses it, which narrows the empty-`-o` check without replacing it. **`glm` probed DOWN** with `409 no-eligible-account / routing_forbidden` — a Perch routing setting, not a capability fact; it keeps its policy place and carries `probe_state`.
- **A lane that scored level with the yardstick was being refused.** `opus-design` inherits opus's bench row at a lower effort, so it grades `REF` — and `REF` is in neither the drop-in band nor the guarded one, so it fell through to *refused*: the most capable lane in its class, rejected for measuring identical to the reference. This is the file's own "REF is not a verdict" rule running backwards. A non-reference lane grading `REF` now clamps to guarded, with a check that proves it. Latent rather than live — `design` is not shape-gated — and found by reading what `--matrix` printed rather than by a test, which is the argument for printing the table at all.
- **Smaller corrections in the same pass.** `--matrix` column headings named five retired lanes and none of the new ones, so three astra lanes would have collided into one heading; a check now asserts every lane has one. `lane_budgets.json` and `usage-sources.md` still described the retired codex lanes, and the price table now carries both placeholder rows and the reason the cost stage abstains on them.
- **The selftest moves with the policy.** 49 checks, all passing. New ones assert the frontier reservation across every class, that `--hard` exits 2 on the three protected classes, that a peer row names its source and never becomes a cost, that a placeholder price is declared, that a penalty applies only to its measured subject, and that an inert penalty leaves a `route_guard` behind.

## 1.2.0 - 2026-08-22

The Gemini evidence clamp keeps its behaviour and loses its justification. Until now, the reason a proxy row could never hard-block a lane was that Gemini's worst shapes had been measured through a bash-only container scaffold, and reading that as a verdict on the `agy` lane would retire a lane on a confound. That was reasoning, not evidence. It has now been tested, and for the shape it was doing the most work on it is wrong.

- **A same-scaffold control, and it kills the scaffold explanation for `static-page`.** Hold the harness constant — same seven tasks, same current contract, same mini-in-a-container loop — and seven other models clear 62–83%: `qwen3.8-max` 82.9%, `grok-4.5` / `muse-spark` / `glm-5.2-fast` / `deepseek-v4-flash` 80%, `kimi-k3` 77.8%, `deepseek-0731` 61.8%. Gemini scores 42.9% at `high` and 35.3% at `medium`. The bash-only loop is not what makes this shape hard, and the argument that it was should not be reinstated.
- **On `brownfield-integration` the same control says the opposite, partly.** That cohort spans 20 to 65 with Gemini at the bottom, so the scaffold is doing real work *and* the model is behind. The 30-point gap to opus is part harness, part model, and this control cannot say in what proportion. The file now says that instead of implying the scaffold covers it.
- **The clamp survives on a different, stated reason.** Mini pins `temperature: 0` for every route it drives, and Google flags sub-1.0 temperature as degrading for the Gemini 3.x family specifically — a warning the control models do not inherit, so the within-scaffold comparison stays fair while the lane comparison does not. The `agy` lane and the opus reference both set no temperature at all. A vendor-flagged degradation setting on one side is not a basis for retiring a lane. `shape_grade`'s docstring and the `gemini` lane's registry comment now carry that reason rather than the disproven one.
- **`static-page` is the one shape whose failure mode is known, so its guard names it.** On those tasks 58% of Gemini's failing assertions at `medium` and 86% at `high` state a bound (`exactly N`, `no`, `not`, `only`), against 8% for opus and 6% for `sol@max`. It delivers what the brief asks for and exceeds what the brief caps — `exactly one soft elevation shadow` failed on every card and every toast in its set on a run that passed 37 of its 39 other assertions. The guard now asks for a reference input and for each produced value to be read back against every stated bound, rather than for more care. `visual-design` picks up the same clause.
- **What would actually lift the clamp is named, and it is cheap.** The seven `static-page` tasks re-run through `agy` at default sampling. Nobody has run it. Until someone does, the grade stays AMBER over a raw RED and `--matrix` keeps marking the cell.

No scores, gates, thresholds or routing decisions changed. `capability_matrix.json` is untouched; this release changes what the numbers are taken to mean.
