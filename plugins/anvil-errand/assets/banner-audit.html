Recommendation.

**Ships.** The banner restates the icon's one signature move at bench scale: a single incandescent ribbon, thin where it departs off the upper right and thick where it arrives on a hot billet, resting on a graphite ledge extruded along the icon's own oblique axis. Out and back is the whole of "errand", and it costs one stroke rather than a second object.

Wordmark is Instrument Sans at 700. That is the plurality face in this family (nine of twenty-seven banners) and the face already worn by ship-armada, ship-fleet, ship-feature and armada-sync, which are this skill's immediate neighbours in the orchestration group. An audit found twelve display faces where the standard asks for one, so the deliberate choice here was to add nothing.

Derived from `build_icon.py` rather than from a sibling: `LIGHT_DIR` (0.52, 0.80, 0.30) puts the key up-left-front, so the ground's specular sits at 34% across rather than overhead, which is where most of the family puts it. `VIEW_X` -0.34 and `VIEW_Y` -0.55 at `DEPTH` 152 give the ledge's far cap its up-and-left offset of (-28.4, -46.1) at this scale. The three ledge faces take their values from the ramp positions the icon's Lambert term lands on, in the order it measured: top plane brightest, then the left end, then the near flank. `HARDIE_U/V/S` and `PRITCHEL_U/V/R` are cut into the face plane with the same projection. `BILLET_U0..U1`, `V0..V1` and `BILLET_H` place and size the work. `ARC_W_FAR` 0.6 to `ARC_W_NEAR` 21 at `ARC_SAMPLES` 88 is the ribbon, with `HOT_CORE` reserved for the landing end and `HOT_LO` for the body exactly as the icon reserves them. `SHADOW` #4E4437 casts at its own 26/12 vector, scaled by 272/1024. Ground is `PORC_HI` / `PORC_MID` / `PORC_LO` with `VIGNETTE` as the edge fall-off.

At 400px the wordmark, the essence line and the whole device still read: the bench, the billet and the taper all survive, and the hyphen's ember is legible as a mark rather than a smudge. At 200px the wordmark holds and the arc still reads as arriving, but the essence line is gone and the billet reduces to one warm dot on a dark block. The two holes are the only thing making the ledge read as an anvil face at that size, and they are below the threshold, so a thumbnail says "hot work landing on a bench" rather than "anvil".

Known liabilities. The ribbon leaves the frame at full opacity rather than fading, because the layout's own overflow cuts it before its gradient reaches zero. That is the deliberate bleed the family already uses on better-loop, and here it reads as the path continuing to the other machine, but it is a cut rather than a designed exit. The ledge is a derived slab standing in for the anvil's face, not the anvil, so the device depends on the real icon beside it to name the tool. The essence line runs to three lines at 26px, one more than most of the set.

The arc is drawn beneath the billet so the billet occludes its last stretch. Drawn over the top it cut a bright chevron across the work, which read as an arrowhead rather than as an arrival.
