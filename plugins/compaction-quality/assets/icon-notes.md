# compaction-quality icon notes

**Direction: "Load-Bearing".** The studio's *Tahoe Gel-Glass* sub-register (a), porcelain cushion tile carrying a coloured gel object. Chosen because *compaction* is a geological word before it is a Claude Code one: strata under pressure, hundreds of layers, and a few seams that carry the record. Runner-up: sub-register (c) dark glass, rejected on two counts. The dark deep-sea register is already spoken for by the sibling plugin `trawl`, and a dark ground would have put the discarded bulk in shadow when the whole argument is that you can *see* how much got thrown away.

**Glyph (subject-mined, device bank #16 + #20 + #22):** a squat cylinder of compressed sediment, wider than it is tall, poured in dark graphite gel with rounded shoulders top and bottom. The composition performs the verb (#16): three thin discs are still shedding off the top and fading out, the wall below them carries ~40 hairline strata that tighten with depth, and the whole thing is a solid filled silhouette. Squat is the shape of compaction; the first pass was taller and lost the read entirely.

**Signature:** the five vermilion seams, one of them four times the width of the rest and glowing from inside, blooming warm light through the graphite and spilling onto the porcelain beneath. Vermilion is not decoration and not a brand colour: a user correction is the person saying *no, not like that*, and red is the mark that gets made in a margin. Five is the measured median. Risk taken: four of the five are deliberately quiet, so the icon states its own hierarchy rather than showing five equal stripes.

**Palette:** one warm-neutral family plus one accent. Porcelain ground `#FFFFFE→#F7F5F1→#E4E1DA` with an inner rim light and a soft edge vignette; graphite gel body `#5E676E→#3E464D→#262C31→#1E2429`; strata `#A8B1B8` at 9-30%; the seams `#8E2A0A→#DC5520→#FF9260` with the dominant one reaching `#FFD9BD`. One soft top light plus the sanctioned second source, the emissive interior under glass. Zero hard speculars. Bounce light off the porcelain floor separates the base from its own shadow.

**Audit: 11/12, zero failures on the non-negotiable 1-4.** Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the squircle as a *clip* (no baked corners, no baked shadow); focal 664px = 64.8% of tile, 180px side margins, solid mass centred at y=496 with the contact shadow carrying the balance below it. Check #10 passes by construction: four layers `#bg/#mid/#fg/#highlight` mapping 1:1 onto Icon Composer, identity carried by shape and value. Point deducted at #4: at 16px the five seams collapse into one warm band, so the icon keeps its identity but loses its argument at menu-bar size. Full contact sheet with every take scored, losers included: `audit.html`.

**Register note:** dark-on-light figure-ground makes the macOS Dark variant the weakest of the four, carried by silhouette plus the seam alone; and the porcelain ground sits close in value to a light Tinted variant, where the graphite core does all the separation work.

**Three engines, five iterations.** Engine A is the shipping master (`icon.svg`, generated by `build_icon.py` so the strata geometry stays exact across iterations). Engine B (`icon-engineB-arrow.svg`) lost badly: field-less, taller than wide, broken seams, 5/12. One thing was salvaged from it and it earned its place, the discontinuous part-width hairlines that read like sedimentary bands pinching out. Engine C rasters (`icon-c1.png`, `icon-c2.png`) won the material comparison, and C1 set the bar twice.

**v1 to v5, what the renders caught that the source did not.** v1 hard-failed #3: a concentric inner ellipse on the top face turned the silhouette into an open pot, and quadratic Béziers whose end tangents are not vertical met the side walls at a visible corner, so the object looked sliced rather than resting. v2 replaced both with cubic halves at 4/3 handle length (tangent-vertical at the wall) and rounded shoulders. v3 cooled the ground off a cream cast and narrowed the warm bleed, which had been washing the middle third orange. v4 dropped the shed discs to residue opacity so they stopped competing with the signature, and added the floor bounce. v5 came from a straight side-by-side call that C1 still looked better than the master, which it did: the glyph grew from 58.6% to 64.8% of the tile, the lathe texture roughly doubled in density, the wall gained a warm near-black base instead of a uniform cool grey, and the emissive interior got a bright core plus a second glowing seam, with the warm bleed then tightened so the upper body stays dark and the heat concentrates at the seam. Each pass was judged on real renders at 1024 / 128 / 32 / 16, never on the source.
