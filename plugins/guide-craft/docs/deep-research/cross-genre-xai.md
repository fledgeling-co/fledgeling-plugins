I'll gather primary documentation on APIs, ToS, App Store guidelines, and meta-site patterns across the eight genres before synthesizing the blueprint.The architecture that survives all eight genres is a provenance-tagged catalog plus an official-API adapter, not a universal stats scraper. Store distribution is a licensing problem first and a product problem second.

## Executive Summary

- (High Confidence) Reuse one stack: a patch-versioned static catalog, a per-publisher ingest adapter that obeys documented rate limits and retention rules, an aggregation store keyed by patch, queue, and rank bracket with an explicit sample floor, and a provenance tag on every number (`official_telemetry`, `ladder_snapshot`, `lab_measurement`, `editorial`, `user_contributed`). Import/export codecs are the cross-genre primitive; telemetry is not.
- (High Confidence) Official, key-gated player or match APIs exist for Riot titles, Bungie/Destiny, Blizzard Battle.net, Valve Steam, Grinding Gear Games (Path of Exile), PUBG, and—since 27 July 2026—EA SPORTS FC, but only for three named partners. They were not found for Call of Duty player stats, Apex Legends, Fortnite live match data, Capcom fighters, or Madden/NBA 2K.
- (High Confidence) Hard limits that a small team must design around: Riot personal keys at 20 requests/1 second and 100 requests/2 minutes, production keys at 500 requests/10 seconds and 30,000 requests/10 minutes, per region; Steam Web API at 100,000 calls/day; Blizzard at 100 requests/second and 36,000/hour; Blizzard API data TTL of 30 days; EA FC account-data retention of 28 days.
- (High Confidence) What ships above the fold is a ranked recommendation plus one copyable artifact used between matches: champion/comp/loadout tier, price, or frame table, then a build, deck, squad, or loadout the player can paste before queueing. Live in-match prescription is the feature Riot forbids and overlay platforms reject.
- (High Confidence) Meta numbers fail in the same five ways everywhere: rank-bracket skew, win rate confounded by who picks, patch-boundary contamination, low-sample noise, and creator or lab data diverging from population telemetry. Ladder sites measure survivors. Guide sites measure authors. Frame-data wikis measure the lab.
- (High Confidence) App Store failure is now name-and-icon specific. Guideline 5.2.1 bars third-party trademarks without permission; 5.2.2 requires the service’s terms to permit the use; 4.1(c), added 13 November 2025, bars another developer’s brand or product name in the app name or icon without approval. Labeling an app “Unofficial” is not a license.
- (High Confidence) Publisher policies are not interchangeable. Riot allows store apps only with a written license or a valid API key plus policy compliance, and since 29 May 2025 forbids ads inside Riot properties. Bungie treats revenue-generating API use, including ads, as commercial and requires prior written permission. EA forbids paywalled fan content and data mining, and is not accepting new FC Community API partners. Capcom’s 4 November 2025 guidelines generally prohibit commercial derivatives.
- (High Confidence) Trajectory through late 2026 is closed-partner and API-only. From 6 October 2026 Riot blocks third-party game-memory access by default, with a grace period for known tools until April 2027. Products that depend on memory reads, scrapes, or unlicensed trademarks in the binary name are on the wrong side of both publisher and Apple policy.

#### 3. Statistical and provenance pitfalls

These are properties of the data, not of a particular site’s UI.

**Rank-bracket skew.** u.gg’s own FAQ says the recommended build is computed inside the selected filter, and that tiers combine win rate, pick rate, and weighted ban rate. [U.GG FAQ](https://u.gg/faq) [[21]](https://u.gg/faq) Changing the bracket changes the population. A Silver win rate and an Emerald+ win rate are not two measurements of the same champion. <INFERENCE from="u.gg FAQ filter definition; the general fact that pick rate falls as rank rises">Defaulting a season guide to “all ranks” or to “Platinum+” without printing the bracket is a provenance failure. The blueprint should refuse to render a build recommendation without a visible bracket and a game count.</INFERENCE>

**Win rate versus pick rate.** A high win rate at a very low pick rate is a specialist or counter pick. A moderate win rate at a high pick rate is the meta staple. u.gg states that it sorts in a way that foregrounds win rate while still folding pick rate and ban rate into the tier. [U.GG FAQ](https://u.gg/faq) [[21]](https://u.gg/faq) <INFERENCE from="that FAQ plus the TFT ban on Legend win rates">A single “tier” column hides the trade-off. The reusable widget is a scatter of win rate against pick rate, with ban rate as a third encoding, and a hard sample floor below which the point is drawn but not ranked. For TFT, that widget cannot include Legend or Legend-augment win rates at all.</INFERENCE>

**Patch-boundary contamination.** MetaBot’s TFT pipeline waits for at least 100,000 matches on the new patch before switching the public view, which it says usually takes a day or two, and it filters ingested matches to the latest patch. [MetaBot data methodology](https://metabot.gg/en/data-methodology) [[22]](https://metabot.gg/en/data-methodology) Riot’s own TFT match-history note says the service is optimized for fresh data and that older matches are more expensive to look up. [TFT Developer API Policy, match-history best practices](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698732381587-Teamfight-Tactics) [[8]](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698732381587-Teamfight-Tactics) <INFERENCE from="the 100,000-match gate and Riot’s fresh-data cost note">The ingest job must stamp `patch_id` from the match payload, not from the clock, and the read API must not union the last N days across a patch boundary. **Low-sample traps.** A 60% win rate on 200 games is not a 60% champion. Sites that publish a floor (1,000 games as an index threshold on at least one independent meta site; “about 2,000 games” as a practical threshold in secondary U.GG commentary) are doing the minimum. [LoL Meta champion deep dive, 1 August 2026](https://lolmeta.gg/learn/champion-deep-dive-worked-example) [[31]](https://lolmeta.gg/learn/champion-deep-dive-worked-example) The secondary U.GG commentary that cites a 5,000-game low-confidence flag and a 2,000-game action threshold is not u.gg’s own FAQ and should not be treated as u.gg’s published rule. Copying another site’s floor without stating it is how two “official API” sites disagree and both look authoritative.</INFERENCE>

**Creator versus telemetry.** poe.ninja’s build pages are the top of the ladder, which the FAQ defines via the official ladder API. That is a survivor snapshot. It is not the build distribution of the league. [poe.ninja FAQ](https://poe.ninja/faq) [[25]](https://poe.ninja/faq) Maxroll’s Diablo season coverage is written guides and author-updated tier lists; the Lord of Hatred launch note said builds would not be ranked on hard metrics at release. [Maxroll, Lord of Hatred update plan](https://maxroll.gg/) [[24]](https://maxroll.gg/?page=8) Dustloop’s numbers are lab timings, and the site’s own guide says they do not encode spacing. [Dustloop, Using Frame Data](https://www.dustloop.com/wiki/images/cache/c/c1/Using_Frame_Data.html) [[28]](https://www.dustloop.com/wiki/images/cache/c/c1/Using_Frame_Data.html) CODMunity’s store listing markets both “pros and verified creators” and “attachment pick rates” without a retrieved methodology. <INFERENCE from="those four provenance models">A season guide that mixes a creator loadout, a ladder snapshot, and a match-derived win rate in one tier list without labels will be wrong in a way the player cannot see. **Economy-specific trap.** Public stash APIs return listed items, not cleared trades. poe.ninja’s value is the filter for outliers and price-fixed listings, not the raw ask. [Poetrade, poe.ninja guide, 14 June 2026 SECONDARY; corroborates the official ladder and stash sources named in poe.ninja’s FAQ and GGG’s API docs](https://poetrades.net/poe-ninja-guide-2026/) [[33]](https://poetrades.net/poe-ninja-guide-2026/) [GGG developer docs](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs) [[19]](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs) Treating the minimum listed price as the market price is the ARPG equivalent of the low-sample trap.

#### 4. App Store intellectual property and naming failure modes

Apple’s current text, not a blog paraphrase, is the rejection standard.

Guideline 5.2.1: “Don’t use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission, and don’t include misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata in your app bundle or developer name. Apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property and other relevant rights.” [App Store Review Guidelines, 5.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) [[34]](https://developer.apple.com/APP-store/review/guidelines/)

Guideline 5.2.2: if the app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, you must be specifically permitted to do so under that service’s terms, and authorization must be provided on request. [App Store Review Guidelines, 5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) [[34]](https://developer.apple.com/APP-store/review/guidelines/) A scraped CoD or FC site fails this even if the pixels are original, because the terms do not permit the use. EA forbids data mining. Bungie forbids commercial API use without written permission. Riot forbids store distribution without a license or a compliant API key.

Guideline 4.1(c), added 13 November 2025: “You cannot use another developer’s icon, brand, or product name in your app’s icon or name, without approval from the developer.” [Apple Developer, 4.1 copycat reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection/) [[35]](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection/) [9to5Mac, 13 November 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-to-crack-down-on-copycat-apps/) [[36]](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-to-crack-down-on-copycat-apps/) Guideline 4.1(a) still bars copying another app’s name or UI with minor changes. 4.1(b) treats impersonation as a Developer Code of Conduct violation that can remove the account, not just the binary. [Apple Developer, 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection/) [[35]](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection/)

Guideline 4.2 rejects apps that do not rise above a repackaged website. [App Store Review Guidelines, 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) [[37]](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) A season guide that is a WKWebView around the same pages as the website is the predictable second rejection after a 5.2 rename.

<INFERENCE from="5.2.1, 5.2.2, 4.1(c), and the publisher clauses above">The naming pattern that fails is “[Game] Companion,” “[Game] Loadouts,” or an icon built from the game’s mark, submitted by a developer who is not the publisher. The pattern that can be defended is an original name and icon, a description that refers to compatibility without putting the trademark in the name, a visible non-affiliation notice, and—where the publisher requires it—a production key or a written license attached in Resolution Center. “Unofficial” in the subtitle does not satisfy 5.2.1, because the guideline requires permission, not a disclaimer.</INFERENCE>

Secondary rejection guides describe fan apps and “[Game] Guide & Maps” titles as typical 5.2.1 triggers. Those guides are not Apple. [ASOitis, Guideline 5.2, 9 October 2026 SECONDARY](https://asoitis.com/app-review/guideline-5-2-intellectual-property) [[38]](https://asoitis.com/app-review/guideline-5-2-intellectual-property) [AppSubmitter, 5.2.1 guide SECONDARY](https://www.appsubmitter.io/app-rejection-guides/app-store-guideline-5-2-1-intellectual-property/) [[39]](https://www.appsubmitter.io/app-rejection-guides/app-store-guideline-5-2-1-intellectual-property/) They are consistent with the guideline text. ### What is the current state, and what is the strongest supporting evidence for it?

The current state, as of the sources retrieved through November 2026, is a split market. Riot, Blizzard, Valve, Bungie, GGG, and PUBG still offer documented third-party data planes, each with a key, a quota, and a conduct rule that pushes analytics to before or after the match. EA FC has moved the sports companion market to a closed whitelist of three. Fighting games and Call of Duty remain editorial or lab markets. Apple has made the game’s name in the app name an explicit rejection trigger.

### What are the contrasting viewpoints or competing evidence?

**Win rate as the sort key versus win rate as a biased estimator.** u.gg’s FAQ defends sorting by win rate because “if a champion wins more, it has a higher win rate,” while still folding pick rate and ban rate into the tier. [U.GG FAQ](https://u.gg/faq) [[21]](https://u.gg/faq) The competing view, stated on an independent methodology page, is that a win rate measures the players who locked the champion as much as it measures the champion, so a bracket gap is a gap in who plays it. [LoL Meta, 1 August 2026](https://lolmeta.gg/learn/champion-deep-dive-worked-example) [[31]](https://lolmeta.gg/learn/champion-deep-dive-worked-example) Both can be true. The architectural response is to show both rates and the bracket, not to pick a side in the UI and hide the other.

**Ladder popularity versus guide optimality.** poe.ninja will not list a character that is not on the official ladder. [poe.ninja FAQ](https://poe.ninja/faq) [[25]](https://poe.ninja/faq) Maxroll will publish a build that has not cleared the ladder, because the product is a guide. [Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/) [[24]](https://maxroll.gg/?page=8) Players treat both as “the meta.” They are answering different questions. A reusable blueprint should not merge them into one ranking.

**Commercial fan apps: three incompatible publisher answers.** Riot permits commercial products that hold a valid project-specific key, are registered, keep a free tier, and stay transformative. [Legal Jibber Jabber, exception 3](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal) [[6]](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal) [Riot General Policies](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698591841939-General-Policies) [[7]](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698591841939-General-Policies) Bungie defines revenue, including ad revenue driven by API data, as commercial use requiring prior written permission. [Bungie API Terms](https://www.bungie.net/terms) [[9]](https://www.bungie.net/terms) EA allows passive ads on fan sites, forbids paywalls, and separately refuses Community API access to anyone outside three partners. [EA content policy](https://help.ea.com/en/articles/security-and-rules/ea-content-policy) [[11]](https://help.ea.com/en/articles/security-and-rules/ea-content-policy) [EA FC Community API Update](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-community-api-update) [[12]](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-community-api-update) A single monetization module—IAP plus ads plus a game trademark in the name—is not portable across these three.

**“The API key is permission” versus “the API key is a technical grant.”** Riot’s exception 3 treats a valid key plus policy compliance as the commercial license for that project. iRacing says the opposite in one sentence: issuance of a key or OAuth client “constitutes only a technical grant to access authorized APIs” and “does not constitute authorization for any activity otherwise prohibited.” [iRacing, 1 October 2026](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000179757-third-party-development-please-review-the-iracing-terms) [[20]](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000179757-third-party-development-please-review-the-iracing-terms) Do not generalize Riot’s model to iRacing, Bungie, or EA.

Riot’s own General Policies page, updated 29 May 2025, contains the operative sentence (“You may not place advertisements in Riot properties, which include in-game, loading screens, and the Riot Client”). ## Knowledge Gaps

- **No public API exists, or none was published.** Call of Duty player and match statistics, official Apex Legends stats, Fortnite live-match or replay access, Capcom frame data, Madden, and NBA 2K. A companion for those titles cannot be an honest telemetry product on current evidence.
- **Closed partner programs.** EA is not accepting new FC Community API applicants. FUTBIN-like account features are not available to a new developer. Market pages on unapproved sites remain in tension with EA’s data-mining ban.
- **Operator methodology not retrieved.** MetaTFT’s own sampling, rank filter, and sample floor were not on a page retrieved here. CODMunity’s “attachment pick rates” have no published denominator. light.gg’s commercial arrangement with Bungie, if any, was not in the terms page.
- **Rejection dockets.** No primary App Store resolution text was found for the named companion apps. Policy text supports the failure mode. Case outcomes do not.
- **Usage measurement.** No audited between-match session counts, retention, or feature-use shares were found for u.gg, FUTBIN, DIM, or CODMunity. Feature prominence is a proxy.
- **Stale contracts still governing.** Steam Web API Terms were last updated July 2010. Blizzard API Terms were last updated 1 October 2019. Riot’s Legal Jibber Jabber store clause was last updated August 2018. They are still the posted rules. A newer private addendum, if one exists, was not retrieved.
- **Blizzard throttle page.** The 100 rps / 36,000 per hour figures were captured from the getting-started URL in search. A direct fetch in this session redirected and returned an empty body. Treat the number as documented but re-verify before hard-coding a client.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **File two App Store review builds that differ only in the name and icon**—one original, one using the game’s title—against Guideline 4.1(c) and 5.2.1. Rationale: press confirms the rule text; only a Resolution Center reply tells you whether a compatibility phrase in the subtitle is tolerated for your genre.
2. **Submit the Riot production-key application against a finished website that hosts the iOS download, the ToS, and the privacy policy.** Rationale: Riot’s FAQ says a website is required in most cases even for a mobile app, and public betas on a personal key are prohibited. The calendar risk is review, which Riot says is typically weekly and can run to three weeks.
3. **Run a patch-cutover study on one Riot title: confidence intervals on win rate at 6 hours, 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days, split by rank bracket.** Rationale: the 100,000-match gate is one operator’s choice, not a law. Your sample floor should be computed for the bracket you will actually show, especially Challenger and top-500 Warzone-style slices where `n` never gets large.
4. **Get a written Bungie position before any Destiny IAP or ad model, and a written EA position before any FC account feature.** Rationale: Bungie’s terms define ad-funded API use as commercial. EA’s whitelist is closed. Both are binary gates, not design tweaks.
5. **For CoD, fighters, and non-iRacing sims, spec the v1 product as editorial plus calculator plus codec, with provenance badges, and do not staff a telemetry pipeline.** Rationale: no official match feed was found. A fake win rate is the failure mode that both misleads players and fails 5.2.2 if it depends on a scrape the terms forbid.
