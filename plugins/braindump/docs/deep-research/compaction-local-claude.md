# Context-Compaction Summaries for Coding Agents: Design and Benchmarking

## Executive Summary

- **(High Confidence) The dominant failure mode is not "the summary is bad prose" — it is that a specific load-bearing span is absent, and absence is near-deterministically fatal.** The ConstraintRot study (1,323 episodes, seven model families) found policy-violation rates of 0% when a governing constraint was in full context, 30% after compaction on average, and up to 59% for the worst model; conditioned on the constraint *surviving* the summary, violation stayed at 0%, but when it was dropped, violation hit 38% [Chen, "Governance Decay," arXiv:2606.22528, June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528). This is the single most important design fact: **optimize the summarizer for span-level recall of a small set of "must-survive" items, not for overall summary quality.**
- **(High Confidence) Compaction is a weak lever compared with alternatives, and should be treated as a last resort rather than the primary mechanism.** On LOCA-bench at 128K environment-description length, LLM compaction bought +2.6pp (DeepSeek-V3.2), +2.7pp (Gemini-3-Flash), and **−2.7pp** (GPT-5.2-Medium), while programmatic tool calling bought +13.3, +9.4 and +10.6pp respectively [Zeng, Huang & He, LOCA-bench, arXiv:2602.07962, Feb 2026, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1). Independently, JetBrains found that simple observation masking *matched or slightly exceeded* LLM summarization's solve rate on SWE-bench Verified while being ~52% cheaper than the raw-agent baseline [Lindenbauer et al., "The Complexity Trap," DL4Code @ NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433).
- **(High Confidence) Position effects are real but are the wrong explanation for the specific losses you care about.** Lost-in-the-middle and context rot degrade *reading* the long context [Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle," TACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172); conversational summarizers additionally show measurable leading-vs-recency skew [PoSum-Bench, EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.404/). But the compaction-specific losses — standing constraints, corrections, dead ends — are better explained by **salience mis-specification**: a correction stated once in turn 12 is low-frequency, low-recency, and semantically unremarkable next to 200 tool calls, so it loses the salience competition regardless of position.
- **(High Confidence) The highest-leverage prompt-level techniques, in order: (1) exempt a small class of items from compaction entirely rather than asking the model to summarize them well; (2) require verbatim quotation for that class; (3) impose an explicit negative-knowledge section.** Constraint Pinning restored violations to 0% [arXiv:2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528); extract-then-generate raised FactCC from sub-10% baselines across four datasets [Zhang, Liu & Zhang, arXiv:2304.04193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04193); and including failure trajectories rather than successes-only moved WebArena success from 46.5 → 49.7 [ReasoningBank, arXiv:2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140).
- **(Medium Confidence) Do not rely on a self-critique pass inside the summarizer.** Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback does not reliably improve reasoning outputs and sometimes degrades them [Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798). A *checklist verification against the transcript* (external evidence) is a different and defensible operation; "re-read your summary and improve it" is not.
- **(High Confidence) The only defensible primary benchmark metric is downstream continuation success; LLM-as-judge summary quality is a secondary, bias-laden signal.** G-Eval's correlation with human raters on SummEval is ρ ≈ 0.514 [Liu et al., G-Eval, arXiv:2303.16634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634), well below inter-human agreement of 0.8–0.9, and judges carry position, verbosity and self-preference bias — the last being directly disqualifying if you judge a Claude-written summary with a Claude judge.
- **(High Confidence) Your A/B benchmark will almost certainly be underpowered unless you design for it.** Sample size scales with the inverse square of the minimum detectable effect, agent benchmarks have large per-run stochastic variance, and task clustering (by repo/domain) inflates the required N further ["Resolution Diagnostics for Paired LLM Evaluation," arXiv:2605.30315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30315). Use paired designs on identical transcripts, McNemar or paired bootstrap on per-item deltas, and report the MDE alongside the headline number.
- **(Medium Confidence) The right architecture is hybrid: compaction plus an out-of-context durable artifact.** Anthropic's own long-running-agent guidance abandons pure compaction — "compaction… doesn't always pass perfectly clear instructions to the next agent" — in favour of a `claude-progress.txt` plus a structured JSON feature list plus git history [Anthropic, "Effective harnesses for long-running agents"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents).

## Detailed Findings

### RQ1: How should an LLM coding agent write a context-compaction summary that maximally preserves what a fresh session needs, and how should it be evaluated against a baseline?

#### The decisive answer, stated up front

<INFERENCE from="ConstraintRot's 0%-if-present / 38%-if-dropped conditional; Anthropic's recall-then-precision tuning advice; extract-then-generate FactCC results; ReasoningBank's failure-inclusion ablation; LOCA-bench's weak compaction deltas">
A compaction summary should not be written as a summary. It should be written as a **two-tier artifact**: a *pinned tier* of items reproduced verbatim and never compressed, and a *narrative tier* that is genuinely summarized. The measured evidence says almost all of the recoverable value sits in the pinned tier, and almost all of the loss is items that should have been pinned but were narrated instead.
</INFERENCE>

Concretely, the skill should instruct the agent to emit:

**Tier 1 — Pinned, verbatim, uncompressed** (these are the items whose absence is catastrophic and whose presence is cheap):
1. **Standing constraints and prohibitions** — every "always/never/don't" the user or project instructions established, quoted word-for-word with the turn it came from. Anthropic's own Claude Code summarization prompt already does this for one narrow class: security-relevant constraints (sensitive files, forbidden operations, credential handling) are to be preserved word-for-word [Claude Code v2.1.205 conversation-summarization prompt, community extraction](https://github.com/Piebald-AI/claude-code-system-prompts/blob/main/system-prompts/agent-prompt-conversation-summarization.md). The ConstraintRot result argues this class should be *widened to all standing constraints*, not just security ones.
2. **User corrections** — every instance where the user rejected, redirected, or corrected the agent, quoted verbatim. The baseline prompt asks for "specific user feedback — especially if the user asked for something to be done differently" but places it inside a narrative "Errors and fixes" section, which permits paraphrase.
3. **Rejected approaches and dead ends, with the reason** — see the negative-knowledge finding below.
4. **Exact identifiers** — absolute file paths, function signatures, error strings, failing test names, command invocations. These are the tokens a successor cannot reconstruct by inference and cannot guess.

**Tier 2 — Narrative, compressed**: task state, what was built, why, what remains. This is where ordinary summarization competence applies and where the marginal return on prompt engineering is low.

The skill should also carry an explicit **anti-drift clause on next steps** — the baseline already has a good one: any proposed next step must be "DIRECTLY in line with the user's most recent explicit requests," with verbatim quotes showing where work stopped [ibid.](https://github.com/Piebald-AI/claude-code-system-prompts/blob/main/system-prompts/agent-prompt-conversation-summarization.md). Keep this; it is the single strongest element of the incumbent prompt.

#### (1) What long-context agent summarization loses, and why

**Standing constraints.** This is the best-measured loss. ConstraintRot grades tool calls deterministically across long-horizon scenarios in seven model families over 1,323 episodes. Violation rises from 0% (policy in full context) to 30% post-compaction, peaking at 59% for the worst families; the conditional decomposition is the important part — 0% violation when the constraint survives into the summary, 38% when it does not [arXiv:2606.22528, v2 27 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528). The paper attributes failure to the constraint's *absence from context*, not to reasoning degradation. It also demonstrates a "Compaction-Eviction Attack," in which adversarial in-context content biases the summarizer into omitting a legitimate policy, reported to defeat all seven evaluated families. <MISSING_DATA>The abstract does not disclose scenario counts, domains, compaction algorithm, or the implementation of the proposed Constraint Pinning mitigation (verbatim retention vs. re-injection vs. protected buffer). The PDF/TeX source would be needed.</MISSING_DATA>

**Negative knowledge (rejected approaches, dead ends).** There is good qualitative and indirect quantitative evidence, and a real gap in direct measurement. #### (2) Prompt-level techniques for summarization-for-continuation

The core distinction: a summary for a reader optimizes comprehension; a summary for continuation optimizes **sufficiency for action**. A reader tolerates paraphrase; a successor agent that receives `src/auth/session.ts` as "the session file" has to re-search.

| Technique | Measured result | Applicability | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exemption / pinning** (some content never enters the summarizer) | Constraint Pinning restores violation to 0% on ConstraintRot [arXiv:2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528); Anthropic's server-side `pause_after_compaction` exists precisely to let you re-insert content verbatim after the summary [Anthropic, Compaction docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/compaction) | Directly | **Adopt. Highest measured effect of anything here.** |
| **Verbatim preservation instructions** | Extract-then-generate lifted FactCC from sub-10% baselines across four datasets; self-extracted salient sentences worked about as well as ORACLE extractions [arXiv:2304.04193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04193). Anthropic's own guidance recommends word-for-word quote extraction before the task for >20k-token documents [Anthropic, "Reduce hallucinations"](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) | Directly | **Adopt, with a caveat** (below). |
| **Extract-then-compress pipeline** (two passes: enumerate candidates → compress) | Same FactCC evidence; the extraction step need not be oracle-quality | Directly | **Adopt for the pinned tier.** Cost is one extra sampling step. |
| **Structured section schema** | The incumbent Claude Code prompt uses 9 numbered sections; Anthropic's SDK default uses 5; server-side default uses free prose [Anthropic, Context editing docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing). Counter-evidence: format restriction measurably degrades *reasoning* — GPT-3.5 answered GSM8K correctly in NL and failed under format constraints; stricter constraints → greater degradation [Tam et al., EMNLP 2024 Industry Track](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442) | Partly | **Adopt sections; do not adopt strict JSON/constrained decoding.** Markdown headings with a free-text `<analysis>` scratchpad first (as the incumbent does) is the right shape — it preserves a reasoning channel. |
| **Salience / keep-drop criteria** | Anthropic's explicit tuning method: "start by maximizing recall so the prompt captures every relevant piece of information, then iterate to improve precision by eliminating superfluous content" [Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) | Directly | **Adopt as the development method, not as prompt text.** Recall-first is exactly right; ConstraintRot says the asymmetry of costs (missing constraint = 38% violation; redundant sentence = a few tokens) justifies heavily favouring recall. |
| **Self-critique / verification pass** | Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback fails to improve reasoning and sometimes degrades it [Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798); Tyen et al. show error *localization* is the bottleneck, not correction | Conditionally | **Adopt only in verified form.** A pass that re-reads the transcript to check "did I capture every user correction?" has external evidence and is a retrieval task, not intrinsic self-correction. A pass that only re-reads the summary is not supported. |
| **Chain of Density** (fixed length, iteratively increase entity density) | Humans preferred CoD summaries over a plain GPT-4 prompt; but readability and coherence degrade when entity density gets too high [Adams et al., NewSum @ EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.newsum-1.7/) | Weakly | **Skip as a mechanism; borrow the insight.** CoD's contribution is that entity density is the axis that matters — which is what the pinned tier delivers more directly and without the length constraint that motivated CoD (you do not need fixed length). |

**The caveat on verbatim preservation you must design around.** Faithfulness gains from quote-grounding may partly reflect increased *extractiveness* rather than better selection: using a faithfulness–abstractiveness trade-off curve as a control, several proposed faithfulness methods failed to consistently beat the control at matched abstractiveness ["Faithful or Extractive?", arXiv:2108.13684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13684). Worse for your eval: LLM judges often fail to detect inconsistencies when content is copied verbatim, so verbatim-heavy summaries get scored generously. **Implication: your benchmark must control for summary length and extractiveness, or your rewritten skill will "win" simply by copying more.**

#### (3) Evaluation methodology for continuation summaries

**Metric hierarchy — use all three tiers, weight the first.**

| Tier | Metric | What it measures | Cost | Bias exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Primary** | Downstream continuation success | Does a fresh session, seeded only with the summary, complete the task correctly? | High (full agent run per transcript per arm) | Low — but high variance |
| **2. Secondary** | Exact-match recall of pre-labelled load-bearing spans | Did the specific must-survive items appear? | Low (string/normalized match) | **None** — this is the deterministic metric |
| **3. Tertiary** | LLM-as-judge rubric scores; NLI faithfulness (AlignScore / MiniCheck / SummaC) | Precision, coherence, hallucination | Medium | High (see below) |

**Build Tier 2 first; it is the metric that will actually move your design.** The construction: for each real transcript, hand-label (once) a set of *load-bearing spans* — the standing constraints, user corrections, rejected approaches, absolute file paths, error strings and failing-test names that a successor demonstrably needs. Then measure per-category recall in each candidate summary by normalized exact/near-exact match. This is FactScore-style atomic decomposition applied to a continuation, and it is deterministic, cheap, re-runnable, and immune to judge bias. <INFERENCE from="ConstraintRot's finding that presence-in-summary fully determines compliance (0% vs 38%), combined with the deterministic cheapness of string matching">Because presence in the summary is nearly sufficient for correct downstream behaviour on the constraint class, span recall is not merely a proxy for continuation success on that class — it is close to a direct measure of it. That makes it the correct primary optimization target during development, with downstream success reserved for final validation.</INFERENCE>

**Downstream task-success evaluation.** This is what the compaction literature actually uses. CompactionRL fixes the execution agent (GLM-4.7-Flash) and varies only the summary agent, reporting pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified plus average summaries triggered per trace — the correct A/B shape, and directly transferable [Li, Hou, Jing, Tang & Dong, CompactionRL, arXiv:2607.05378, Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05378). Their framing of the failure mode matches the pinned-tier thesis: a summary omitting a crucial file path, error message, failed command, or partial solution renders subsequent actions ineffective even under a strong execution policy. Note their reporting caveat: SWE-bench Verified evaluated on a random 200-instance subset while public baselines use the full set.

**LLM-as-judge protocol and its known biases.** If you use a judge at all, the constraints are non-negotiable:

- **Never judge with the same model family that wrote the summary.** Self-preference bias is well-documented and stronger in more capable models ["Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge," arXiv:2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21819v2). Judging a Claude-written compaction summary with Claude is the single most likely way to produce a wrong result.
- **Randomize and swap presentation order**, then report the flip rate. Position bias is systematic in pairwise comparison and is largely mitigable by order rearrangement ["Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge," arXiv:2410.02736](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02736).
- **Control for length.** Verbosity bias favours longer responses; your new prompt will likely produce longer summaries, which will inflate judge scores independent of quality.
- **Calibrate the ceiling.** G-Eval reaches ρ ≈ 0.514 on SummEval (0.582 coherence, 0.507 consistency) [arXiv:2303.16634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634), against inter-human agreement of roughly 0.8–0.9. Reliability is also criterion-dependent: coherence and relevance judge reliably; fluency and consistency should be treated skeptically regardless of judge model ["Diagnosing LLM Judge Reliability," arXiv:2604.15302](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15302v1).
- **Reference-free judging is structurally biased** toward text resembling the judge's own model and against human-written text ["On the Limitations of Reference-Free Evaluations of Generated Text," arXiv:2210.12563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.12563).

**NLI faithfulness metrics** (AlignScore, MiniCheck, SummaC-Conv) are worth running as a hallucination guard, since your rewritten prompt may increase invention. But their fine-grained agreement is limited: the best-performing metric in one comparative analysis reached Spearman 0.445 / Kendall 0.297 against a Pearson of 0.565, indicating poor instance-level correlation ["A Comparative Analysis of Faithfulness Metrics and Humans," arXiv:2408.12398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.12398). Use them to catch regressions, not to rank arms.

**Fair A/B protocol on the same transcripts.** The design that survives scrutiny:

1. **Corpus.** Real transcripts, stratified by the properties that matter — number of turns, presence of user corrections, presence of abandoned approaches, presence of standing constraints. Freeze the corpus before you look at results. Include transcripts where a correction occurred *early* and was still binding at the end; that is the discriminating case.
2. **Pairing.** Both summarizers run on identical transcript prefixes cut at identical points. Analyze **per-transcript deltas**, not two independent means [arXiv:2605.30315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30315).
3. **Held-fixed variables.** Same summarizer model, same temperature, same continuation model, same tools, same task. Vary only the prompt. CompactionRL's execution-agent-fixed design is the template [arXiv:2607.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05378).
4. **Replication.** Multiple runs per condition; agentic benchmarks have substantial stochastic variance, with one study attributing 10.56–33.70% of total variance to within-model variability ["Within-Model vs Between-Prompt Variability," arXiv:2601.21339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21339).
5. **Statistics.** McNemar for paired binary success; paired bootstrap on per-item deltas for continuous scores. Report the observed gap, N, the MDE at your N, and the resolution ratio q = N/N*. Correct for clustering if transcripts group by repo or project — this materially raises N* [arXiv:2605.30315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30315). Sample size grows quadratically with 1/MDE: halving the detectable gap costs 4× the runs.
6. **Confounds to neutralize:** summary token length (report it; consider a length-matched arm), extractiveness ratio, and number of compaction events per trace.
7. **Report null results honestly** as "no lift larger than X pp," not as "no difference."

#### (4) Memory and continuity architectures vs. pure summarization

The measured verdict: **pure summarization is the weakest of the available mechanisms**, and every serious 2025–2026 system pairs it with durable out-of-context state.

| Architecture | Measured result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Programmatic tool calling** (code instead of stepwise tool turns) | +13.3pp DeepSeek, +10.6pp GPT-5.2, +9.4pp Gemini-3-Flash, +6.0pp Claude-4.5-Opus at 128K — while *shortening* trajectories in every case | [LOCA-bench Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1) |
| **Memory tool** (file-based, out of context) | +5.3pp GPT-5.2, +9.4pp Gemini-3-Flash; **−2.7pp DeepSeek-V3.2** (frontier models benefit, open models sometimes do not) | [ibid.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1) |
| **Observation masking** (replace old tool outputs with placeholders; keep reasoning + actions) | Matches or slightly exceeds LLM summarization solve rate; 52% cheaper than raw agent and +2.6pp solve rate with Qwen3-Coder 480B; ~$0.03/instance cheaper than LLM-Summary. Hybrid masking+summary: −7% vs pure masking, −11% vs pure summarization | [Complexity Trap, DL4Code @ NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433) |
| **LLM compaction** | +2.6pp DeepSeek, +2.7pp Gemini-3-Flash, **−2.7pp GPT-5.2** | [LOCA-bench Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1) |
| **Structured reasoning memory across sessions** | ReasoningBank: +8.3pp WebArena, up to +34.2% relative vs prior memory designs, −16% steps; retrieval k=1 outperforms k=4 (49.7 vs 44.4) | [arXiv:2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140) |
| **Retrieval over prior sessions** | LongMemEval: long-context LLMs show 30–60% performance drop on LongMemEval_S (~115k tokens, 30–40 sessions); indexing/retrieval/reading optimizations recover much of it | [Wu et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813) |
| **Compaction + progress file + git + JSON feature list** | Anthropic's recommended production pattern; no head-to-head numbers published | [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents) |

Two things follow for your skill. First, **the retrieval-k finding is a warning about your own design**: more retrieved context made ReasoningBank *worse*. A longer, more comprehensive summary is not monotonically better; the pinned tier must stay short and high-precision. Second, **the durable-artifact pattern outperforms in-context handoff for exactly the items you are worried about**. Anthropic's own long-running-agent guidance abandons pure compaction because it "doesn't always pass perfectly clear instructions to the next agent," substituting `claude-progress.txt`, git history, and a JSON feature list — choosing JSON specifically because the model "is less likely to inappropriately change or overwrite JSON files" [ibid.](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents). Practitioner reports converge: the most frequently requested Claude Code fix across compaction bug reports is automatic re-reading of `CLAUDE.md` / `MEMORY.md` after compaction, because the compaction summary neither preserves nor references them [claude-code issue #21925, Jan 2026](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/21925), [issue #31409, Mar 2026](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/31409), [issue #9796, Oct 2025](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/9796).

### RQ2: What is the current state, and what is the strongest supporting evidence?

The field has moved from "compaction is how you extend context" to "compaction is the lossiest available option, use it last." The strongest single piece of evidence is ConstraintRot's conditional decomposition (0% violation if the constraint survives, 38% if not, over 1,323 episodes and seven model families) [arXiv:2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528), because it converts a vague quality question into a binary presence/absence question with a measurable, near-deterministic consequence. The second strongest is the pair of independent negative results — JetBrains showing observation masking matches LLM summarization on SWE-bench Verified at lower cost [arXiv:2508.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433), and LOCA-bench showing compaction is the weakest of six context-management strategies and net-negative for GPT-5.2 [arXiv:2602.07962](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1). Anthropic's own product direction corroborates: server-side compaction shipped with `pause_after_compaction` specifically so callers can re-insert content verbatim, and the long-running-agent guidance recommends durable files over compaction for handoff.

### RQ3: Contrasting viewpoints and competing evidence

- **Is compaction worth doing at all?** Against: LOCA-bench's near-zero deltas; JetBrains's masking-matches-summarization result; the multi-turn-inertia authors' decision not to adopt summarization due to context collapse and brevity bias. For: CoMem reports 41.0% on SWE-bench Verified with DeepSWE-32B, slightly *above* the 40.4% full-context baseline, arguing aligned summarization filters noise for mid-sized models, with 1.45×–2.08× inference speedups [CoMem, arXiv:2605.30842 — figures from search summary, not directly verified](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30842) <CONFIDENCE:LOW>I did not fetch the CoMem paper directly; treat these specific numbers as unconfirmed.</CONFIDENCE:LOW>. CompactionRL reports +7.0 and +5.5 absolute pass@1 gains on SWE-bench Verified from *training* the summarizer [arXiv:2607.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05378) — but that is a fine-tuning result, explicitly out of scope for you.
- **Structured schema: help or harm?** Format restriction measurably degrades reasoning [arXiv:2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442), but structured-generation maintainers argue carefully designed schemas plus a reasoning field avoid most of the degradation, and the paper conflates prompt-level format instructions with constrained decoding. The incumbent Claude Code prompt already threads this needle with a free `<analysis>` block preceding structured output — a design you should keep.
- **Lost-in-the-middle's universality** is contested (see RQ1 table).

### RQ4: What changed recently, and what is the trajectory?

Three shifts in the last 12 months. **First, compaction became infrastructure**: `compact_20260112` moved it server-side across Claude API, Bedrock, Vertex and Foundry, with ZDR support, which makes the summarizer prompt a first-class configurable surface rather than harness-internal code. **Second, the field started measuring the right thing** — ConstraintRot, LOCA-bench and CompactionRL all evaluate compaction by downstream agent behaviour rather than summary quality, and all three appeared in 2026. **Third, the centre of gravity moved from compaction to structure**: programmatic tool calling and memory tools now beat compaction by 3–5× on LOCA-bench, and the strongest cross-session result (ReasoningBank) is a structured memory of strategies distilled from *both* successes and failures, not a transcript summary. <INFERENCE from="the server-side compaction API's pause_after_compaction hook, Anthropic's harness guidance recommending progress files, and LOCA-bench's ranking of strategies">The trajectory is toward compaction as a *narrow* mechanism operating on a deliberately reduced surface — with constraints, corrections and identifiers routed around it into pinned or durable storage — rather than as the general solution to context exhaustion.</INFERENCE>

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Build the load-bearing-span labelling pass before writing a single prompt variant.** Rationale: ConstraintRot's 0%/38% split makes span presence the highest-signal, cheapest, bias-free metric available, and it is the only one that will let you iterate in minutes rather than hours. Label four categories (standing constraints, user corrections, rejected approaches, exact identifiers) across 40–60 real transcripts. This corpus is also the artifact that fills the two published measurement gaps above.

2. **Run a three-arm rather than two-arm benchmark: incumbent Claude Code prompt, your rewrite, and a pinning-only variant** (incumbent prompt + verbatim constraint/correction block, nothing else changed). Rationale: if the pinning-only variant captures most of the gain — which the ConstraintRot and Complexity Trap evidence predicts — you have a much smaller, more robust skill to maintain, and you will know your other changes are decoration. This is the build-vs-buy question in miniature: the minimum change that beats the baseline is the one worth shipping.

3. **Power the benchmark before running it, and publish the MDE.** Rationale: with agent-level stochastic variance in the 10–34% range and transcripts likely clustered by project, a 40-transcript single-run A/B will not resolve a 5pp difference. Compute N* for your target MDE, correct for clustering, and if you cannot afford N*, say so and lead with the deterministic span-recall metric instead of an underpowered success-rate claim.

4. **Instrument length and extractiveness as first-class confounds.** Rationale: the faithfulness–abstractiveness literature and verbosity bias in judges both mean a longer, more quote-heavy summary will score better without being better. Add a length-matched arm, or at minimum report tokens-per-summary and copy ratio next to every score.

5. **Prototype the durable-artifact escape hatch alongside the prompt rewrite.** Rationale: LOCA-bench ranks memory tools and programmatic tool calling 3–5× above compaction, Anthropic's own harness guidance routes handoff through files rather than summaries, and the top community request is post-compaction memory re-read. A skill that writes standing constraints and dead ends to a durable file — and instructs the successor to read it — sidesteps the lossy channel entirely for exactly the content that matters most. Benchmark it as a fourth arm.
