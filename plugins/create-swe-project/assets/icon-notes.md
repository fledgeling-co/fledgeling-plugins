# create-swe-project icon notes

**Direction: "First Water"** is a hybrid of the studio's *Tahoe Gel-Glass* sub-register (a), *porcelain + gel object*, crossed with a Tahoe-softened *Object Tile*. The subject is a slipway, so the icon is the moment the hull takes the water: bow still high on the way, stern already in. The porcelain daylight register is a deliberate sibling call; the dark deep-sea register belongs to trawl, and two plugins in one marketplace should not share a ground. Runner-up: sub-register (b), saturated tile plus white frosted glyph, rejected because a frosted hull on a hue field turns the app's one warm accent into decoration, and white-on-hue is the corpus's named #2 failure.

**Glyph (subject-mined, device bank #16 + #21 + #6):** a hull with a short steep bow entry, full depth held through the body, and a quarter rounding to a transom, sliding down an inclined way with regular sleepers. The composition physically enacts the app's verb (#16): the thing you asked for is going into the water. Two earlier drafts read as an open canoe, and the cause was a long tapering bow plus a bright rim running the whole gunwale; the fix was a shorter entry and a fuller body.

**Signature:** two authored overlaps, not one. The hull's after half passes beneath the surface and its hue visibly cools and deepens there, and the way runs on under the water for the whole lower right of the tile. That is the era's craft tell, and it is the one thing a flat pre-masked raster cannot fake, because it demands real transparency between real layers.

**Palette:** two families only. Warm marigold is reserved strictly for the hull (`#FFDD79` → `#FFC64C` → `#F9A62E` → `#ED8213` → `#C25E0B`, rim `#A34C08`); cool slate carries the way and the water (`#E9EFF3` → `#CBD7DF` → `#8D9DAB`, water `#CFE1EB` → `#A8C7D9` → `#7BA5BE`); the ground is a neutral porcelain cushion. One soft top-left light. No emissive interior: that second source is trawl's, and taking it would blur two siblings together.

**Audit: 11/12, zero failures on the non-negotiable 1 to 4.** Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the squircle as a *clip*, no baked corners or shadow, symmetric 132px margins. Silhouette and 16px squint were verified on real renders rather than imagined: a solid-black silhouette still names "hull entering water off a slipway", and the 32px render magnified six times still carries. Check #10 variant robustness passes by construction, with four layers `#bg`/`#mid`/`#fg`/`#highlight` mapping 1:1 onto Icon Composer and identity held in shape and value. The point is deducted at #7: above the waterline the way is light slate on near-white porcelain and leans on the hull to be found.

**Three engines, and the master lost the first round.** Engine B (Arrow vector, `icon-engineB-arrow.svg`) scored 7/12 and lost on three non-negotiables at once; its chunkier hull proportion is what started fixing the canoe problem, so it earned its place. Engine C raster (`icon-engineC1.png`, corpus-referenced against Safari and Photos) scored 10/12 and **won the material comparison outright**, and it also proved the open-water composition can read after two hand-authored attempts at it had failed. It still cannot ship, because a flat pre-masked raster is failure mode #10 by definition. So the Engine A master was **rebuilt to C1's composition and material**, which is what the pipeline prescribes: when the raster wins the look, the vector master becomes the answer to it. An earlier hand-authored direction, "On the Blocks" (the hull framed up in its cradle, no water), was abandoned at that point; it scored the same 11/12 but it was a weaker idea, and matching the score is not the same as matching the icon. `icon-engineC2.png` is the control take at 8/12. Every score and every loser is on `audit.html` with the reason it lost.

**Known liabilities.** The hull spans roughly 74% of the tile, wider than the 55 to 65% the composition guidance calls for; that is copied deliberately from C1 and is worth re-testing. The sleepers and the underwater run of the way are gone by 32px, so the small-size read rests entirely on the hull, and the hull is slender enough at 16px to be nameable rather than instant.

**Ship decision (user):** C2 selected as the shipping brand mark for icon-128/256 and the banner; masked full-bleed to the squircle as `icon-c2-masked.png`. The layered SVG master stays in the repo as the vector source and macOS-submission path.
