# ship-fleet icon notes

**Direction: "Berth Grid".** Tahoe gel-glass **sub-register (a)**: a vellum-porcelain cushion carrying coloured gel objects. The tile is a harbour basin seen from above and angled, because that is the position ship-fleet actually occupies — it does not sail, it *prepares*: it surveys everything remaining in a repo, readies each feature in its own berth, and lets them go one at a time. Runner-up: **Instrument Emblem**, the orchestrator's own dependency graph as a node-and-edge mark, rejected because a graph is what the *artifact* looks like rather than what the *work* looks like, and because a thin node-link lattice has no carrying shape at 16px.

**Glyph (subject-mined; device bank #16 the icon performs the verb, #18 edge-bleed physicality, #20 data-as-glyph):** a stone shore running off three edges of the mask, with four finger piers projecting into the basin at slightly unequal lengths and three berth slips between them. Two clay vessels lie bow-in, held at their berths. The top slip is **empty**, and one vermilion gel vessel is already clear of the pier heads on a departing heading, its wake running back through the slip it vacated.

**Signature move:** the ordered berth grid with one vessel about to depart — and the *vacated* slip is what makes it a fleet being worked rather than a marina. The wake is the connective tissue: it starts inside an empty berth, so the composition states cause and effect in one element instead of two.

**Distinct from its siblings by construction.** ship-armada owns three saturated hulls in echelon at open sea on a chart plate; this is the harbour — organised, moored, one thing leaving. The shipyard icon owns a hull on a cradle; ship-feature owns a single vessel at launch. Here no vessel is under construction and no vessel is alone: the subject is the *grid*, and the accent is the exception to it.

**Risk taken:** a warm sand basin rather than the pale vellum plate the sibling icons float their objects on. It was forced by measurement, not taste, and then walked back by measurement. On the family plate the piers read **1.11:1** against the water they sit in — the berth grid, which is the whole signature move, had no figure-ground at all. Deepening the basin fixed the piers and quietly cost the vessels, because the vessels are dark and the piers are light: at the deepest setting tried, the focal sat at 1.85:1. Sweeping four settings showed the trade cleanly — the basin cannot lift both — and the shipped setting spends 0.16 of pier ratio to take the focal to **2.18:1** and the berthed pair to **2.32:1**. The piers can afford it; they also carry coursing, walls, mouldings and cast shadows, and rubric #7 is about the glyph. The tile is still a step warmer and deeper than its shelf-mates, so the **dark system variant is the one to check first** on any revision.

**Palette:** two hue families plus a neutral. Warm vellum stone (`#FFFDF8→#F7F0E2→#EDE3CF` top faces, `#D8CAAE→#9C8A66` walls) over a warm sand basin (`#A38C61→#6E5A33` at 0.185→0.38 over the family plate), and one vermilion gel ramp reserved strictly for the departing vessel (`#F98247/#F2683A/#E24A1C/#CC3A0C` hull, `#C7350C→#97270A` well, `#FFC9A4` lit-edge rim). Berthed craft are a warm-neutral clay (`#D6D0C3→#8D8471`), which is a value, not a third hue. One soft top-left key on a single axis from (150,110) to (830,900); every ramp in the file hangs on that one segment in `userSpaceOnUse`. Rim highlights and soft ambient occlusion, zero hard speculars, no second light source.

**Authored overlap** (the era's craft tell, and the one thing a flat pre-masked raster cannot fake under system tinting): the basin's ripple grain is redrawn clipped to the gel hull in a warm `#FFD9C2` tint, so the water visibly reads *through* the vessel. Forcing that effect is what forces the layer discipline #10 wants.

**Audit: 11/12, zero failures on the non-negotiable 1–4.** Full-bleed 1024 artwork with the set's exact squircle as a *clip*, no baked corners or shadow. Checks 3 and 4 verified on real renders rather than imagined: the filled-black silhouette (`silhouette.png`, generated by `silhouette.py`) names a shore with four piers projecting and one pointed hull clear of them on a heading; the 16px squint sheds coursing, bollards and foam cleanly to four pale stepped bars and one ember dash. The focal measures **2.18:1** against its own surround at 1024 and 2.17:1 at 32px, by a dilated-ring measure rather than a hand-placed sample. #10 passes by construction: four layers `bg/mid/fg/highlight` mapping 1:1 onto Icon Composer, identity carried by `fg`'s filled shapes. **Point deducted at #7:** nothing here reaches the 3:1 bar against the basin — focal 2.18:1, berthed pair 2.32:1, pier stone 1.36:1. The numbers are in family (the sibling create-mac-icon measures 1.96:1 and the winning raster 2.28:1 by the same measure) and the berthed craft clear 3.14:1 against the pier stone that frames them, but the bar is the bar and the point goes.

**Three engines, six takes, nine fidelity rounds.** The set is on `audit.html`, losers included with their scores. Engine B ran twice; the better take (`icon-engineB-arrow-e5f2b5.svg`) was the miss at 5/12: it baked its own rounded-corner mask, rendered the basin an opaque mid-brown, and drew the wake in the reserved accent as red streaks. Engine C's first raster won the material judgment and became the loop's target; its second take lost on tone-on-tone collapse. Trajectory in `runs/`.

**What the loop actually corrected** (kept because none of it was obvious in advance, and each was only visible against a measured reference):

1. *Chroma is what makes gel look translucent, and the first ramp threw it away in shadow.* Measured on the raster, the reference's darkest gel pixel is `rgb(198,32,5)` — **S 0.98, V 0.78**. The master's was `rgb(151,31,30)` — S 0.80, V 0.59, and 8° cooler in hue. Both a luminance-range check and a mean-saturation check passed it; only sampling the darkest pixel saw it. Re-ramped to hold S 0.94 / V 0.80 at the dark end, the hull stopped reading as opaque paint. This is the third time in this marketplace that a shadow which desaturated read as an opaque one.
2. *A wake is a fan, not a band.* One tapered translucent band reads as a smear on the water. The raster's is five fine foam streaks that diverge astern over a shallow trough of displaced water, and rebuilding it that way is most of what makes the vessel read as *moving* rather than *placed*.
3. *Flat bollards read as paint on the pier.* Two concentric circles is a decal; a moulding needs a cast shadow, a body, a cap ellipse and a small catch — four elements, and the toy-scale render depends on them more than on any gradient.
4. *A gradient that costs nothing at 1024 can still cost 16px.* A broad water-sheen ellipse was tried twice (r04, r05) and lost ~0.002 of composite at **every** size including the 16px read. The tile already carries a plate radial, a vignette and the water ramp; a fourth broad field was redundant, and splitting r04 into two rounds is what proved the sheen rather than the clay re-hue was the cause. Both rejected sources are kept as `runs/rNN-candidate.svg`.
5. *A hand-placed contrast sample is worth nothing, and it hid the icon's one real defect for six rounds.* Sampling "the ember" and "the water beside it" by eye returned **3.10:1** and closed the question. A dilated-ring measure — every red-dominant pixel against a 45px dilation of that mask, median to median — returned **1.85:1**. The difference is not noise: the hand sample had landed on the hull's dark side and on a light patch of basin. Nothing else in the loop found it, because the composite is a similarity score and the reference has the same weakness. **Measure a region against its own surround, never against a point you chose.**
6. *A whole-image contrast floor can fire in exactly the wrong direction.* Lightening the basin (r08) gained **+0.0800** of composite, the run's largest move, and the gate rejected it because 32px self-contrast fell 0.338 → 0.309. That statistic is a p90−p10 spread the tile ground dominates; the basin had moved *toward* the stone laid over it. At the same 32px, on an identical 37-pixel footprint, the focal's contrast against its own surround rose **1.93 → 2.17:1**. Shipped over the gate, per the ordering.

**What the first render corrected, before the loop ever started** — worth recording because it is a whole-composition failure that no metric would have named: a quay bar spanning the full tile height with four equal piers off it **is a letter E**. The raster engine, briefed independently, produced the same E. The fix was to stop drawing the quay as a bar and draw the *shore* instead — a filled apron cut by the mask on three edges, so the harbour reads as larger than the frame — and to stagger the pier lengths so the teeth are not a comb. Neither change is visible in a residual; both were visible in the first second of looking.

**Register note:** the porcelain here lives in the stone as much as in the ground. The basin's depth is the icon's one genuinely contested parameter — it trades the berth grid against the vessels and cannot lift both — so any future revision should treat it as a *local* problem rather than a global one: shade the slips, where the piers need separation, and leave the open water bright, where the focal does. r08 is the shipped compromise, not a settled answer.

**Shelf check.** The master was put at 32px beside ship-armada, create-mac-icon, trawl, mac-doctor and report before shipping. That is what caught the focal being the weakest mark on the shelf (fixed in r07 by enlarging it 12%) — the fidelity reference is a raster, and a raster is not the shelf.
