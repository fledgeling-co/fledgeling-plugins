report icon — contact sheet & audit

Direction “The Fold” · four takes across two engines · audited on real renders at 1024 / 128 / 64 / 48 / 32 / 16 css px from 2× retina sources, plus Dark and Tinted variants of both layered takes. The shipping take changed on 19 Aug 2026 — see the decision block at the foot of the page.
What ran, and what refused. This commission was built on 9 Aug with one hand-authored take and no contact sheet. A retrospective sheet on 19 Aug scored that single take and said so; the three-engine floor was still unmet, because nobody had ever run Engine B or Engine C for this icon. Both were commissioned on 19 Aug 2026 and only one came back. Engine B (Arrow 1.1, svg: true) refused — the gateway returned “A positive credit balance is required for all requests, including BYOK”, so there is no independent vector take and no Arrow shapes were salvaged into the master. Under the skill's own fallback clause, Engine A was widened instead: a second hand-authored take that keeps the direction and the geometry and inverts the value architecture the porcelain take loses a point on. That widened take is the one that now ships, as icon.svg rebuilt by build_icon.py; the porcelain original is preserved whole as icon-A1-porcelain.svg, rebuilt by build_icon_a1_porcelain.py, and is scored beside it below. So the floor is met by four real artifacts from two engines, not three — the widened take is not presented as an Engine B substitute, and every take's source file sits in this directory under the name cited beside it. Still not done: no fidelity loop has run and no blind panel has seen any of these takes, so nothing here is a judged comparison; the scores are this pass's own reading of the twelve-point rubric in icon-directions.md against the renders and the measurements below. The Clear appearance variant is also unrendered — three of #10's four registers were captured, not four.

Every figure in this table was measured before the swap and none of them moved with it — the columns changed places, the pixels did not. Contrast is WCAG relative luminance computed from the rendered pixels, not from the source hexes, which is why one earlier figure moves: the 19 Aug sheet reported the crease clearing at 3.27:1 from a hex pair, and across the band as actually painted the median accent pixel is 2.65:1 against the ground on A1, because the pale roll highlight lightens most of it. Only its darkest decile reaches 4.02:1. A1's hex-derived pairs still hold and agree with the pixels where they overlap: paper #F2ECE1 on ground #EFE8DB is 1.04:1, ruling #B4AA96 is 1.96:1 on the paper's shaded end and 2.28:1 on its lit end. Face and ruling values are medians over segmented regions; the 16px spread is the standard deviation of relative luminance over the opaque pixels of the 32px source; the centroid is the gradient-magnitude centre of mass, which ignores smooth ground ramps.

Rubric #10, finally rendered: the same artwork on three grounds

Produced by variants.py, which substitutes the three ground stops and the crown highlight inside the bg layer and leaves every other layer exactly as authored. That substitution is only possible on a take with a real layer plan, which is the whole of #10: neither Engine C raster has a bg group, so neither has a row here. Clear is not simulable for a tile that carries its own ground, and is therefore unrendered rather than passed.
Each take is hostage to a ground, and to the opposite one. The take that now ships is weakest on Dark, at 1.10:1; A1's weakness was Default, at 1.08:1 — the register it actually shipped in. That asymmetry is the whole of the swap, and it also means the swap moves a liability rather than removing one. Neither take disappears; both stay nameable at 64px in all three registers, which is what moves #10 to a pass for both. The second channel is why the shipping take survives its own worst register: its ruling holds 6.90:1 against its own face in every register, so when its silhouette merges into a dark ground the internal structure still carries the object. A1's ruling is 2.04:1 in every register, so when its silhouette merges — which is what Default and Tinted both do — nothing inside it takes over.

The 12-point rubric, scored 19 Aug against the renders

1. Mask discipline — designed for the squircle, no baked corners or shadow (non-negotiable) PASS — full-bleed 1024 with the family superellipse read from create-mac-icon/assets/squircle-path.txt and applied as a clip. No baked corner radius, no baked tile shadow. Both Engine C rasters also pass, but only because they were masked at render time with :raster-mask; as delivered they are square-cornered full-bleed frames. PASS — the same path, the same clip, so the whole family still shares one tile.
2. Grid adherence — optically centred, safe-zone margins (non-negotiable) PASS — identical geometry to A1 by construction: the sheet is 552px wide at x=236, so side margins are identical, and the gradient-energy centroid measures (496, 487) against a 512 target. At 53.9% of tile width it sits just under the 55–65% composition constant; noted, not deducted, because the stepped leaves widen the read below the crease. PASS — centroid (494, 479). The same 53.9% note applies and is not deducted for the same reason.
3. Silhouette test — nameable filled solid black (non-negotiable) PASS — the strongest in the set, because the graphite face means the filled-black read and the rendered read are nearly the same picture. PASS — and the check the design was driven by. Filled to one tone: a tall block, a band, then narrower stepped blocks. icon-notes.md records why the first cut's three same-width leaves with 20px gaps failed — a 20px gap survives a 32× reduction as 0.6 of a pixel — and that the fix was to make the two halves differ in silhouette rather than in spacing.
4. 16px squint — survives menu-bar and Spotlight (non-negotiable) PASS — 0.3601, 2.55× A1 and the best in the set. At 16px the read is unambiguous: dark block, warm line, two stepped dark blocks. PASS, and the weakest pass in this table. On A1-32.png at true 16px and at ×6: pale block, grey striping, one warm band, two paler stepped blocks. Luminance spread 0.1410 — below the 0.176 median measured across this family, above the 0.102 of an icon that was reopened for failing here. The stepped silhouette survives, but it is delineated by the leaf shadows rather than by the fills, which is what #7 is about.
5. Single light model PASS — key from the upper left, rim light on the top and left edges, contact shadows offset +7x/+9y. The slit is emissive rather than a second key, which the corpus supports ("gel extrusion with strong inner glow — the house reads backlit"). It inherits the same accent-ramp inconsistency as A1, and it is not deducted here either. PASS — one key from the upper left. The sheet takes #FFFEFC at top-left falling to #F2ECE1 bottom-right, each leaf casts onto the one below in the same direction, and the crease's vertical roll is lit along its top edge. One inconsistency was considered and not deducted: the crease's horizontal ramp runs ACCENT_LO at the left to ACCENT_HI at the right, so the accent is darkest at the lit end. It is one element against an otherwise coherent scene, not the two-conflicting-keys failure this rubric line is for.
6. Palette economy — ≤2 hue families, accent reserved for the focal PASS — two families: warm neutrals (porcelain ground, graphite sheet, porcelain ruling, warm-grey rim) plus one vermilion, which now includes the slit's glow and highlight. Accent coverage 3.89% of the tile, all of it on or adjacent to the fold. PASS — warm porcelain ground, warm-grey ruling, one vermilion. ACCENT_HI/MID/LO are three values of one hue and the accent is spent on the crease alone, covering 2.13% of the tile.
7. Figure-ground — glyph vs ground ≥3:1, survives grayscale PASS, decisively — and the check this take exists to answer. Sheet face to ground 10.03:1 at 1024 and 9.90:1 at 256; porcelain ruling to graphite face 11.00:1; grayscale separation 180 levels of 255 against A1's 4. Darkening RULE_INK in A1 would not have got here — the ground relationship had to change, not the ink. FAIL — the narrowest value band of any icon in this marketplace, and measured on pixels rather than inferred from hexes. Sheet face against tile ground is 1.04:1 at 1024 and 1.08:1 on a clean paper patch at 256; the ruling, which is the icon's entire texture, is 1.98:1 against the face. In grayscale the face and ground are 4 levels apart out of 255, so the object is held together by its drop shadows and a 2.5px #DCD2BF hairline. Take those away and there is a warm line on an empty field. The subject still reads at every size, so this is a deduction rather than a hard failure — but it is the substantive one, and it was the icon's own default register.
8. Depth coherence — planes ordered, shadows match light, no z-fight FAIL — the only check the shipping take loses, and it ships losing it. The slit spans y=512–536, the sheet's lower edge is y=505 and the first leaf's upper edge is y=563, so the accent touches neither plane: a 7px gap above, a 27px gap below. The direction's entire claim is that the crease is the one place the two states meet, and here the two states do not meet at all — the join is implied by the glow rather than made by contact. Invisible below 128px and bridged by the glow at 1024, which is why it was judged the cheaper failure to ship. Everything else in the layer is coherent: per-leaf contact shadows instead of A1's single slab, and the glow clipped to the faces it falls on so it no longer smears across the porcelain between the leaves. PASS — ground, sheet, crease, then leaves in front of and below the sheet. The crease begins exactly at CREASE_Y=505, which is the sheet's lower edge, so it abuts one of the two planes it joins; the gap to the first leaf is 20px. One defect is worth recording even though it does not drop the point: the leaf contact shadow is a single blurred rect spanning LEAF_H*2 + LEAF_GAP, so its middle shows through the gap between the two leaves as a grey panel resting on nothing. Visible in the 1024 hero above.
9. Era coherence PASS — same ground treatment as A1 and the same measured falloff (−0.0335), and a graphite gel object on a porcelain cushion is a register this family already ships (proctor). Same missing perimeter rim light. PASS — cushioned ground with a crown highlight and a soft vignette (edge falloff −0.0335), one soft key, no hard specular. Deliberately not the previous era's page curl: this crease is horizontal and spans the full width, where dossier-report's is a diagonal corner flap. One measured shortfall against the era fingerprint, shared with the shipping take and recorded rather than deducted: the corpus describes the cushion as edge-darkening plus a subtle inner rim light around the perimeter, and neither A take has the rim — measured at −0.0335 along the left edge, i.e. darkening only.
10. Variant robustness — survives Default/Dark/Clear/Tinted, identity not hostage to one background (76% of the corpus fails here) PASS, with a liability. Default 9.90:1, Tinted 6.08:1, Dark 1.10:1 — the graphite sheet nearly merges into a dark ground, and what keeps it legible there is its ruling, still 6.90:1 against its own face. Five named layers, so the substitution runs at all. Clear unrendered. PASS on the same standard, with the opposite liability, and this was a correction. The structural half was closed on 19 Aug — fidelity.py structure had reported 0 named <g id> layers where the docstring claimed a bg/mid/fg/highlight plan, and after the fix it reports 5 (art / bg / mid / fg / highlight) at rms 0.000000 against the previous render. The variant half was recorded as a failure on the argument that porcelain-on-porcelain would die under a changed ground. Rendered, it does the opposite: Dark measures 11.72:1 and Tinted 1.76:1, and the object stays nameable at 64px in all three. Clear was not rendered.
11. Personality — ≥1 nameable device beyond glyph-on-gradient PASS — a crease that turns a continuous scroll into a paginated stack, read as light escaping the fold rather than pigment sitting on the ridge. Not deducted, but on the record and now shipping: dark rounded panel + pale horizontal ruling + one vermilion mark on porcelain is tui-craft's established vocabulary in this marketplace, and the two tiles sit in the same lineup. The pixel measurement does not support that as a geometric collision — this take correlates with tui-craft at +0.477 against a family-pair median of +0.396 and a 90th percentile of +0.610, and A1's own correlation with it is +0.463 — so this is a judgment about shared vocabulary, stated with the number that argues against it. PASS — the same device, with the colour coming out only where the two states meet. Not a document glyph, not a chart, not a clipboard.
12. No-text PASS — the ruling is abstract bars sized to read as texture rather than as countable lines, knocked out instead of printed. No glyphs, no words, no screenshot. PASS — same construction, printed instead of knocked out.
Recommendation: ship the inverted fold — icon.svg, rebuilt by build_icon.py, 11 / 12. Swapped in on 19 Aug 2026, and this was the user's decision rather than a measurement. The recommendation that reached them at the point of decision was the conservative one: keep the porcelain master. The two takes tie at 11 / 12, the porcelain artwork was already shipping in three rasters, a README row and a site tile, and the inverted take arrives carrying a rubric deduction of its own plus a lineup risk against tui-craft. The user chose the inverted take anyway, and the reason is the one thing in the comparison that is not symmetric: Default is the register everyone sees. Both takes fail in exactly one register and they are opposite ones — A1 measures 1.04:1 face-to-ground on Default and 11.72:1 on Dark, the inverted take 10.03:1 on Default and 1.10:1 on Dark. Legibility bought on Dark is bought where almost nobody is looking; legibility bought on Default is bought in the README row, the marketplace tile, the digest email and the Finder list. That trade is a priority call, not a number, which is why it was the user's to make. Nothing measured changed with the swap: the columns above moved, the pixels did not, and on count the two takes still tie. What now ships, and what it ships broken. (1) The floating accent (#8). The one deduction on the tile that ships: the slit spans y=512–536 with a 7px gap to the sheet above and a 27px gap to the first leaf below, so the crease touches neither of the two planes the direction says it joins. Invisible below 128px, bridged by the glow at 1024. The fix is a single named constant in build_icon.py — raise the slit to y=505 so it abuts the sheet's lower edge, or lift the first leaf to close the 27px gap — and it is the cheapest remaining point in the set. (2) Dark at 1.10:1. Not a rubric deduction, because #10 passes on all three rendered registers, but it is the liability the swap accepted: on a dark ground the graphite sheet nearly merges and legibility rests entirely on the ruling's 6.90:1 against its own face. The porcelain take was 11.72:1 there. If the family ever ships a Dark-first surface, this is the icon that needs a second look first. (3) The tui-craft vocabulary. Now a shipped adjacency rather than a hypothetical one: two dark-panel tiles with pale ruling and a vermilion mark in the same marketplace. The correlation measurement argues against it (+0.477 against a family median of +0.396) and a careful look at a 16px strip is what would settle it. (4) No perimeter rim light. The corpus's cushion is edge-darkening plus a subtle inner rim light; both A takes have only the darkening, measured at −0.0335. The cheapest remaining gain in era fidelity. (5) Engine B never ran. Arrow refused on a credit balance, so there is no independent vector take and nothing was salvaged from one. Four takes across two engines, stated at the top rather than buried here. (6) No loop, no panel, one Clear variant short. No fidelity round has been run against C2 despite it being the material winner, no blind panel has ranked these four, and #10 rests on three of its four registers. (7) A1's own failure, now off the shelf.
