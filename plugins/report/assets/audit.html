report icon — contact sheet & audit

Direction “The Fold” · four takes across two engines · audited on real renders at 1024 / 128 / 64 / 48 / 32 / 16 css px from 2× retina sources, plus Dark and Tinted variants of both layered takes.
What ran, and what refused. This commission was built on 9 Aug with one hand-authored take and no contact sheet. A retrospective sheet on 19 Aug scored that single take and said so; the three-engine floor was still unmet, because nobody had ever run Engine B or Engine C for this icon. Both were commissioned on 19 Aug 2026 and only one came back. Engine B (Arrow 1.1, svg: true) refused — the gateway returned “A positive credit balance is required for all requests, including BYOK”, so there is no independent vector take and no Arrow shapes were salvaged into the master. Under the skill's own fallback clause, Engine A was widened instead: A2 below is a second hand-authored take (build_icon_a2.py → icon-A2-fold-inverted.svg) that keeps the direction and the geometry and inverts the value architecture the master loses a point on. So the floor is met by four real artifacts from two engines, not three — A2 is not presented as an Engine B substitute, and every take's source file sits in this directory under the name cited beside it. Still not done: no fidelity loop has run and no blind panel has seen any of these takes, so nothing here is a judged comparison; the scores are this pass's own reading of the twelve-point rubric in icon-directions.md against the renders and the measurements below. The Clear appearance variant is also unrendered — three of #10's four registers were captured, not four.

Contrast is WCAG relative luminance computed from the rendered pixels, not from the source hexes, which is why one earlier figure moves: the 19 Aug sheet reported the crease clearing at 3.27:1 from a hex pair, and across the band as actually painted the median accent pixel is 2.65:1 against the ground, because the pale roll highlight lightens most of it. Only its darkest decile reaches 4.02:1. The hex-derived pairs still hold and agree with the pixels where they overlap: paper #F2ECE1 on ground #EFE8DB is 1.04:1, ruling #B4AA96 is 1.96:1 on the paper's shaded end and 2.28:1 on its lit end. Face and ruling values are medians over segmented regions; the 16px spread is the standard deviation of relative luminance over the opaque pixels of the 32px source; the centroid is the gradient-magnitude centre of mass, which ignores smooth ground ramps.

Rubric #10, finally rendered: the same artwork on three grounds

Produced by variants.py, which substitutes the three ground stops and the crown highlight inside the bg layer and leaves every other layer exactly as authored. That substitution is only possible on a take with a real layer plan, which is the whole of #10: neither Engine C raster has a bg group, so neither has a row here. Clear is not simulable for a tile that carries its own ground, and is therefore unrendered rather than passed.
Each take is hostage to a ground, and to the opposite one: A's worst register is Default, at 1.08:1 — the one it ships in, and A2's is Dark at 1.10:1. Neither disappears; both stay nameable at 64px in all three, which is what moves #10 to a pass for both. The asymmetry that decides the recommendation is the second channel. A2's ruling holds 6.90:1 against its own face in every register, so when its silhouette merges into a dark ground the internal structure still carries the object. A's ruling is 2.04:1 in every register, so when its silhouette merges — which is what Default and Tinted both do — nothing inside it takes over.

The 12-point rubric, scored 19 Aug against the renders

CheckA · icon.svg (ships)A2 · icon-A2-fold-inverted.svg
1. Mask discipline — designed for the squircle, no baked corners or shadow (non-negotiable) PASS — full-bleed 1024 with the family superellipse read from create-mac-icon/assets/squircle-path.txt and applied as a clip. No baked corner radius, no baked tile shadow. PASS — the same path, the same clip, so the whole family still shares one tile. Both Engine C rasters also pass, but only because they were masked at render time with :raster-mask; as delivered they are square-cornered full-bleed frames.
2. Grid adherence — optically centred, safe-zone margins (non-negotiable) PASS — the sheet is 552px wide at x=236, so side margins are identical, and the gradient-energy centroid measures (494, 479) against a 512 target. At 53.9% of tile width it sits just under the 55–65% composition constant; noted, not deducted, because the stepped leaves widen the read below the crease. PASS — identical geometry by construction, centroid (496, 487). The same 53.9% note applies and is not deducted for the same reason.
3. Silhouette test — nameable filled solid black (non-negotiable) PASS — and the check the design was driven by. Filled to one tone: a tall block, a band, then narrower stepped blocks. icon-notes.md records why the first cut's three same-width leaves with 20px gaps failed — a 20px gap survives a 32× reduction as 0.6 of a pixel — and that the fix was to make the two halves differ in silhouette rather than in spacing. PASS — the strongest in the set, because the graphite face means the filled-black read and the rendered read are nearly the same picture.
4. 16px squint — survives menu-bar and Spotlight (non-negotiable) PASS, and the weakest pass in this column. On master-32.png at true 16px and at ×6: pale block, grey striping, one warm band, two paler stepped blocks. Luminance spread 0.1410 — below the 0.176 median measured across this family, above the 0.102 of an icon that was reopened for failing here. The stepped silhouette survives, but it is delineated by the leaf shadows rather than by the fills, which is what #7 is about. PASS — 0.3601, 2.55× the master and the best in the set. At 16px the read is unambiguous: dark block, warm line, two stepped dark blocks.
5. Single light model PASS — one key from the upper left. The sheet takes #FFFEFC at top-left falling to #F2ECE1 bottom-right, each leaf casts onto the one below in the same direction, and the crease's vertical roll is lit along its top edge. One inconsistency was considered and not deducted: the crease's horizontal ramp runs ACCENT_LO at the left to ACCENT_HI at the right, so the accent is darkest at the lit end. It is one element against an otherwise coherent scene, not the two-conflicting-keys failure this rubric line is for. PASS — key from the upper left, rim light on the top and left edges, contact shadows offset +7x/+9y. The slit is emissive rather than a second key, which the corpus supports ("gel extrusion with strong inner glow — the house reads backlit"). It inherits the same accent-ramp inconsistency from the master's convention, and it is not deducted here either.
6. Palette economy — ≤2 hue families, accent reserved for the focal PASS — warm porcelain ground, warm-grey ruling, one vermilion. ACCENT_HI/MID/LO are three values of one hue and the accent is spent on the crease alone, covering 2.13% of the tile. PASS — the same two families: warm neutrals (porcelain ground, graphite sheet, porcelain ruling, warm-grey rim) plus one vermilion, which now includes the slit's glow and highlight. Accent coverage 3.89% of the tile, all of it on or adjacent to the fold.
7. Figure-ground — glyph vs ground ≥3:1, survives grayscale FAIL — the narrowest value band of any icon in this marketplace, and now measured on pixels rather than inferred from hexes. Sheet face against tile ground is 1.04:1 at 1024 and 1.08:1 on a clean paper patch at 256; the ruling, which is the icon's entire texture, is 1.98:1 against the face. In grayscale the face and ground are 4 levels apart out of 255, so the object is held together by its drop shadows and a 2.5px #DCD2BF hairline. Take those away and there is a warm line on an empty field. The subject still reads at every size, so this is a deduction rather than a hard failure — but it is the substantive one, and it is the icon's own default register. PASS, decisively. Sheet face to ground 10.03:1 at 1024 and 9.90:1 at 256; porcelain ruling to graphite face 11.00:1; grayscale separation 180 levels of 255 against the master's 4. This is the one check the take exists to answer, and darkening RULE_INK in the master would not have got here — the ground relationship had to change, not the ink.
8. Depth coherence — planes ordered, shadows match light, no z-fight PASS — ground, sheet, crease, then leaves in front of and below the sheet. The crease begins exactly at CREASE_Y=505, which is the sheet's lower edge, so it abuts one of the two planes it joins; the gap to the first leaf is 20px. One inherited defect is worth recording even though it does not drop the point: the leaf contact shadow is a single blurred rect spanning LEAF_H*2 + LEAF_GAP, so its middle shows through the gap between the two leaves as a grey panel resting on nothing. Visible in the 1024 hero above. FAIL — and the only check it loses. The slit spans y=512–536, the sheet's lower edge is y=505 and the first leaf's upper edge is y=563, so the accent touches neither plane: a 7px gap above, a 27px gap below. The direction's entire claim is that the crease is the one place the two states meet, and here the two states do not meet at all — the join is implied by the glow rather than made by contact. Everything else in the layer is coherent: per-leaf contact shadows instead of the master's single slab, and the glow clipped to the faces it falls on so it no longer smears across the porcelain between the leaves.
9. Era coherence PASS — cushioned ground with a crown highlight and a soft vignette (edge falloff −0.0335), one soft key, no hard specular. Deliberately not the previous era's page curl: this crease is horizontal and spans the full width, where dossier-report's is a diagonal corner flap. One measured shortfall against the era fingerprint, shared with A2 and recorded rather than deducted: the corpus describes the cushion as edge-darkening plus a subtle inner rim light around the perimeter, and neither A take has the rim — measured at −0.0335 along the left edge, i.e. darkening only. PASS — same ground treatment and the same measured falloff, and a graphite gel object on a porcelain cushion is a register this family already ships (proctor). Same missing perimeter rim light.
10. Variant robustness — survives Default/Dark/Clear/Tinted, identity not hostage to one background (76% of the corpus fails here) PASS, and this is a correction. The structural half was closed on 19 Aug — fidelity.py structure had reported 0 named <g id> layers where the docstring claimed a bg/mid/fg/highlight plan, and after the fix it reports 5 (art / bg / mid / fg / highlight) at rms 0.000000 against the previous render. The variant half was recorded as a failure on the argument that porcelain-on-porcelain would die under a changed ground. Rendered, it does the opposite: Dark measures 11.72:1 and Tinted 1.76:1, and the object stays nameable at 64px in all three. Clear was not rendered. PASS on the same standard, with the opposite liability. Default 9.90:1, Tinted 6.08:1, Dark 1.10:1 — the graphite sheet nearly merges into a dark ground, and what keeps it legible there is its ruling, still 6.90:1 against its own face. Five named layers, so the substitution runs at all. Clear unrendered here too.
11. Personality — ≥1 nameable device beyond glyph-on-gradient PASS — a crease that turns a continuous scroll into a paginated stack, with the colour coming out only where the two states meet. Not a document glyph, not a chart, not a clipboard. PASS — the same device read as light escaping the fold rather than pigment sitting on the ridge. Not deducted, but on the record: dark rounded panel + pale horizontal ruling + one vermilion mark on porcelain is tui-craft's established vocabulary in this marketplace, and A2 would sit beside it in a lineup. The pixel measurement does not support that as a geometric collision — A2 correlates with tui-craft at +0.477 against a family-pair median of +0.396 and a 90th percentile of +0.610, and the master's own correlation with it is +0.463 — so this is a judgment about shared vocabulary, stated with the number that argues against it.
12. No-text PASS — the ruling is abstract bars sized to read as texture rather than as countable lines. No glyphs, no words, no screenshot. PASS — same construction, knocked out instead of printed.
